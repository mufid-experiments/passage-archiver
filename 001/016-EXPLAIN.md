# Line 016 · どこか懐かしくて振り向いた

## Context

- Line 015 (previous): 頬を掠める春風が
- **Line 016 (explained here): どこか懐かしくて振り向いた**
- Line 017 (next): もう迷わない

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 015 (subject of 振り向いた omitted).

The line is grammatical and holds the two predicates of the sentence begun on line 015. The first, どこか懐かしくて, is the i-adjective 懐かしい in its te-form, with the adverb どこか. Its subject is 春風 from line 015: the breeze is what seems nostalgic, and the speaker is the one who feels it. Here the te-form gives the cause of what follows. The second, 振り向いた, is the past-tense main verb, and it ends the sentence. Its subject is not stated. It is the speaker (僕, line 027), since a breeze cannot turn around. Dropping a subject that context makes clear is ordinary Japanese. Nothing is reordered, and there is no poetic license. Restored prose: 頬を掠める春風がどこか懐かしくて、（僕は）振り向いた。 Line 017 starts a new sentence.

## Reading

- Kana: どこか なつかしくて ふりむいた
- Romaji: dokoka natsukashikute furimuita

## How it fits together

- This line: `[[どこ か] 懐かしく て] [振り向い た]`
- Whole sentence: `[[[頬 を 掠める] 春風] が [どこ か] 懐かしく て] (僕 は) [振り向い た]`

どこか modifies 懐かしく, whose subject is 春風が on line 015. て makes that clause the cause and joins it to the main verb 振り向いた. The main verb has a different subject: the unstated speaker (僕). The te-form has no tense of its own, so the past た of 振り向いた covers both parts. The breeze seemed nostalgic, and the speaker turned around.

## Translation

- Literal: "(it) being somehow nostalgic, (I) turned around"
- Natural: "…felt somehow nostalgic, and I turned around" (with line 015: "The spring breeze brushing past my cheek felt somehow nostalgic, and I turned around.")

## Notes

- **Place in the first chorus.** The chorus (lines 011–018) has three sentences: 011–014, ending in the question 笑えているかな; 015–016; and 017–018, もう迷わない with line 018 attached by inversion. This line closes the middle one. 振り向いた is a terminal form, so line 017 starts a new sentence.
- **〜くて as a cause.** The te-form itself only joins two clauses. The causal sense comes from their meaning, typically a feeling followed by the reaction it triggers. The two clauses can have different subjects, as here: 風が気持ちよくて眠ってしまった "the breeze was so pleasant that I fell asleep". 懐かしかったから振り向いた would state the reason outright; the te-form just runs the feeling into the reaction. A cause te-form can't lead into a volitional form or a request: not ×寒くて窓を閉めよう but 寒いから窓を閉めよう "it's cold, so let's shut the window".
- **どこか here vs. line 021.** On line 021 (いつかどこか遠い空で), どこか means "somewhere", and a place noun follows it. Here it comes straight before an adjective and means "somehow, in some way". The speaker can't point to what it is about the breeze, but the feeling is definitely there. Compare どこか変だ "something is off somehow". どこか懐かしい is a common collocation (どこか懐かしい風景 "a landscape that feels somehow nostalgic"). Near-synonyms: なんとなく, どことなく.
- **懐かしい.** It comes from the verb 懐く (なつく, "to grow attached to"). Its main modern sense is recalling, with pleasure, someone or something you once knew well (懐かしい曲 "a song that takes me back"). English "nostalgic" is the usual gloss but can sound more wistful than the Japanese. In plain statements a feeling adjective describes the speaker's own feeling. For someone else's you would normally add 〜そう or 〜がる (懐かしそうに振り向いた "turned around, looking nostalgic"). So the one who feels it here is the speaker (see line 015).
- **Past tense.** 振り向いた is the chorus's only past-tense main verb. The sentences around it are non-past (笑えているかな on line 014, 迷わない on line 017), and the past 過ごした on line 018 sits inside a relative clause. In form, godan く-verbs change き to い before た and て (振り向き → 振り向い + た), as in 描いた (line 020) and 咲いた (line 010). The one exception is 行く → 行った.
