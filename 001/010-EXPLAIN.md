# Line 010 · ほどけた雪の隙間 咲いた花笑う

## Context

- Line 009 (previous): 大丈夫 君の手を背中に感じて
- **Line 010 (explained here): ほどけた雪の隙間 咲いた花笑う**
- Line 011 (next): 巡りゆく季節の隅っこで

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: two phrases; particles に and が omitted

The space divides the line into two phrases. Phrase 1, ほどけた雪の隙間, is a noun phrase headed by 隙間 with no particle after it, so its role as the place of the scene is left to context; prose would add に (or で). Phrase 2, 咲いた花笑う, is a full clause with the subject particle が dropped. It ends in the terminal form 笑う, so the line closes a sentence. Restored prose: ほどけた雪の隙間（に）咲いた花（が）笑う。 Using ほどける ("come untied") for snow and 笑う for flowers (personification, 擬人法) are deliberate poetic choices, not errors (b). Everyday prose would say 雪が解けた (とけた) and 花が咲いている.

## Reading

- Kana: ほどけた ゆき の すきま / さいた はな わらう
- Romaji: hodoketa yuki no sukima / saita hana warau

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | ほどけ | ほどけ (hodoke) | ichidan verb ほどける (解ける), continuative form (連用形) | to come undone, to loosen, to unravel; here the snow breaking up as it thaws |
| 2 | た | た (ta) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) た, attributive form (連体形) | completion / resulting state: "that has come undone"; ほどけた modifies 雪 |

| 4 | の | の (no) | case particle (格助詞), attributive | links 雪 to 隙間: "the gaps in the snow" |
| 5 | 隙間 | すきま (sukima) | noun | gap, opening; the location particle に (or で) is omitted after it |
| 6 | 咲い | さい (sai) | godan verb 咲く, continuative form with euphonic change (イ音便: 咲き → 咲い) | to bloom |
| 7 | た | た (ta) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) た, attributive form (連体形) | completion / resulting state: "that have bloomed, in bloom"; modifies 花 (not sentence-final) |
| 8 | 花 | はな (hana) | noun | flower(s); subject of 笑う, with が omitted |
| 9 | 笑う | わらう (warau) | godan verb 笑う, terminal form (終止形) | to smile, to laugh; ends the sentence |

## How it fits together

`[[ほどけ た] 雪 の] 隙間 (に) ／ [[咲い た] 花] (が) 笑う`

Phrase 1: ほどけた modifies 雪, and 雪の modifies 隙間 ("the gaps in the snow that has come undone"); with the omitted に, this is where the flowers bloom. Phrase 2: 咲いた modifies 花, which is the subject (が omitted) of the predicate 笑う. Phrase 1 can also be taken as a stand-alone noun phrase (体言止め) that sets the scene before the clause.

## Translation

- Literal: "the gap(s) of the snow that came undone; the flower(s) that bloomed smile"
- Natural: "In the gaps where the snow has come undone, flowers in bloom are smiling"

## Notes

- Line 009's open te-form (感じて) leads into this scene. Line 010 still has its own subject (花) and ends with the terminal form 笑う. Line 011 (巡りゆく季節の隅っこで) starts a new sentence, part of the passage that recurs at lines 033–040.
- The line moves the song from winter to spring: the skin-piercing white of line 002 is coming apart, and flowers show through. 雪の隙間 is close to the haiku spring word 雪間 (ゆきま), a patch where the snow has melted and the ground shows.
- ほどける normally describes knots and ties (靴ひもがほどける "a shoelace comes undone") or, figuratively, feelings (心がほどける "one's heart loosens"). Used for snow, it pictures the snow unravelling and may also hint at the narrator's feelings easing. Written in kana, it can't be confused with 解ける read とける ("melt"). Its transitive partner is ほどく "to untie".
- 笑う for flowers is a traditional personification. The spring season word 山笑う ("the mountains smile") describes hills coming alive with new green, and classical 笑む (えむ) can mean "(a bud) opens". The kanji 咲 began in Chinese as a variant of 笑; Japanese gave it the meaning "to bloom".
- The smiling flowers echo line 014 (ちゃんと笑えているかな), where the narrator wonders whether they themselves can smile properly.
- た inside a noun-modifying clause often marks a resulting state rather than a past event: ほどけた雪 "snow that has come undone", 咲いた花 "flowers in bloom" (compare 疲れた顔 "a tired face"). Terminal and attributive た look the same, so position decides: here both modify the following noun.
