# Line 032 · 君だけがいない世界を

## Context

- Line 031 (previous): 新しい世界
- **Line 032 (explained here): 君だけがいない世界を**
- Line 033 (next): 巡りゆく季節の隅っこで

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: を-phrase with its verb left unsaid (言いさし).

This is (b): grammatical, but a deliberate poetic fragment. Everything inside the line is standard: a relative clause (君だけがいない) modifying 世界, then the object particle を. But を needs a verb, and none arrives. 待つ on line 030 already has its role (its subject is 新しい世界), and line 033 restarts the chorus, so the phrase is left hanging (言いさし). A prose version has to supply a verb that takes a place or path with を, most naturally 君だけがいない世界を（生きていく／歩いていく）; in plain prose or speech the bare line would sound unfinished, but in lyrics it is a common way to leave a thought open.

## Reading

- Kana: きみ だけ が いない せかい を
- Romaji: kimi dake ga inai sekai o

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 君 | きみ (kimi) | pronoun (代名詞), second person | "you" (familiar; very common in lyrics) |
| 2 | だけ | だけ (dake) | adverbial particle (副助詞) | "only, alone"; limits the subject to 君 |
| 3 | が | が (ga) | case particle (格助詞) — subject marker | marks 君だけ as the subject of いない |
| 4 | い | い (i) | ichidan verb いる (上一段), irrealis form (未然形) before ない | to be (there), to be present (for people and animals) |
| 5 | ない | ない (nai) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) ない, attributive form (連体形) — negation | "not"; 君だけがいない "only you are not there" modifies 世界 |
| 6 | 世界 | せかい (sekai) | noun | world |
| 7 | を | を (o) | case particle (格助詞) — object / path marker | marks 君だけがいない世界 as the object (the space lived or walked through) of a verb that is never sung |

## How it fits together

`[[君 だけ が] い ない] 世界 を → (verb unsaid)`

The relative clause 君だけがいない ("where only you are absent") modifies 世界. を marks the noun phrase as the object of a following verb, but that verb is withheld; its implied subject would be the singer. In meaning, the phrase re-describes 新しい世界 from line 031: the new world is one where only you are missing.

## Translation

- Literal: "the world where only you are not present (object)…"
- Natural: "…a world where you're the only one missing." With an implied verb: "(I'll go on living in / walking through) a world where you alone are missing."

## Notes

- Nothing in lines 030–032 can take this を. 踏み出した sits inside a relative clause, and 待つ already has its partner (〜に待つのは新しい世界: the world is what waits). So the verb is simply left out. The を of a space moved or lived through (道を歩く "walk a road", 空を飛ぶ "fly through the sky", 今を生きる "live the present") fits the starting-line / step-out imagery, which is why 生きていく or 歩いていく is the natural completion. That verb is an inference; the lyric never says it.
- As a pure restatement of 新しい世界, prose would say …新しい世界、君だけがいない世界だ. Choosing を instead points forward to something the singer will do in that world.
- Line 033 restarts the chorus. It is identical to line 011, which follows a line with no を (line 010 ends in 笑う). Still, 巡る (めぐる) can take a path-を (世界を巡る "go around the world"), so when the lines are sung straight through they can also be heard as 君だけがいない世界を巡りゆく季節 "the seasons that circle through a world where only you are missing". That parse is grammatical but crosses a section boundary. Treat it as a possible double meaning, not the main reading.
- Compare the other を-lines in the song. On line 034 (幾つもの出会いと別れを), を gets its verb 繰り返し on the next line. On line 040 (君と過ごした日々を胸に), the verb is dropped by idiom (〜を胸に（抱いて） "with … in one's heart"). Line 032 is the loosest of the three: no verb and no set phrase.
- Standard vs poetic: in conversation, a を-phrase can end an utterance when it is inverted after its verb (食べたよ、ケーキを) or when the speaker trails off. In slogans it implies a verb of giving or wishing (世界に平和を "peace to the world"). There is no earlier verb here for it to be inverted from, so this is the trailing-off kind, used as lyrical ellipsis.
- 君だけがいない means "only you are absent": everything else is still there. Compare 君がいない / 君のいない世界 "(a world) without you" (in relative clauses が often becomes の), 君しかいない "there's no one but you", and 君だけじゃない "it's not just you".
