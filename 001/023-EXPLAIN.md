# Line 023 · 舞い上がる花びらに微笑む横顔

## Context

- Line 022 (previous): また出会えるから
- **Line 023 (explained here): 舞い上がる花びらに微笑む横顔**
- Line 024 (next): ここにはいない君を映し出す桜

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: noun-ending fragment (体言止め)

The line is a grammatical noun phrase with no predicate. Two nested modifiers (舞い上がる → 花びら, and 舞い上がる花びらに微笑む → 横顔) end on the noun 横顔. Ending a line on a bare noun (体言止め) is a standard poetic device that presents an image rather than making a statement. What's left out is whose face it is, plus any verb. A prose version might be 舞い上がる花びらに微笑む（君の）横顔（が浮かぶ）. It's normal to say that a 横顔 "smiles": the face stands for the person (metonymy), as in 微笑む顔 "a smiling face".

## Reading

- Kana: まいあがる はなびら に ほほえむ よこがお
- Romaji: maiagaru hanabira ni hohoemu yokogao

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 舞い上がる | まいあがる (maiagaru) | godan compound verb (複合動詞) 舞い上がる, attributive form (連体形) | "to fly up, to swirl up into the air"; 舞い (連用形 of 舞う "to dance, to flutter") + 上がる "to rise". Modifies 花びら |
| 2 | 花びら | はなびら (hanabira) | noun | "petal(s)"; 花 "flower" + ひら "thin flat piece", with rendaku (ひら → びら) |
| 3 | に | に (ni) | case particle (格助詞), target | marks 花びら as what the smile is directed at: "at" |
| 4 | 微笑む | ほほえむ (hohoemu) | godan verb 微笑む, attributive form (連体形) | "to smile (gently)"; the clause 舞い上がる花びらに微笑む modifies 横顔 |
| 5 | 横顔 | よこがお (yokogao) | noun | "profile, face seen from the side"; 横 "side" + 顔 "face", with rendaku (かお → がお). Head noun of the whole line |

## How it fits together

`[[舞い上がる 花びら] に 微笑む] 横顔`

舞い上がる modifies 花びら. 花びらに is the target of 微笑む. The whole clause 舞い上がる花びらに微笑む modifies the head noun 横顔. Nothing follows as a predicate, and the person whose face it is (most naturally 君) is never named.

## Translation

- Literal: "a profile smiling at petals flying up"
- Natural: "(your) face in profile, smiling at the petals swirling up into the air"

## Notes

- **A pair of images:** Lines 023 and 024 are parallel noun-ending phrases, each built as [modifying clause] + noun: [舞い上がる花びらに微笑む] 横顔 / [ここにはいない君を映し出す] 桜. No verb links them; they simply sit side by side. The 花びら here are presumably petals of the 桜 on line 024.
- **Not the main clause of line 022:** Line 022 closes its own unit (…また出会えるから, with the main clause unsaid). Line 023 is a noun phrase, so it can't be that main clause. It starts a new image.
- **Whose profile?** The lyric doesn't say. A 横顔 is a face seen from the side, the way you see someone standing next to you, so it most naturally belongs to 君 as the speaker remembers or imagines them. Line 024 supports this: there the cherry blossoms "project" the absent 君.
- **微笑む:** The two kanji 微笑 are read together as ほほえ(む), a 熟字訓-type reading. The on-reading 微笑 (びしょう) is the noun "smile". The verb is also written 頬笑む, from 頬 ほほ "cheek" + 笑む えむ "to smile". It means a gentle smile, not a laugh (笑う).
- **〜に微笑む:** に marks who or what the smile is aimed at (彼女は私に微笑んだ "she smiled at me"). With emotion verbs, に can also mark the trigger (冗談に笑う "laugh at a joke"). "Smile at the petals" covers both readings.
- **舞い上がる:** Because of 舞う "to dance", the petals are dancing upward, as if on the wind (春風 "spring breeze" appears on lines 015 and 026). The verb can also mean "to be carried away with joy", but with 花びら it is literal.
