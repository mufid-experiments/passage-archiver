# Line 018 · 君と過ごした日々を胸に

## Context

- Line 017 (previous): もう迷わない
- **Line 018 (explained here): 君と過ごした日々を胸に**
- Line 019 (next): 神様は僕らを切り離して

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: verbless 「〜を胸に」 phrase (verb omitted), modifying line 017 by inversion (倒置法).

The line is grammatical, but it is not a sentence on its own. It has an object (日々を) and a location (胸に) but no main verb; 過ごした only belongs to the relative clause describing 日々. This is the set pattern 「XをYに」. The verb is left unsaid (here something like 抱いて "holding" or 刻んで "engraving"), and the phrase works adverbially, meaning "with X in Y". Line 019 opens the next section with a new topic (神様は), so the phrase attaches back to line 017's 迷わない and is placed after it for effect. Restored prose: 君と過ごした日々を胸に（抱いて）、（僕は）もう迷わない. This is idiomatic, not an error.

## Reading

- Kana: きみ と すごした ひび を むね に
- Romaji: kimi to sugoshita hibi o mune ni

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 君 | きみ (kimi) | pronoun (代名詞) | "you"; the usual intimate "you" of song lyrics |
| 2 | と | と (to) | case particle (格助詞) — companion | "with": marks 君 as the person the time was spent with. A verb follows, so this is not the listing "and" と of line 012 (出会いと別れ) |
| 3 | 過ごし | すごし (sugoshi) | godan verb 過ごす, continuative form (連用形) | to spend (time). す-verbs take た with no sound change: 過ごす → 過ごした |
| 4 | た | た (ta) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) た, past, attributive form (連体形) | past tense; closes the relative clause 君と過ごした, which modifies 日々 |
| 5 | 日々 | ひび (hibi) | noun | days; 々 repeats the kanji (日日), and the second ひ is voiced to び (rendaku) |
| 6 | を | を (o) | case particle (格助詞) — object marker | marks 日々 as the object of the unspoken verb (抱いて etc.) |
| 7 | 胸 | むね (mune) | noun | chest; figuratively the heart, where feelings and memories are kept |
| 8 | に | に (ni) | case particle (格助詞) — location / target | "in (my heart)": where the days are kept. The line stops here; the verb that would follow is omitted |

## How it fits together

`[[君 と 過ごし た] 日々] を 胸 に （抱いて）`

君と過ごした is a relative clause (連体修飾節) modifying 日々. Its subject, the speaker (僕), is omitted. 日々を (object) and 胸に (location) belong to an unspoken verb. The whole phrase is adverbial (連用修飾): it describes the state in which the speaker "won't hesitate" in line 017.

## Translation

- Literal: "the days (I) spent with you, in (my) chest"
- Natural: "with the days I spent with you held in my heart"

## Notes

- **The 「〜を胸に」 construction.** 「XをYに」 with no verb is a standard pattern: を marks the thing, に marks the place it is held, and the verb is understood. With 胸に the natural fillers are 抱いて (いだいて, "cherishing"; だいて is the reading for a physical hug), 刻んで ("engraving"), 秘めて ("keeping inside") or しまって ("storing away"). 胸に alone suggests all of them. Other examples: 花束を手に ("bouquet in hand"), 夕日を背に ("with the setting sun at one's back"), 夢を胸に ("with a dream in one's heart"). With 手に and 背に the dropped verb is usually just して (駅を背にして → 駅を背に). The pattern sounds slightly literary and resolute, and it is very common in lyrics, speeches and headlines.
- **Link to line 017.** Japanese normally puts modifiers before the predicate, so the prose order is 君と過ごした日々を胸に、もう迷わない, "With the days I spent with you in my heart, I won't hesitate anymore." Placing the phrase after the verb (倒置法) ends the chorus on the memory instead of on a verb. The line can also be heard as a free-standing tag with its own understood main verb ("(I'll go on) holding…"). The meaning is much the same either way.
- **Link to line 019.** 神様は僕らを切り離して ("God cut us apart, and …") opens the next section, so nothing in 018 carries over. Its 僕ら ("we": 僕 + 君) is the same pair as in 018, the speaker who spent those days and 君. Right after "the days I spent with you", lines 019–020 say that God separated the two and drew 別々の線 ("separate lines").
- The past tense た marks those days as over. Later lines say this directly: ここにはいない君 ("you, who are not here", line 024) and 君だけがいない世界 ("a world where only you are missing", line 032).
- 胸 (literally "chest") is the usual Japanese seat of feelings and memories, where English says "heart". 心に is also possible, but 胸に is the more common choice in this pattern.
