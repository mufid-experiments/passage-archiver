# Line 026 · 思いやる余裕なんて無くて

## Context

- Line 025 (previous): 自分の事でいっぱいで つらくて
- **Line 026 (explained here): 思いやる余裕なんて無くて**
- Line 027 (next): 慰めて欲しい、なんて

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: te-form link (〜無くて) in the sentence that runs from line 025 to line 028.

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. 思いやる "be considerate of, think of (others)" is a one-verb relative clause on 余裕 "room, leeway". The clause says what the room would be for, so 余裕 is not the verb's subject or object. なんて stands where the subject particle が would be (余裕がない → 余裕なんてない) and adds a dismissive emphasis: "no room at all, nothing of the kind". 無くて is the te-form of the i-adjective 無い "there is no". Two things are unstated, as is normal: whom the speaker would think of (人を "others", or 相手を "the other person") and the speaker themselves (the わたし of line 020). The line adds the third link to the chain begun on line 025: いっぱいで, つらくて, and now 無くて. The chain goes on through the quote on line 027 to its main predicate, 掛けてしまったの, on line 028. Restored prose, lines 025–028: （わたしは）自分の事でいっぱいで、つらくて、（人を）思いやる余裕なんて無くて、「慰めて欲しい」なんて（言って）、（相手に）負担ばかり掛けてしまったの。

## How it fits together

- This line: `(line 025 →) [[[(人を) 思いやる] 余裕] なんて] [無く て] → line 027`
- Whole sentence: `(わたしは) [自分 の 事 で いっぱい で]、 [つらく て]、 [[(人を) 思いやる] 余裕 なんて 無く て]、 [「慰め て 欲しい」 なんて (言って)]、 (相手に) [負担 ばかり 掛け て しまっ た] の`

Line 025 gives the causes (full of their own problems, hurting) and this line the result: no room to spare for anyone else. Lines 027–028 then say what happened instead. Like the links before it, 無くて has no tense of its own; it takes the past from しまった on line 028.

## Translation

- Literal: "there being no such thing as room to be considerate (of others), and…"
- Natural: "with no room at all to think of anyone else…" (whole sentence, lines 025–028: "I was so caught up in my own problems, and it was all so hard, that I had no room at all to think of anyone else; saying things like 'I want you to comfort me,' I ended up being nothing but a burden.")

## Notes

- **The same shape as line 008.** 努力を積み上げる暇もなく (line 008) and 思いやる余裕なんて無くて are built alike: a relative clause naming what the time or room is for, a noun for time or room, a particle in place of が, and the adjective ない. In the first verse there is no time to build up effort; in the second there is no room to think of others. The first is a complaint about circumstances; the second is a confession.
- **なんて, twice in two lines.** After a noun, as here, なんて dismisses or plays down: お金なんてない "I have no money, none at all", 余裕なんて無い "no room whatsoever". After a clause or a quote, as on line 027 (慰めて欲しい、なんて), it means "something like…", usually with a note of criticism or embarrassment. Both uses colour the stanza as self-reproach: the speaker looks back on themselves with a frown.
- **思いやる and 思いやり.** 思いやる is to put oneself in another's place and act kindly: 相手の気持ちを思いやる "be considerate of the other person's feelings". The noun 思いやり "consideration, compassion" is the everyday word (思いやりのある人 "a caring person"). It comes from 思い "thought" + 遣る "send": sending one's thoughts out to someone. The kanji spelling 思い遣る is rare; kana is standard.
- **余裕.** "Margin, leeway": 時間の余裕 "time to spare", お金の余裕 "money to spare", 心の余裕 "emotional room, peace of mind". 余裕がない "be stretched too thin" describes someone with no capacity left for anything extra. Colloquially, 余裕 on its own can mean "easy" (余裕で勝つ "win easily"). Here it is the 心の余裕 that line 025's いっぱい "full" leaves no room for.
- **無くて in kanji.** Style guides write ない in kana (欠点がない "there are no faults"), as the song does on line 008 (暇もなく). The kanji 無 makes the absence visible on the page, but it is a stylistic choice, like 事 on line 025.
- **Link to lines 025 and 027.** 自分の事 "my own matters" (line 025) and 思いやる "thinking of others" are opposites: the speaker's mind is full of the first, so there is no room for the second. Line 027 shows the result: instead of giving comfort, the speaker asks for it.
