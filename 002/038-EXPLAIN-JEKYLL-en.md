### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="038" %}

The line is grammatical. It is the main clause that line 037’s conditional (〜さえあれば) was waiting for, so the two lines make one sentence. The predicate is the na-adjective <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> with the copula dropped, <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>（だ）, which is ordinary in casual speech and in lyrics. The subject is left out too, as usual: most naturally the speaker (“I’ll be all right”), or things in general (“it’ll be all right”). これからも (“from now on, too”) and きっと (“surely”) both modify <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>. Nothing is inverted or poetic in the grammar, and the line could be said just like this in conversation. The space marks a breath after the time phrase. A stanza break follows, and line 039 starts a new sentence, so nothing carries over. Restored prose: <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じる<ruby>素<rt>す</rt>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>な<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちさえあれば、（わたしは）これからもきっと<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>（だ）。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="038" %}

Line 037 supplies the condition and this line the consequence: given that one thing, all will be well. これからも (when) and きっと (how sure the speaker is) both modify the predicate <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>, whose だ is omitted. The subject is unstated: the speaker (わたし, line 020) or the situation in general. The sentence, and the stanza, end here.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="038" %}

3.2.1 shows that “a state-of-being can be implied without using だ”, as in the casual greeting <ruby>元<rt>げん</rt>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>？ — <ruby>元<rt>げん</rt>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>。 “(Are you) well? — (I’m) well.”, where even the subject goes unsaid; だ would make the statement “more emphatic and forceful”. <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> is a na-adjective (3.4.2), so it works the same way. 3.12.4 has the same word with だよ, <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>だよ “it’s ok, you know”, said to someone else; the bare <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> of the lyric sounds more like something said to oneself. 3.3.3’s inclusive も adds the time ahead to the time already lived, and これから is これ + the から “from” of 3.8.2.

## Notes

### Completes line 037

The whole sentence is <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じる<ruby>素<rt>す</rt>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>な<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちさえあれば、これからもきっと<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>（だ）. It has the familiar shape of これさえあれば<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> “as long as I have this, I’ll be fine”: one condition, then a reassurance. It is also the song’s last new sentence. Lines 039–040 only repeat lines 005–006, so <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> is the last word of new text.

### The shape of the final stanza

Its four two-line sentences alternate between what has been and what lies ahead. Lines 032 (<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>をくれたんだ) and 036 (<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にしてきたんだ) look back, in the past tense with んだ; lines 034 (<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>閉<rt>と</rt></ruby>ざされたりしない) and 038 look forward, in the non-past. これから takes up the <ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby> of line 034, and the stanza ends on a forecast rather than a report.

### これからも

も adds the time ahead to the time already lived: “from now on, too (as up to now)”. これからは would instead mark a break (“from now on, unlike before”), which would suit a clean start after the days rejected on line 030 (あんな<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>は もう<ruby>嫌<rt>いや</rt></ruby>だよ). With も, the reassurance can be heard as resting on continuity: what has carried the speaker so far (031–036) will go on doing so. The same pattern, time word + も, occurs on line 012 (いつも <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>も <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かなくて “always, and now too, (it) doesn’t reach”): there も stretched a failure across time, and here it stretches <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> into the future.

### きっと

An adverb of the speaker’s own conviction: “surely, I’m sure”. It often pairs with a guess, as on lines 017–018 (きっとそこに / <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>初<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>からあった<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>なんだろう, a guess about the past). Here it stands with a bare predicate and looks ahead, so it can sound like a promise to oneself. <ruby>必<rt>かなら</rt></ruby>ず “without fail” would be more categorical, and たぶん “probably” much weaker.

### <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> without だ

<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>だ would be a flat declaration, <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>だよ a reassurance aimed at someone, <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>だろう a guess, and <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>です polite. The bare stem is the everyday casual form and suits talking to oneself. In Chinese, <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> meant “a fine, stalwart man” (in that sense Japanese reads it <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>); from “someone solid you can rely on” it came to mean “secure, safe”, and then “all right”. Its core, <ruby>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> (じょうぶ), now means “sturdy, healthy” (<ruby>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby> “a strong body”). <ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby> appears only here in the song.

### Link to line 039: the refrain returns

Nothing carries over in grammar. After the stanza break, line 039 (<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>く<ruby>香<rt>かお</rt></ruby>り やがて<ruby>緑<rt>みどり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>芽<rt>め</rt>吹<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>いて) starts a new sentence with its own topic, <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>は, and lines 039–040 repeat lines 005–006 word for word. The first time, the refrain followed line 004’s doubt (<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるのかな “I wonder if I can put it into words and get it across”). This time it follows the confidence of lines 037–038, after line 036 (<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にしてきたんだ) has answered that doubt. The spring scene is the same, but what leads into it has changed, so the same breeze, new green and scattering blossoms can be heard first beside a worry and now beside a reassurance. これからも also looks ahead along a line, while the refrain’s また (“again”, line 040) turns in a cycle; the future the speaker is sure of can be heard as running on into the springs still to come.
