### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="002" %}

The line is a grammatical noun phrase, not a sentence. It ends on the noun <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> with no predicate (<ruby>体<rt>たい</rt>言<rt>げん</rt>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>め), so it presents a situation instead of making a statement about it. すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って is the third te-form in the chain that line 001 began (<ruby>揉<rt>も</rt></ruby>めて, <ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>って, すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って), and its subject is the same unstated speaker (the わたし of line 020). ばかり (“nothing but”) follows the te-form, and の links the whole chain to the head noun <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>. As a full sentence it would be something like （わたしは）…すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかりの<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>（を<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>っている） “(I) spend day after day doing nothing but…”, or simply …すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかりの<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>（だ）. The space before <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> is a pause for rhythm; prose would write すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかりの<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>. Line 003 does not continue this phrase. It opens a new sentence, which ends on line 004. Nothing needs correcting. Restored prose, with line 001: <ruby>些<rt>さ</rt>細<rt>さい</rt></ruby>なことで<ruby>揉<rt>も</rt></ruby>めて、<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>って、すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかりの<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="002" %}

The te-forms of line 001 run straight on into すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って, and ばかり follows the last of them. Grammatically ばかり attaches only to すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って, but what it describes is the whole cycle: quarrel, get angry, fail to connect, again and again. The の-phrase then modifies <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>, the head of the two-line phrase. With no predicate, the phrase stands on its own (<ruby>体<rt>たい</rt>言<rt>げん</rt>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>め), and the te-forms take no tense: the <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> is simply the speaker’s present.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="002" %}

5.6.4’s ばかり is so much of something “to the point where there’s nothing else”, as in おばさんばっかり “nothing but middle-aged ladies”. Its examples put it after nouns, and once after a plain verb (<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶばっかり); ばかり after a te-form, as here, has no section of its own. 5.12.1 is the other ばかり, after the past tense: <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたばかり “just ate” (see the note on 〜たばかり below). 3.10.2 is why the line has の, not だ, before <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>: だ cannot be used to modify a noun, and the particles of the next chapter, here the の of 3.11.4, do that work. すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う is a compound of the kind at the end of 4.1.2, a second verb on the stem of the first (<ruby>擦<rt>す</rt></ruby>れ + <ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う). Ending on a noun (<ruby>体<rt>たい</rt>言<rt>げん</rt>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>め) is a matter of style that the book does not take up.

## Notes

### Link to line 001

Line 001 has no predicate of its own: its te-forms <ruby>揉<rt>も</rt></ruby>めて and <ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>って hang open until this line supplies the third link, すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って, and the noun the chain describes, <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>. Together the two lines are a single noun phrase, the song’s opening picture of everyday friction.

### Link to line 003

<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> closes the phrase, and line 003 (<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かって<ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち) starts a new sentence that line 004 completes (<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるのかな). The link is one of meaning: days of すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>い leave behind feelings nobody understands, and doubt about getting them across in words. Line 003 also ends on a bare noun, <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち, so on first hearing it sounds like a second <ruby>体<rt>たい</rt>言<rt>げん</rt>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>め beside <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>; line 004 then shows that <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち is an object (see line 003).

### 〜てばかり, not 〜たばかり

After the te-form, ばかり means “nothing but”: すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかり “always missing each other”, <ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いてばかりいる “do nothing but cry”. After the past た it means “just now”: すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ったばかり “(we) have only just passed each other”. Only the vowel of the verb ending differs, so it is worth hearing carefully. As a predicate, 〜てばかり takes いる (すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかりいる); before a noun it takes の, as here. The song’s ばかり returns twice for too much of something unwanted: <ruby>出<rt>で</rt>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ない<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>ばかりが (line 007) and <ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>ばかり (line 028).

### <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> as a noun

<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> is usually an adverb: <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby>すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う “(we) miss each other every day”. Here it is a noun with a modifier in front of it, the head of the phrase, so it means “days, daily life”: すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかりの<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> “a daily life of nothing but missing each other”. The song closes this picture on line 030, where the speaker rejects あんな<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby> “days like those”.

### すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う, literal and figurative

Literally, two people pass each other going opposite ways, close but without stopping (<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt>達<rt>だち</rt></ruby>とすれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う “pass a friend at the station”). Figuratively, they fail to connect: <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちがすれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う “(our) feelings don’t meet”, and the noun すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>い is the everyday word for that kind of disconnect. After <ruby>揉<rt>も</rt></ruby>めて <ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>って, the figurative sense is the natural one. The same root, <ruby>擦<rt>す</rt></ruby>る (する) “brush, rub”, begins すり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける on line 010 (<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けてしまう, “slip through my outstretched hand”): in both verbs something passes close by and is not held.

### Spelling

The kanji spelling <ruby>擦<rt>す</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う exists, but すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う, with the first element in kana, is the usual one. <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt></ruby> is standard. The song’s kanji choices elsewhere (<ruby>些<rt>さ</rt>細<rt>さい</rt></ruby>, <ruby>揉<rt>も</rt></ruby>めて, <ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない) are discussed at lines 001 and 003.
