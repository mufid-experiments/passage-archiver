# Line 021 · 小さな夢積み上げて

## Context

- Line 020 (previous): それが今の わたしなんだ
- **Line 021 (explained here): 小さな夢積み上げて**
- Line 022 (next): 願い事は暖味で

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: object with を omitted + te-form; the first link of the chain of lines 021–024, which never reaches a main verb.

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. 小さな夢 "small dreams" is the object of 積み上げて "piling up", the te-form of 積み上げる, and the object particle を is dropped (小さな夢を積み上げて), as lyrics often do. The subject is the speaker (the わたし of line 020), unstated as usual. The te-form is open, and it opens a stanza that is one long chain: 積み上げて, 曖昧で (line 022, written 暖味で, see line 022), 必死で (line 023), 立ち止まって (line 024). Each line ends on a link, and none ends on a main verb. Line 020 closes the first half of the song with なんだ, so this line, after the stanza break, starts something new. The tense of the chain is not marked; the stanzas after it (lines 025–030) look back on the past, so the chain can be heard as the start of that story. Restored prose: （わたしは）小さな夢を積み上げて、…

## Reading

- Kana: ちいさな ゆめ つみあげて
- Romaji: chiisana yume tsumiagete

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 小さな | ちいさな (chiisana) | adnominal (連体詞) | small, little; as on line 019 (小さな一歩) |
| 2 | 夢 | ゆめ (yume) | noun | dream; both a dream in sleep and an aspiration; here aspirations. Object of 積み上げて, with を omitted |
| 3 | 積み上げ | つみあげ (tsumiage) | ichidan compound verb (複合動詞) 積み上げる = 積む "pile" + 上げる "raise", continuative form (連用形) before て | to pile up, to build up; as on line 008 |
| 4 | て | て (te) | conjunctive particle (接続助詞), te-form | "…and": the first link of the chain of lines 021–024 |

## How it fits together

- This line: `(わたしは) [小さな 夢] (を) [積み上げ て] → line 022`
- The stanza: `(わたしは) [小さな 夢 (を) 積み上げ て]、[願い事 は 曖昧 で]、[追いかける の に 必死 で]、[息 (を) 切らせ 立ち止まっ て] → (no main verb)`

小さな modifies 夢, and the noun phrase is the object of 積み上げて. The te-form adds the first scene of the stanza. In line 022 the subject switches to 願い事 "the wishes", and in lines 023–024 it returns to the speaker. A te-chain can switch subjects like this. Nothing in the stanza gives the chain a tense or a main verb.

## Translation

- Literal: "piling up small dreams, and…"
- Natural: "Stacking up little dreams, one on top of another…"

## Notes

- **The dropped を.** In prose the object particle would be said: 小さな夢を積み上げて. Lyrics often drop を for rhythm, as on line 003 (気持ち, object of line 004's verbs) and on line 024 (息切らせ = 息を切らせ). The meaning does not change.
- **積み上げる, a second time.** On line 008 (努力を積み上げる暇もなく "with no time even to build up my efforts"), nothing could be built. Here the speaker does build something, but out of small dreams. On line 031 the verb becomes 積み重ねる (積み重ねてきた事だけが), and what has been built up turns out to be the one thing that gave them strength. Piled-up dreams can also topple; the next lines show how unsteady the pile is (wishes vague, a desperate chase, a stop).
- **小さな, again.** Line 019 has 小さな一歩 "a small step", and this line, opening the second half of the song, has 小さな夢 "small dreams". The song links its halves through the word: the first ends by accepting small progress, and the second starts with small dreams.
- **夢.** Like English "dream", 夢 covers both dreams in sleep (夢を見る "have a dream") and aspirations (夢を叶える "make a dream come true", 夢を追いかける "chase a dream"). The second sense is meant here, and line 023's 追いかける "chase" recalls the stock phrase 夢を追いかける.
- **An even stanza.** All four lines of this stanza have eleven morae, provided line 022's 暖味 is read as あいまい (曖昧): ちいさなゆめつみあげて, ねがいごとはあいまいで, おいかけるのにひっしで, いききらせたちどまって. The lines are short and even, and this is the only stanza in the song in which no line has a space. The even count is one reason to read line 022 as 曖昧 (see line 022).
- **Link to line 020.** Line 020 (それが今の わたしなんだ) ends the long stanza of lines 013–020, and the first half of the song, on an assertion. Nothing carries over in grammar. After the break, the story starts again: from small dreams, through a chase, to a stop out of breath (line 024).
