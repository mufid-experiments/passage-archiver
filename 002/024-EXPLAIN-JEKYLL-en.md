### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="024" %}

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. It holds two actions of the speaker (the わたし of line 020). The first is <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>（を）<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ “running out of breath, panting”. <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせる is a variant of the idiom <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らす, and <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ is its continuative form (<ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>形<rt>けい</rt></ruby>), used to link clauses (<ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>中<rt>ちゅう</rt>止<rt>し</rt>法<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>). The object particle を is dropped, and the two words are written together. The second action follows with no て and no space: <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって “coming to a stop”, the te-form of the compound <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まる. That te-form is the last link of the stanza’s chain (<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて, <ruby>曖<rt>あい</rt>昧<rt>まい</rt></ruby>で, <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で, <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって), and the stanza ends without a main verb. A listener can hear the chain left hanging, or running on into the next stanza up to its first main verb, <ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの on line 028 (see Notes). Prose would write <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせて<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって or <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らし、<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって. Restored prose: （わたしは）<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ、<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって、…

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="024" %}

`(lines 021–024) (わたしは) [小さな 夢 (を) 積み上げ て]、[願い事 は 曖昧 で]、[追いかける の に 必死 で]、[息 (を) 切らせ 立ち止まっ て] → (no main verb)`
{: .tree-src}

The two actions follow each other: breath gives out, and then the legs stop. <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ links without て, the written <ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>中<rt>ちゅう</rt>止<rt>し</rt>法<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>, and <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって links with it, so the line ends on the more open of the two. The stanza’s four links add up to a story (small dreams, vague wishes, a desperate chase, a stop) without a main verb to give it a tense.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="024" %}

6.1.4 is the verb stem in place of the te-form, the written way of linking actions, which gives <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ; the spoken te-form <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって (4.4.2) follows at once. In form, <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせる is the causative of <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る: 5.1.1 makes the causative of a godan verb by attaching せる where ない would go (<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせる). In the idiom <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせる, though, it means no more than <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らす “be out of breath”. <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まる is <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つ + <ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まる, a compound of the kind at the end of 4.1.2. The dropped を after <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby> is the casual habit noted in 4.5.1: “particles also get punted off left and right”.

## Notes

### <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らす and <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせる

Both mean “be out of breath, pant”: <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らして<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ってきた “came running, out of breath”, <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせて<ruby>駆<rt>か</rt></ruby>けつけた “rushed over, panting”. <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らす is “use up, run out of” (<ruby>在<rt>ざい</rt>庫<rt>こ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らす “run out of stock”). <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせる is in form the causative of <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る “cut”, but in this idiom it is used just like <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らす. The related noun <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt>切<rt>ぎ</rt></ruby>れ (いきぎれ) “shortness of breath” also has a figurative sense, “running out of steam” (<ruby>後<rt>こう</rt>半<rt>はん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt>切<rt>ぎ</rt></ruby>れする “flag in the second half”), and both senses fit a chase that has worn the speaker out.

### Two verbs pressed together

<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ runs straight into <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって with no て, no comma and no space, as <ruby>倒<rt>たお</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きて does on line 014 in the first half of the song. There the compressed pair was falling and getting up again. Here it is losing one’s breath and stopping, with no getting up.

### The stanza never closes

Lines 021–024 end on four open links and no main verb, so the stanza itself stops mid-sentence, like the speaker. It can be heard as left hanging (<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いさし), or as running on across the stanza break into the next chain (lines 025–027, いっぱいで, つらくて, <ruby>無<rt>な</rt></ruby>くて, なんて) up to the first main verb after it, <ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの on line 028. Line 012 (<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かなくて) is the same kind of open ending, in the first half of the song.

### <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まる

<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まる is “stop (walking), stand still”, often to look at something or to think (<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>める is close). The same verb returns on line 033, in the conditional <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まっても “even if I stop”. Here the speaker actually stops; there, stopping is allowed for in the future, and the future still will not close (<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>閉<rt>と</rt></ruby>ざされたりしない, line 034).

### The end of a run

The stanza moves from piling up dreams (line 021) through a chase (line 023) to a halt, the same arc as lines 013–015 in the first half (trying, falling, unable to move forward). The difference is what comes next. After line 015 the scenery flows on (line 016). After this line, the next stanza turns inward (line 025, <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>でいっぱいで “so caught up in my own problems”).

### Link to line 025

After the stanza break, line 025 opens a new chain on what the chase left behind: the speaker, stopped and out of breath, with no room in their head for anything but their own troubles. That chain ends on line 028, with a regret (<ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの “all I did was put a burden on you”).
