# Line 016 · やがて景色は流れてゆく

## Context

- Line 015 (previous): 前に進めずに もどかしさ募る
- **Line 016 (explained here): やがて景色は流れてゆく**
- Line 017 (next): 掴み取った何かは きっとそこに

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — complete sentence: topic 景色は + predicate 流れてゆく; it begins and ends on this line.

The line is a grammatical, complete sentence with nothing omitted. やがて "before long, in time" is an adverb, 景色は "the scenery" is the topic and subject, and 流れてゆく is the te-form of 流れる "flow" plus the subsidiary verb ゆく "go on ~ing, ~ away". ゆく is the literary form of いく, and its terminal form ends the sentence. Line 015 closes its own sentence on 募る, so this line starts a new one, and line 017 starts another. Two things are open, the meaning rather than the grammar: whether the scenery flows past because the speaker is moving, or because time is passing (see Notes). The register is slightly literary (やがて, ゆく), like the refrain (lines 005–006). Restored prose: やがて景色は流れてゆく。

## Reading

- Kana: やがて けしき は ながれて ゆく
- Romaji: yagate keshiki wa nagarete yuku

## How it fits together

`やがて [景色 は [流れ て ゆく]]。`

やがて modifies the predicate, 景色は is its topic, and 流れてゆく is one predicate: the te-form carries the lexical meaning, and ゆく adds continuation and movement away. The non-past describes a process now under way: the scenery is starting to stream by and will keep doing so.

## Translation

- Literal: "before long, the scenery goes on flowing"
- Natural: "And in time, the scenery starts to stream past."

## Notes

- **What does 景色が流れる mean?** The phrase is used of scenery seen while moving: 車窓の景色が流れる "the scenery streams past the train window", 景色が後ろへ流れていく "the view slips away behind (as one runs)". On that reading the speaker, stuck on line 015 (前に進めずに "unable to move forward"), is in fact moving again, because the view is going by. 流れる is also the verb for time passing (月日が流れる "the months and days go by"), so the line can also mean that time moves on and the scenes of life change, as the seasons do in the refrain. Both readings carry the speaker past the frustration of lines 013–015.
- **やがて, again.** The song uses やがて twice, here and on line 005 (やがて緑は芽吹いて "soon new green buds appear"). Both times it marks change that comes by itself, given time, and both times the subject is something outside the speaker, marked with は: 緑は, 景色は.
- **ゆく and く.** The literary ゆく appears where the song describes the world around the speaker: 散ってゆく (lines 006, 040) and 流れてゆく here. The casual contractions are used for the speaker's own state: 増えてく (= 増えていく, line 007) and 抱えてる (= 抱えている, line 011). This line, with its literary ゆく, sounds closer to the refrain than to the lines just before it.
- **景色.** 景色 (けしき) is the everyday word for "view, scenery". Its reading is irregular: 景 is normally けい. The word is in the appendix of special readings (付表) of the 常用漢字 list. 風景 (ふうけい) is close in meaning but more "landscape, scene", also used of pictures, and 景観 (けいかん) is formal, as in planning.
- **流れる.** Besides water, time and scenery, 流れる is used of sound and news (音楽が流れる "music is playing", ニュースが流れる "news goes out"), and of plans that fall through (計画が流れる "the plan is called off"). Its transitive partner is 流す "let flow, wash away", and the passive 流される "be swept along" describes someone carried by events.
- **The hinge of the stanza.** Lines 013–015 are all effort and failure, ending on 募る. This short line is a pause and a turn. Lines 017–018 then reflect (掴み取った何かは きっとそこに / 最初からあった物なんだろう "whatever I grasped was surely there from the start"), and lines 019–020 accept the present (それが今の わたしなんだ "that's who I am now").
- **Link to line 017.** ゆく ends the sentence, and line 017 opens the next with a new topic: 掴み取った何かは "the something (I) grasped". The verb of grasping comes after the scenery has flowed: once the speaker has moved on, something is in their hand.
