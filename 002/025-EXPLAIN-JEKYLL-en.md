### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="025" %}

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. It holds two predicates, both states, and their subject, the speaker (the わたし of line 020), is unstated. The first is the na-adjective いっぱいだ “full” in its continuative form (<ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>形<rt>けい</rt></ruby>) いっぱいで, with <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>で saying what the speaker is full of. The second, つらくて, is the te-form of the i-adjective つらい “painful, hard to bear”. The space marks a pause where prose would put a comma. The line has two different で: the first is a case particle, the second is the ending of いっぱいで (see the words and Notes). Both links stay open. The chain runs on through <ruby>無<rt>な</rt></ruby>くて (line 026) and the quote on line 027 to its main predicate, <ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの, on line 028. Line 024 ends the previous stanza on an open te-form of its own (<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって); after the stanza break, this line starts a new sentence. It is ordinary spoken Japanese, with nothing inverted or poetic. Restored prose: （わたしは）<ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>でいっぱいで、つらくて、（<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>を）<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いやる<ruby>余<rt>よ</rt>裕<rt>ゆう</rt></ruby>なんて<ruby>無<rt>な</rt></ruby>くて、「<ruby>慰<rt>なぐさ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>欲<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>しい」なんて（<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>って）、（<ruby>相<rt>あい</rt>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>に）<ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="025" %}

`(lines 025–028) (わたしは) [自分 の 事 で いっぱい で]、 [つらく て]、 [[(人を) 思いやる] 余裕 なんて 無く て]、 [「慰め て 欲しい」 なんて (言って)]、 (相手に) [負担 ばかり 掛け て しまっ た] の`
{: .tree-src}

<ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>で depends on いっぱい: it names what the speaker (or their head or heart, <ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby> / <ruby>胸<rt>むね</rt></ruby>) is full of. いっぱいで and つらくて are two links with the same unstated subject. いっぱいで can be heard as the cause of つらくて (“so taken up with my own problems that it hurt”), or the two as states side by side. Neither has tense of its own. Both take the past from しまった on line 028, so the sentence looks back: “I was…, it was…”. Together with <ruby>無<rt>な</rt></ruby>くて on line 026 they set out the causes, and line 028 gives what came of them.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="025" %}

4.4.1 chains descriptions: a na-adjective takes で, and an i-adjective replaces い with くて (<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>くて), which gives いっぱいで and つらくて. Its own example has both kinds in a row: <ruby>田<rt>た</rt>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>さんは、お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちで、かっこよくて、<ruby>魅<rt>み</rt>力<rt>りょく</rt>的<rt>てき</rt></ruby>ですね “Tanaka-san is rich, handsome, and charming, isn’t he?”. It adds that such a で “obviously cannot be the context particle で” and is better thought of as a stand-in for だ. That is the difference between the two で of this line: the first is the で of 3.8.4, here for what something is full of, a use the book does not show. 3.11.4 is the の of <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>.

## Notes

### The two で

The first で is the case particle that marks what something is full of: <ruby>会<rt>かい</rt>場<rt>じょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>でいっぱいだ “the hall is full of people”, <ruby>胸<rt>むね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>不<rt>ふ</rt>安<rt>あん</rt></ruby>でいっぱいだ “my heart is full of worry”. The second belongs to the na-adjective いっぱいだ, whose continuative form (<ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>形<rt>けい</rt></ruby>) is いっぱいで. Turn the line into a finished sentence and the difference shows: in <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>でいっぱいだった “I was full of my own problems”, the first で stays, and only the second changes (to だった).

### Link to line 024

Line 024 (<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって) closes the stanza of lines 021–024, a chain of te-forms that never reaches a main verb of its own. This line opens a new stanza and a new sentence. Because <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって is left open, though, a listener can also hear the two chains run together up to the first main verb after it, <ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの on line 028. Either way the story runs on: the previous stanza ends with the speaker stopped and out of breath after a desperate chase (<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかけるのに<ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で, line 023), and this one opens on what that left behind, a head full of their own problems.

### いっぱい

Written <ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt>杯<rt>ぱい</rt></ruby>, it literally means “one cupful” (コーヒーを<ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt>杯<rt>ぱい</rt></ruby> “a cup of coffee”); in the senses “full” and “a lot” it is usually written in kana. As a na-adjective it means “full” (お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がいっぱい “I’m full”), and with <ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby> or <ruby>胸<rt>むね</rt></ruby>, “so full of something that there is room for nothing else” (<ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>がそのことでいっぱい “I can’t think of anything else”, <ruby>胸<rt>むね</rt></ruby>がいっぱい “overcome with emotion”). Close expressions: <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>のことで<ruby>精<rt>せい</rt>一<rt>いっ</rt>杯<rt>ぱい</rt></ruby> (せいいっぱい) “have one’s hands full with one’s own affairs”, and colloquial いっぱいいっぱい “maxed out, at one’s limit”. Line 026 draws the consequence: when you are full, there is no <ruby>余<rt>よ</rt>裕<rt>ゆう</rt></ruby>, no room to spare.

### つらい

The kanji <ruby>辛<rt>つら</rt></ruby>い has two readings, つらい “painful, hard to bear” and からい “spicy, hot”; kana rules out the second. つらい is for hardship that weighs on you (つらい<ruby>別<rt>わか</rt></ruby>れ “a painful parting”), while <ruby>苦<rt>くる</rt></ruby>しい (くるしい) leans toward physical distress or pressure (<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>苦<rt>くる</rt></ruby>しい “it’s hard to breathe”). In a plain statement a feeling adjective describes the speaker’s own feeling, so it is the speaker who is hurting here.

### <ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby> in kanji

The song writes <ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby> in kanji here and on lines 007 and 031 (<ruby>出<rt>で</rt>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ない<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>, <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>重<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>ねてきた<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>), but こと in kana on line 001 (<ruby>些<rt>さ</rt>細<rt>さい</rt></ruby>なこと). Style guides prefer kana for the formal noun; the kanji is a stylistic choice, like <ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない on line 003 and <ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby> on line 018. After a word for a person, 〜のこと means “things to do with ~” or simply that person: <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>のことが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き “I like you”, <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>のことばかり<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>える “think only of oneself”. Here <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby> is the opposite of what line 026 says there was no room for: <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いやる, turning one’s thoughts to others.
