# Line 032 · 進む力をくれたんだ

## Context

- Line 031 (previous): 積み重ねてきた事だけが 前に
- **Line 032 (explained here): 進む力をくれたんだ**
- Line 033 (next): 躓いても 立ち止まっても 決して

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 031: object + くれた (giving toward the speaker) + explanatory んだ.

The line is grammatical and ends the sentence that line 031 began. 進む "move forward" is a one-verb relative clause on 力 "strength". Together with 前に at the end of line 031, it makes 前に進む力 "the strength to move forward", and を marks that as the object. The verb くれた is the past of くれる "give (to me)", the giving verb used when the receiver is the speaker or someone on their side. Its subject is on line 031: 積み重ねてきた事だけ "only what (I) had built up", a thing, not a person, which Japanese readily allows with くれる. The ending ん + だ (the explanatory の, contracted, plus the copula) presents the sentence as a conclusion the speaker has reached by looking back. The receiver (わたしに "to me") is unstated, as is normal with くれる. Line 033 starts a new sentence. Restored prose: 積み重ねてきた事だけが、（わたしに）前に進む力をくれたんだ。

## Reading

- Kana: すすむ ちから を くれた ん だ
- Romaji: susumu chikara o kureta n da

## How it fits together

`[積み重ねてきた事] だけ が (line 031) [[前 に (line 031) 進む] 力] を (わたしに) [くれ た] ん だ。`

進む is a relative clause of the content type: 力 is not its subject or object, and the clause says what the strength is for, as in 生きる力 "the strength to live". くれた takes its subject from line 031 and its object from this line. The past tense looks back over the time just rejected on line 030: through all of it, one thing was giving the speaker strength.

## Translation

- Literal: "(only the things I had piled up) gave (me) strength to advance"
- Natural: "(It was only what I'd built up, bit by bit, that) gave me the strength to keep moving forward."

## Notes

- **くれる: giving to the speaker.** Japanese has three verbs for giving and receiving. あげる is giving away from the speaker's side, くれる is giving toward the speaker's side, and もらう is receiving. Because くれる always points at the speaker, the receiver わたしに can be left out. The giver here is abstract, and Japanese uses くれる freely with such givers: この歌が勇気をくれた "this song gave me courage", 時間が答えをくれる "time will give me the answer".
- **From others to oneself.** Earlier in the song, the speaker wanted others to act for them: to understand (分かって貰えない, line 003) and to comfort (慰めて欲しい, line 027). Here the giver is the speaker's own accumulated effort. The strength came from within, through what they themselves had built.
- **んだ, looking back.** The past + んだ explains, with the weight of a discovery: "that's what it was that…". The final stanza alternates between looking back and looking ahead. Lines 032 and 036 (言葉にしてきたんだ) end on the past + んだ, and lines 034 (未来は閉ざされたりしない) and 038 (きっと大丈夫) on the non-past. This is the first of the stanza's four two-line sentences.
- **力をくれる.** 力をくれる "give strength" and 力になる "be a help" (君の力になりたい "I want to help you") are everyday phrases. 力 is also physical strength (力が強い "strong"), ability (日本語の力 "one's Japanese ability") and power (自然の力 "the power of nature"). With 進む in front, it is the strength to keep walking.
- **The path, resumed.** Line 015 (前に進めずに) was the speaker unable to move forward; line 024 (立ち止まって) was the speaker coming to a stop. This line gives them the strength to go on, and line 033 allows that they may stumble and stop again (躓いても 立ち止まっても) without the future closing.
- **Link to line 033.** んだ ends the sentence. Line 033 begins the next one with two concessive clauses, 躓いても 立ち止まっても "even if I stumble, even if I stop", and the adverb 決して "never", whose negative arrives on line 034.
