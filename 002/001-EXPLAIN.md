## Line 001 · 些細なことで 揉めて 怒って

### Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: te-form chain with no predicate; it runs on into line 002.

The line is grammatical but is not a sentence. 些細なことで ("over little things") gives the cause, and 揉めて and 怒って are the te-forms of two verbs, one event after the other ("quarrel, get angry, and…"). A te-form has no tense and cannot end a sentence, so the line stays open: the chain goes on with a third te-form, すれ違って, at the start of line 002, and the whole sequence ends up describing the noun 毎日 there. The subject, the speaker (the わたし of line 020), is not stated, and neither is the person the quarrel is with; both omissions are normal in Japanese. The spaces are pauses for rhythm: prose would drop the first and put a comma for the second. Restored prose, with line 002: 些細なことで揉めて、怒って、すれ違ってばかりの毎日。 Nothing needs correcting.

- Literal: "over trivial things, quarrelling, getting angry, and…"
- Natural: "Fighting over little things, getting angry…"

## Line 002 · すれ違ってばかりの 毎日

### Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: noun-ending phrase (体言止め) that completes the phrase begun on line 001.

The line is a grammatical noun phrase, not a sentence: it ends on the noun 毎日 with no predicate (体言止め), presenting a situation instead of making a statement. すれ違って is the third te-form in the chain begun on line 001 (揉めて, 怒って, すれ違って). ばかり ("nothing but") follows it, and の links the chain to the head noun 毎日 ("days"). As a full sentence it would be something like （わたしは）…すれ違ってばかりの毎日（を送っている） "(I) spend day after day doing nothing but…", or simply …すれ違ってばかりの毎日（だ）. The space before 毎日 is a pause for rhythm; prose would write すれ違ってばかりの毎日. Nothing is ungrammatical.

### Reading

- Kana: すれちがって ばかり の / まいにち
- Romaji: surechigatte bakari no / mainichi

### How it fits together

`[[(line 001: 些細なことで 揉めて 怒って) すれ違っ て] ばかり] の 毎日`

The te-forms of line 001 run on into すれ違って, and ばかり follows the last of them. The の-phrase then modifies 毎日, which heads the whole two-line phrase. Grammatically ばかり attaches to すれ違って, but what repeats is the whole cycle: quarrel, get angry, fail to connect, 毎日 "every day". With no predicate, the phrase stands on its own (体言止め).

### Translation

- Literal: "days of nothing but passing each other by"
- Natural: "day after day of never quite connecting" (with line 001: "Day after day of fighting over little things, getting angry, and never quite connecting")

## Notes

- **How lines 001 and 002 connect.** They are one noun phrase, not a sentence. Line 001 has no predicate: its te-forms 揉めて and 怒って run straight on into すれ違って at the start of line 002, and ばかりの then turns the whole chain into a description of 毎日. Prose: 些細なことで揉めて、怒って、すれ違ってばかりの毎日 "days of nothing but fighting over little things, getting angry, and never quite connecting". The register is plain and conversational, and the song opens on a picture of everyday friction rather than a statement.
- **What ばかり covers.** A single ばかり can cap a whole chain of te-forms: 食べて寝てばかりいる "do nothing but eat and sleep". Heard that way, quarrelling, getting angry and failing to connect are one cycle that repeats 毎日, "every day". After a te-form, ばかり is followed by いる in a predicate (すれ違ってばかりいる "keep missing each other") and by の before a noun, as here. ばかり returns at line 007 (出来ない事ばかりが) and line 028 (負担ばかり掛けてしまったの), each time for too much of something unwanted.
- **Who is quarrelling?** 揉める and すれ違う both involve two sides (〜と揉める "quarrel with ~", 〜とすれ違う "pass ~ by"), but neither line names them. The speaker is the わたし of line 020, the song's only first-person pronoun. The other party is never named. Lines 027–028 (慰めて欲しい、なんて / 負担ばかり掛けてしまったの) also imply someone close, who may or may not be the same person.
- **すれ違う, literal and figurative.** Literally, two people passing each other going opposite ways, close but without stopping (駅で友達とすれ違う "pass a friend at the station"). Figuratively, failing to connect: 気持ちがすれ違う "(our) feelings don't meet", and the noun すれ違い is the everyday word for that kind of disconnect between people. After 揉めて 怒って, the figurative sense is the natural one. In compounds, 違う often means "cross, go past each other" or "fail to match": 行き違う "cross (in transit), miss each other", 食い違う "conflict, fail to match".
- **揉める and 怒る.** 揉める (もめる) is "quarrel, get into a dispute", of arguments that turn messy: お金のことで揉める "argue over money", 会議が揉める "the meeting gets contentious". It shares its kanji with the godan verb 揉む (もむ) "rub, knead", a different verb. 怒る is read おこる in everyday Japanese; いかる is literary, though the noun 怒り is read いかり. 怒る can take に for the person one is angry with (友達に怒る) and, colloquially, を for "scold" (子供を怒る); here it has neither and simply means "get angry".
- **Spelling.** 些 and 揉, like 貰 on line 003, are not on the 常用漢字 (Jōyō) list, so newspapers normally write ささい and もめる in kana; the kanji here are a stylistic choice. 些細 is 些 "a little" (as in 些か いささか "somewhat") + 細 "fine, small". こと is in kana on this line, while the song writes 事 at lines 007, 025 and 031 (and in 願い事, line 022). Both spellings are standard, and the difference carries no meaning.
- **Link to line 003.** The 体言止め on 毎日 closes the phrase. Lines 003–004 are a new sentence (分かって貰えない気持ち / 言葉にして 伝えられるのかな), joined to these lines by meaning rather than grammar: the すれ違い of lines 001–002 leads into feelings that nobody understands, and into the doubt about putting them into words.
