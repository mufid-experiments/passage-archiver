# Line 033 · 躓いても 立ち止まっても 決して

## Context

- Line 032 (previous): 進む力をくれたんだ
- **Line 033 (explained here): 躓いても 立ち止まっても 決して**
- Line 034 (next): 未来は閉ざされたりしない

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: two concessive ても clauses plus the adverb 決して, whose negative (and the main clause) come on line 034.

The line is grammatical but has no predicate of its own. 躓いても and 立ち止まっても are two parallel concessive clauses (〜ても、〜ても "even if…, even if…"), and their subject, the speaker (わたし, line 020), is unstated, as is normal. 決して "never" is an adverb that can only be completed by a negative, so the line stops before its statement is finished: the negative arrives on line 034 as the ない of 閉ざされたりしない, together with the main clause and its topic, 未来は. Line 032 closes its sentence with the terminal だ of くれたんだ, so this line starts a new sentence. The one marked feature is the position of 決して. The neutral order puts it after the topic, just before the predicate it negates (未来は決して閉ざされたりしない). Placing it before the topic is also possible in prose, as an emphatic order, and here the line break makes the listener wait for the negative. Restored prose: 躓いても、立ち止まっても、未来は決して閉ざされたりしない。

## Reading

- Kana: つまずいて も / たちどまって も / けっして
- Romaji: tsumazuite mo / tachidomatte mo / kesshite

## How it fits together

`[躓い て も] ／ [立ち止まっ て も] ／ 決して → line 034 [未来 は] [閉ざさ れ たり し ない]`

The two ても clauses are parallel, and both attach to the main predicate on line 034: "whether (I) stumble or (I) stop, …". Their subject is the unstated speaker, while the main clause has a different one, 未来 (topic は). A ても clause states a condition that does not change the outcome: whatever happens there, the main clause still holds. 決して modifies the negated predicate 閉ざされたりしない. Like 全然…ない and 少しも…ない, it announces a negative ending before the verb arrives.

## Translation

- Literal: "even if (I) stumble, even if (I) stop, never…"
- Natural: "Even if I stumble, even if I stop, (the future will) never (be closed off)"

## Notes

- **Link to lines 032 and 034.** Lines 031–032 form a finished sentence: what the speaker has built up gave her the strength to 前に進む "move forward". This line starts a new one about the things that interrupt that movement, tripping and stopping, and line 034 completes it: 躓いても、立ち止まっても、未来は決して閉ざされたりしない "Even if I stumble, even if I stop, the future will never be closed off."
- **決して…ない.** In modern Japanese 決して pairs only with a negative (決して忘れない "I'll never forget") or a prohibition (決して開けるな "never open it"). It cannot mean "definitely" in a positive sentence; that is 必ず or 絶対に. It began as the te-form of 決する "decide", but it is now a fixed adverb. 絶対に…ない says much the same in a more everyday tone; 決して sounds more earnest and slightly written.
- **Line-final pointers.** Three times in this stanza a line ends on a word that grammatically belongs with the next line: 前に (031) goes with 進む (032), 決して (033) with the negative on 034, and だけど (035) with the clause on 036. Each time the line break holds the sentence open for a moment.
- **The walking metaphor.** 躓く (trip over something underfoot) and 立ち止まる (stop walking) continue the song's path imagery: 前に進めずに "unable to move forward" (015), 小さな一歩 "a small step" (019), 前に進む力 (031–032). The song has already shown both kinds of setback: the speaker stopped, out of breath, on line 024 (息切らせ立ち止まって), and fell on lines 014 (倒れ起きて) and 029 (立ち上がれなくて). The ても clauses allow for them happening again.
- **躓く.** Dictionaries trace つまずく to 爪 (つま, the tip of the foot) + 突く (つく, "strike"), which is why the kana spelling つまづく is also permitted, though つまずく is standard. The literal use takes に for what one trips on (石に躓く "trip over a stone"); figuratively it means to fail at some point in life (人生に躓く). 躓 is not among the 常用漢字, so everyday writing uses kana; like 貰 (003) and 竦 (029), the kanji is a stylistic choice.
