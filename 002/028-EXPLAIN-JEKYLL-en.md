### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="028" %}

The line is grammatical and ends the sentence that began on line 025. Its predicate, <ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまった, is the first main verb since the stanza began: <ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける “put a burden (on someone)” + しまう “end up, to one’s regret”, in the past. ばかり “nothing but” stands in place of を (<ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける → <ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける): all the speaker gave was burden. The sentence ends on the particle の, which explains the situation softly, like a quieter version of んだ. The person burdened, who would be marked with に (<ruby>相<rt>あい</rt>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>に, あなたに), is unstated, as is the speaker (the わたし of line 020). With this past tense, the whole chain of lines 025–027 is told as the past. Nothing needs correcting. A stanza break follows, and line 029 opens the two-line stanza of lines 029–030. Restored prose, lines 025–028: （わたしは）<ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>でいっぱいで、つらくて、（<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>を）<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いやる<ruby>余<rt>よ</rt>裕<rt>ゆう</rt></ruby>なんて<ruby>無<rt>な</rt></ruby>くて、「<ruby>慰<rt>なぐさ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>欲<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>しい」なんて（<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>って）、（<ruby>相<rt>あい</rt>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>に）<ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="028" %}

`(lines 025–028) (わたしは) [自分 の 事 で いっぱい で]、 [つらく て]、 [[(人を) 思いやる] 余裕 なんて 無く て]、 [「慰め て 欲しい」 なんて (言って)]、 (相手に) [負担 ばかり 掛け て しまっ た] の`
{: .tree-src}

The chain of states and actions (full, hurting, no room, asking for comfort) ends in a single result. The te-forms take their tense from しまった, so the sentence looks back: “I was…, I had no…, and I ended up…”. The final の presents the whole sentence as an explanation, as if telling someone what really happened.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="028" %}

5.3.1 is しまう after a te-form, for an action “done or happened unintentionally”, and “the tense is decided by the tense of しまう”: its past here puts the whole sentence of lines 025–028 in the past, as in そのケーキを<ruby>全<rt>ぜん</rt>部<rt>ぶ</rt>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてしまった “oops, I ate that whole cake”. 3.11.5 is the explanatory の at the end of a sentence (<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいの “the thing is that (I’m) busy now”). The book says that without だ it “sounds very soft and feminine”; in a lyric it is simply soft, a quiet confession, and says nothing certain about the speaker. 5.6.4 is ばかり for so much of one thing that there is nothing else, here standing where を would be.

## Notes

### 〜てしまった: regret

しまう makes the action complete and, very often, regrettable: <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってしまった “I said it (and wish I hadn’t)”. Casual speech contracts it to 〜ちゃった (<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けちゃった), but the song keeps the full form, as on line 010 (<ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けてしまう). There it was non-past, something that keeps happening to the speaker. Here it is past, something the speaker did and now regrets.

### <ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける and its relatives

The same かける appears in several fixed phrases about what one causes others: <ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける “be a burden”, <ruby>迷<rt>めい</rt>惑<rt>わく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける “cause trouble”, <ruby>心<rt>しん</rt>配<rt>ぱい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける “make someone worry”, <ruby>手<rt>て</rt>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける “put someone to trouble”. In these senses the verb is often written in kana (<ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>をかける). <ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける “put strain on the body” shows the literal side of <ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>, a load to carry.

### ばかり, a third time

すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ってばかりの (line 002), <ruby>出<rt>で</rt>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ない<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>ばかりが (line 007), and now <ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>ばかり: each time ばかり marks too much of something unwanted. Here it stands in place of を, the way も and なんて stood in place of が on lines 008 and 026. In the first two the trouble happens to the speaker; here the speaker causes it, which sharpens the self-reproach.

### Sentence-final の

In a plain statement, a bare の with falling intonation explains or confides: “the thing is, I…”. It is softer than のだ / んだ and is common in gentle or intimate speech. Compare the song’s んだ endings (line 020 わたしなんだ, line 032 くれたんだ, line 036 <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にしてきたんだ), which assert a conclusion. This の sounds like a confession told to someone, perhaps the very person who was burdened.

### Who was burdened?

The lyric never says. The natural candidate is the person asked for comfort on line 027, someone close. The other party in the quarrels of lines 001–002 is never named either, so it may or may not be the same person.
