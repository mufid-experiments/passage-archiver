# Line 025 · 自分の事でいっぱいで つらくて

## Context

- Line 024 (previous): 息切らせ立ち止まって
- **Line 025 (explained here): 自分の事でいっぱいで つらくて**
- Line 026 (next): 思いやる余裕なんて無くて

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: two te-form links that open the sentence ending on line 028.

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. It holds two predicates, both states, and their subject, the speaker (the わたし of line 020), is unstated. The first is the na-adjective いっぱいだ "full" in its continuative form (連用形) いっぱいで, with 自分の事で saying what the speaker is full of. The second, つらくて, is the te-form of the i-adjective つらい "painful, hard to bear". The space marks a pause where prose would put a comma. The line has two different で: the first is a case particle, the second is the ending of いっぱいで (see the table and Notes). Both links stay open. The chain runs on through 無くて (line 026) and the quote on line 027 to its main predicate, 掛けてしまったの, on line 028. Line 024 ends the previous stanza on an open te-form of its own (立ち止まって); after the stanza break, this line starts a new sentence. It is ordinary spoken Japanese, with nothing inverted or poetic. Restored prose: （わたしは）自分の事でいっぱいで、つらくて、（人を）思いやる余裕なんて無くて、「慰めて欲しい」なんて（言って）、（相手に）負担ばかり掛けてしまったの。

## Reading

- Kana: じぶん の こと で いっぱい で / つらくて
- Romaji: jibun no koto de ippai de / tsurakute

| 2 | の | の (no) | case particle (格助詞), attributive | links 自分 to 事: "my own (affairs)" |
| 3 | 事 | こと (koto) | formal noun (形式名詞) | "matters, things"; 自分の事 = "my own affairs, my own problems". Usually written in kana, as こと is on line 001 |

| 6 | で | で (de) | na-adjective ending: continuative form (連用形) of いっぱいだ (same shape as the copula's で) | "(I) was full, and…"; it conjugates (いっぱいだ, いっぱいだった) and here links on to つらくて |

## How it fits together

- This line: `(わたしは) [[[自分 の 事] で] いっぱい で]、 [つらく て] → line 026`
- Whole sentence: `(わたしは) [自分 の 事 で いっぱい で]、 [つらく て]、 [[(人を) 思いやる] 余裕 なんて 無く て]、 [「慰め て 欲しい」 なんて (言って)]、 (相手に) [負担 ばかり 掛け て しまっ た] の`

自分の事で depends on いっぱい: it names what the speaker (or their head or heart, 頭 / 胸) is full of. いっぱいで and つらくて are two links with the same unstated subject. いっぱいで can be heard as the cause of つらくて ("so taken up with my own problems that it hurt"), or the two as states side by side. Neither has tense of its own. Both take the past from しまった on line 028, so the sentence looks back: "I was…, it was…". Together with 無くて on line 026 they set out the causes, and line 028 gives what came of them.

## Translation

- Literal: "being full with my own matters, being painful, and…"
- Natural: "I was so caught up in my own problems, and it was all so hard…" (whole sentence, lines 025–028: "I was so caught up in my own problems, and it was all so hard, that I had no room at all to think of anyone else; saying things like 'I want you to comfort me,' I ended up being nothing but a burden.")

## Notes

- **The two で.** The first で is the case particle that marks what something is full of: 会場は人でいっぱいだ "the hall is full of people", 胸が不安でいっぱいだ "my heart is full of worry". The second belongs to the na-adjective いっぱいだ, whose continuative form (連用形) is いっぱいで. Turn the line into a finished sentence and the difference shows: in 自分の事でいっぱいだった "I was full of my own problems", the first で stays, and only the second changes (to だった).
- **Link to line 024.** Line 024 (息切らせ立ち止まって) closes the stanza of lines 021–024, a chain of te-forms that never reaches a main verb of its own. This line opens a new stanza and a new sentence. Because 立ち止まって is left open, though, a listener can also hear the two chains run together up to the first main verb after it, 掛けてしまったの on line 028. Either way the story runs on: the previous stanza ends with the speaker stopped and out of breath after a desperate chase (追いかけるのに必死で, line 023), and this one opens on what that left behind, a head full of their own problems.
- **いっぱい.** Written 一杯, it literally means "one cupful" (コーヒーを一杯 "a cup of coffee"); in the senses "full" and "a lot" it is usually written in kana. As a na-adjective it means "full" (お腹がいっぱい "I'm full"), and with 頭 or 胸, "so full of something that there is room for nothing else" (頭がそのことでいっぱい "I can't think of anything else", 胸がいっぱい "overcome with emotion"). Close expressions: 自分のことで精一杯 (せいいっぱい) "have one's hands full with one's own affairs", and colloquial いっぱいいっぱい "maxed out, at one's limit". Line 026 draws the consequence: when you are full, there is no 余裕, no room to spare.
- **つらい.** The kanji 辛い has two readings, つらい "painful, hard to bear" and からい "spicy, hot"; kana rules out the second. つらい is for hardship that weighs on you (つらい別れ "a painful parting"), while 苦しい (くるしい) leans toward physical distress or pressure (息が苦しい "it's hard to breathe"). In a plain statement a feeling adjective describes the speaker's own feeling, so it is the speaker who is hurting here.
- **事 in kanji.** The song writes 事 in kanji here and on lines 007 and 031 (出来ない事, 積み重ねてきた事), but こと in kana on line 001 (些細なこと). Style guides prefer kana for the formal noun; the kanji is a stylistic choice, like 貰えない on line 003 and 物 on line 018. After a word for a person, 〜のこと means "things to do with ~" or simply that person: 君のことが好き "I like you", 自分のことばかり考える "think only of oneself". Here 自分の事 is the opposite of what line 026 says there was no room for: 思いやる, turning one's thoughts to others.
