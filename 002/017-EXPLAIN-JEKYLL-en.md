### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="017" %}

The line is grammatical but has no predicate. <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った “grasped, seized” is a relative clause on <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か “something”, and は makes <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か the topic of the sentence. Its subject is the speaker (the わたし of line 020), unstated as usual. The rest of the line is two words that belong with line 018. きっと “surely” pairs with だろう at the end of line 018, and そこに “there” goes with あった “was”. Line 018 completes the sentence: [<ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か]は きっと [そこに<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>初<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>からあった<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>]なんだろう “whatever (I) grasped was surely something that had been there from the very start”. So the line stops partway through a relative clause: そこに is its first word, and the noun it modifies, <ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>, comes on the next line. The space separates the topic from the comment. Line 016 closes its sentence with ゆく, so this line starts a new one. Restored prose: <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かは、きっとそこに<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>初<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>からあった<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>なんだろう。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="017" %}

The sentence has a topic-and-comment shape, X は Y だ: X is the thing grasped, and Y is “a thing that was there from the start”. そこに opens the relative clause that modifies Y’s head noun, <ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>, so the line breaks between a modifier and the rest of its clause. きっと, outside the relative clause, belongs to the main predicate and announces the guess that だろう will complete.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="017" %}

4.3.4 adds the question marker か to question words, which makes <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か “something”, and says “you can treat these words just like any regular nouns”: that is why <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か can take a relative clause and は. 3.10.3 puts the past-tense clause <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った before it, and 3.7.2 gives the past of a godan verb in る: <ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った. 3.8.2’s に marks “the location of an object” as the target of ある, which comes on line 018 (そこに…あった). <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る is a compound of the kind at the end of 4.1.2. きっと and そこ are vocabulary.

## Notes

### Link to line 018

The two halves of the sentence meet across the break. そこに goes with <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>初<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>からあった “was there from the start”, and きっと with <ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>なんだろう “is surely a thing…, I suppose”. Line 018 has no topic of its own; its subject is this line’s <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か.

### きっと…だろう

きっと states the speaker’s own conviction, and だろう marks a guess, so together they mean “surely… I suppose”: a confident conjecture. The same adverb, on line 038 (これからも きっと<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>), goes with a bare predicate and looks ahead. Here it looks back, at something already grasped.

### <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る

<ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>む is “grasp, take hold of”; <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る adds <ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る “take” and so means “take hold and make one’s own”, often of something won through effort: <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る “seize one’s dream”, <ruby>勝<rt>しょう</rt>利<rt>り</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る “seize victory”. Its past tense answers line 010 (<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けてしまう “it slips through my outstretched hand”). There, something escaped the speaker’s reach, again and again; here, they have caught something.

### <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か, unnamed

The song never says what was grasped, and <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か keeps it vague, perhaps because it is hard to name. Lines 019–020 come closest: <ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>るに<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りない <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt>歩<rt>ぽ</rt></ruby>だけど / それが<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>の わたしなんだ “it’s a trifling, small step, but that’s who I am now”. Whatever it was, line 018 says it was not new.

### そこ

そこ, the mid-distance demonstrative (<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt>称<rt>しょう</rt></ruby>), points to a place the speaker and listener both have in mind, here the place where the thing was grasped: “right there”. The thought that what one has been striving for was there all along has a famous form in Japan in <ruby>幸<rt>しあわ</rt></ruby>せの<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>鳥<rt>とり</rt></ruby> “the blue bird of happiness”, from Maeterlinck’s play, which the children search for far and wide and find at home.

### Spelling <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>

<ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby> is not on the <ruby>常<rt>じょう</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>漢<rt>かん</rt>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby> (Jōyō) list, so standard writing has つかむ and つかみ<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る in kana. The kanji is a stylistic choice, like <ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby> (003) and <ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby> (010). Its traditional form is <ruby>摑<rt>つか</rt></ruby>.
