### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="021" %}

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby> “small dreams” is the object of <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて “piling up”, the te-form of <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる, and the object particle を is dropped (<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて), as lyrics often do. The subject is the speaker (the わたし of line 020), unstated as usual. The te-form is open, and it opens a stanza that is one long chain: <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて, <ruby>曖<rt>あい</rt>昧<rt>まい</rt></ruby>で (line 022, written <ruby>暖<rt>あい</rt>味<rt>まい</rt></ruby>で, see line 022), <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で (line 023), <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって (line 024). Each line ends on a link, and none ends on a main verb. Line 020 closes the first half of the song with なんだ, so this line, after the stanza break, starts something new. The tense of the chain is not marked; the stanzas after it (lines 025–030) look back on the past, so the chain can be heard as the start of that story. Restored prose: （わたしは）<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて、…

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="021" %}

`(lines 021–024) (わたしは) [小さな 夢 (を) 積み上げ て]、[願い事 は 曖昧 で]、[追いかける の に 必死 で]、[息 (を) 切らせ 立ち止まっ て] → (no main verb)`
{: .tree-src}

<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな modifies <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>, and the noun phrase is the object of <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて. The te-form adds the first scene of the stanza. In line 022 the subject switches to <ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い<ruby>事<rt>ごと</rt></ruby> “the wishes”, and in lines 023–024 it returns to the speaker. A te-chain can switch subjects like this. Nothing in the stanza gives the chain a tense or a main verb.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="021" %}

4.4.2 is the te-form that opens the chain (<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げた → <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて), and <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる is a compound of the kind at the end of 4.1.2. ## Notes

### The dropped を

In prose the object particle would be said: <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて. Lyrics often drop を for rhythm, as on line 003 (<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち, object of line 004’s verbs) and on line 024 (<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ = <ruby>息<rt>いき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ). The meaning does not change.

### <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる, a second time

On line 008 (<ruby>努<rt>ど</rt>力<rt>りょく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる<ruby>暇<rt>ひま</rt></ruby>もなく “with no time even to build up my efforts”), nothing could be built. Here the speaker does build something, but out of small dreams. On line 031 the verb becomes <ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>重<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>ねる (<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>重<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>ねてきた<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>だけが), and what has been built up turns out to be the one thing that gave them strength. Piled-up dreams can also topple; the next lines show how unsteady the pile is (wishes vague, a desperate chase, a stop).

### <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな, again

Line 019 has <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt>歩<rt>ぽ</rt></ruby> “a small step”, and this line, opening the second half of the song, has <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby> “small dreams”. The song links its halves through the word: the first ends by accepting small progress, and the second starts with small dreams.

### <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>

Like English “dream”, <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby> covers both dreams in sleep (<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る “have a dream”) and aspirations (<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>叶<rt>かな</rt></ruby>える “make a dream come true”, <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける “chase a dream”). The second sense is meant here, and line 023’s <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける “chase” recalls the stock phrase <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける.

### An even stanza

All four lines of this stanza have eleven morae, provided line 022’s <ruby>暖<rt>あい</rt>味<rt>まい</rt></ruby> is read as あいまい (<ruby>曖<rt>あい</rt>昧<rt>まい</rt></ruby>): ちいさなゆめつみあげて, ねがいごとはあいまいで, おいかけるのにひっしで, いききらせたちどまって. The lines are short and even, and this is the only stanza in the song in which no line has a space. The even count is one reason to read line 022 as <ruby>曖<rt>あい</rt>昧<rt>まい</rt></ruby> (see line 022).

### Link to line 020

Line 020 (それが<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>の わたしなんだ) ends the long stanza of lines 013–020, and the first half of the song, on an assertion. Nothing carries over in grammar. After the break, the story starts again: from small dreams, through a chase, to a stop out of breath (line 024).
