# Line 009 · 追い付けない ままでいる

## Context

- Line 008 (previous): 努力を積み上げる暇もなく
- **Line 009 (explained here): 追い付けない ままでいる**
- Line 010 (next): 伸ばした手を摺り抜けてしまう

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 008: predicate 〜ないままでいる "remain unable to ~"; the sentence ends here.

The line is grammatical and ends a sentence. 追い付けない is the negative of 追い付ける, the potential of 追い付く "catch up". It modifies the formal noun まま "the (unchanged) state", and ままでいる (まま + the copula's continuative で + the verb いる "be, stay") turns it into "remain in the state of being unable to catch up". いる is in its terminal form, so the sentence ends with the line. Its adverbial is line 008 (努力を積み上げる暇もなく "with no time even to build up effort"). The subject, the speaker (the わたし of line 020), is unstated, and so is what they cannot catch up with, which 追い付く would mark with に. The space between 追い付けない and ままでいる is a pause; prose writes them together. Line 010 starts a new sentence. Restored prose, with line 008: 努力を積み上げる暇もなく、（わたしは）（〜に）追い付けないままでいる。

## Reading

- Kana: おいつけない / まま で いる
- Romaji: oitsukenai / mama de iru

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |

| 1 | 追い付け | おいつけ (oitsuke) | potential verb 追い付ける (from the godan compound 追い付く = 追う "chase" + 付く "reach, attach"), irrealis form (未然形) before ない | can catch up (with); 〜に追い付く names whom or what one catches up with, unstated here |

| 4 | で | で (de) | copula (断定の助動詞) だ, continuative form (連用形) | links まま to いる: 〜ままだ "is still ~" → 〜ままでいる "stays ~" |
| 5 | いる | いる (iru) | ichidan verb いる (上一段), terminal form (終止形) | to be, to stay (of people and animals); here "remain"; ends the sentence |

## How it fits together

`(line 008: 努力を積み上げる暇もなく →) (わたしは) [[(〜に) 追い付け ない] まま] で いる。`

追い付けない is a relative clause on まま, and まま + で + いる is the predicate: the speaker stays in that state. The non-past いる describes the present, a situation that has gone on and is still going on. Line 008's 暇もなく gives the circumstances: with no time to build anything up, the gap does not close.

## Translation

- Literal: "(I) stay in the state of not being able to catch up"
- Natural: "I still can't catch up." (with line 008: "With no time even to build up my efforts, I still can't catch up.")

## Notes

- **〜ないままでいる.** まま marks a state that stays as it was: そのまま "as it is", 靴のまま "with one's shoes on", 窓を開けたまま寝る "sleep with the window open". After a negative it means "still not, without ever ~ing": 分からないまま "without ever understanding". 〜ままだ states the fact ("is still unable"), and 〜ままでいる makes it the subject's ongoing condition, "goes on being unable", with a hint of being stuck. Compare 〜ないでいる "keep not ~ing", often by choice (何も言わないでいる "keep saying nothing"), and the more written 追い付けずにいる, close to the lyric's meaning.
- **Ability, not will.** The negative belongs to the potential 追い付ける, so the failure is one of ability: the speaker would like to catch up and can't. The song keeps returning to this kind of negative potential: 貰えない (003), 進めずに "unable to move forward" (015) and 立ち上がれなくて "unable to stand up" (029).
- **Catch up with what?** 追い付く needs something ahead of the speaker: other people (クラスメイトに追い付く "catch up with one's classmates"), a goal, or someone's level. The lyric never says. Line 007's growing list of things the speaker can't do suggests a comparison with others or with what is expected of them. Line 010 then gives the image of something slipping through their outstretched hand. The chase comes back on line 023 (追いかけるのに必死で "desperate just to keep up the chase"), with the same verb 追う "chase" as its first element.
- **で in ままでいる.** This で is not the particle で "at, by". It is the continuative form of the copula だ, the same で as in 元気でいる "stay well" or 一人でいる "be on one's own". Turn the predicate into a plain statement and it becomes だ again: 追い付けないままだ.
- **Spelling.** 追い付く is also written 追いつく, with the second element in kana, and that spelling is very common in everyday writing. まま also has the kanji 儘, which is rare today. Both of the song's choices are within normal usage.
- **Link to line 010.** いる closes the sentence. Line 010 (伸ばした手を摺り抜けてしまう) is a new sentence with an unstated subject: something slips through the hand the speaker reaches out. It reads naturally as whatever the speaker is unable to catch up with here.
