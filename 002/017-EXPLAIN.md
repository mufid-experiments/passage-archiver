# Line 017 · 掴み取った何かは きっとそこに

## Context

- Line 016 (previous): やがて景色は流れてゆく
- **Line 017 (explained here): 掴み取った何かは きっとそこに**
- Line 018 (next): 最初からあった物なんだろう

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: topic (掴み取った何かは) plus adverbs; the predicate (…あった物なんだろう) comes on line 018.

The line is grammatical but has no predicate. 掴み取った "grasped, seized" is a relative clause on 何か "something", and は makes 掴み取った何か the topic of the sentence. Its subject is the speaker (the わたし of line 020), unstated as usual. The rest of the line is two words that belong with line 018. きっと "surely" pairs with だろう at the end of line 018, and そこに "there" goes with あった "was". Line 018 completes the sentence: [掴み取った何か]は きっと [そこに最初からあった物]なんだろう "whatever (I) grasped was surely something that had been there from the very start". So the line stops partway through a relative clause: そこに is its first word, and the noun it modifies, 物, comes on the next line. The space separates the topic from the comment. Line 016 closes its sentence with ゆく, so this line starts a new one. Restored prose: 掴み取った何かは、きっとそこに最初からあった物なんだろう。

## Reading

- Kana: つかみとった なにか は / きっと そこ に
- Romaji: tsukamitotta nanika wa / kitto soko ni

## How it fits together

`[[(わたしが) 掴み取っ た] 何 か] は ／ きっと ／ [[そこ に → line 018 最初 から あっ た] 物] な ん だろう`

The sentence has a topic-and-comment shape, X は Y だ: X is the thing grasped, and Y is "a thing that was there from the start". そこに opens the relative clause that modifies Y's head noun, 物, so the line breaks between a modifier and the rest of its clause. きっと, outside the relative clause, belongs to the main predicate and announces the guess that だろう will complete.

## Translation

- Literal: "the something (I) grasped, surely, there…"
- Natural: "Whatever it was I finally grasped, I'm sure (it was) right there…" (with line 018: "Whatever it was I finally grasped, I'm sure it had been right there from the very start.")

## Notes

- **Link to line 018.** The two halves of the sentence meet across the break. そこに goes with 最初からあった "was there from the start", and きっと with 物なんだろう "is surely a thing…, I suppose". Line 018 has no topic of its own; its subject is this line's 掴み取った何か.
- **きっと…だろう.** きっと states the speaker's own conviction, and だろう marks a guess, so together they mean "surely… I suppose": a confident conjecture. The same adverb, on line 038 (これからも きっと大丈夫), goes with a bare predicate and looks ahead. Here it looks back, at something already grasped.
- **掴み取る.** 掴む is "grasp, take hold of"; 掴み取る adds 取る "take" and so means "take hold and make one's own", often of something won through effort: 夢を掴み取る "seize one's dream", 勝利を掴み取る "seize victory". Its past tense answers line 010 (伸ばした手を摺り抜けてしまう "it slips through my outstretched hand"). There, something escaped the speaker's reach, again and again; here, they have caught something.
- **何か, unnamed.** The song never says what was grasped, and 何か keeps it vague, perhaps because it is hard to name. Lines 019–020 come closest: 取るに足りない 小さな一歩だけど / それが今の わたしなんだ "it's a trifling, small step, but that's who I am now". Whatever it was, line 018 says it was not new.
- **そこ.** そこ, the mid-distance demonstrative (中称), points to a place the speaker and listener both have in mind, here the place where the thing was grasped: "right there". The thought that what one has been striving for was there all along has a famous form in Japan in 幸せの青い鳥 "the blue bird of happiness", from Maeterlinck's play, which the children search for far and wide and find at home.
- **Spelling 掴.** 掴 is not on the 常用漢字 (Jōyō) list, so standard writing has つかむ and つかみ取る in kana. The kanji is a stylistic choice, like 貰 (003) and 摺 (010). Its traditional form is 摑.
