# Line 027 · 慰めて欲しい、なんて

## Context

- Line 026 (previous): 思いやる余裕なんて無くて
- **Line 027 (explained here): 慰めて欲しい、なんて**
- Line 028 (next): 負担ばかり掛けてしまったの

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: quoted wish (〜て欲しい) + なんて "things like…", with the verb of saying omitted; the chain runs on to line 028.

The line is grammatical but incomplete in the way speech often is. 慰めて欲しい "(I) want (you) to comfort (me)" is a complete clause: the te-form of 慰める "comfort" + the subsidiary adjective 欲しい, in 〜てほしい "want someone to ~". なんて after it turns the clause into a quote, "something like 'comfort me'", and passes judgement on it, here with self-reproach. The verb that would carry the quote is left out: most naturally 言って "saying", possibly 思って "thinking". The person asked for comfort, who would be marked with に (あなたに), is unstated, and so is the speaker (the わたし of line 020). The comma is the song's only punctuation mark; everywhere else spaces mark the pauses. Here it sets the quote apart from なんて, like a beat in which the speaker hears their own words. Prose would write 「慰めて欲しい」なんて without the comma. The chain begun on line 025 continues to its main predicate on line 028. Restored prose: （わたしは）「（あなたに）慰めて欲しい」なんて（言って）、…

## Reading

- Kana: なぐさめて ほしい、 なんて
- Romaji: nagusamete hoshii, nante

## How it fits together

`(lines 025–026 →) (わたしは) [「(あなたに) 慰め て 欲しい」] 、なんて (言って) → line 028 (相手に) [負担 ばかり 掛け て しまっ た] の`

The quote is a full sentence in itself, and なんて attaches to it as a whole. The missing 言って would be one more link in the te-chain, between 無くて (line 026) and the main predicate on line 028. Read with 言って, the line is what the speaker did: asked for comfort. Line 028 says what came of it.

## Translation

- Literal: "'(I) want (you) to comfort (me),' something like that, (saying)…"
- Natural: "saying things like 'I need you to comfort me'…"

## Notes

- **なんて after a quote.** After a clause, なんて works like a quotation mark with an attitude: "something like ~". It is close to など + と, "and the like" + quote. With 言う or 思う it often signals criticism, embarrassment or disbelief: 「好き」なんて言えない "I can't say something like 'I love you'", 彼が来るなんて思わなかった "I never thought he'd come". Here the speaker is quoting their own words and judging them: of all things, they asked to be comforted, when they had no room to comfort anyone (line 026).
- **What is left out.** Japanese often drops the verb after a なんて-quote when the context makes it clear: 「慰めて欲しい」なんて… "(saying) 'comfort me', of all things…". 言って fits best, because line 028 speaks of burdening someone, which suggests the words were said aloud. 思って "thinking" is possible, but a wish that is only thought burdens no one.
- **〜て欲しい.** "Want someone (else) to do": 慰めて欲しい "I want you to comfort me". The person asked takes に: 君に慰めて欲しい. To want something for oneself, Japanese uses 〜たい instead (慰められたい "I want to be comforted"). Style guides write the subsidiary use in kana, 〜てほしい, and keep the kanji 欲しい for wanting a thing (時間が欲しい "I want more time"). The kanji here is a stylistic choice, like 貰えない on line 003.
- **The comma.** The rest of the song separates phrases with spaces. This one comma divides the wish from the judgement on it: the speaker says the words, then hears how they sound. In prose, a quote before なんて takes 「」 or nothing: 「慰めて欲しい」なんて, 慰めて欲しいなんて.
- **Wanting from others.** The song's first half begins with the speaker wanting to be understood, 分かって貰えない気持ち "feelings no one understands" (line 003). Here, in the second half, they want to be comforted. Both 〜てもらう and 〜てほしい look to someone else to act. In the final stanza the direction reverses: the speaker 受け止めて 言葉にしてきた "faced it and put it into words" themselves (line 036), and their own accumulated effort 力をくれた "gave them strength" (line 032).
- **Link to line 028.** The quote leads into the confession that ends the sentence: 負担ばかり掛けてしまったの "all I did was put a burden on (you)". 慰める and 負担を掛ける form a pair: asking to be comforted is how the speaker became a burden.
