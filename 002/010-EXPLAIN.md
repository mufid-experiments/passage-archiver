# Line 010 · 伸ばした手を摺り抜けてしまう

## Context

- Line 009 (previous): 追い付けない ままでいる
- **Line 010 (explained here): 伸ばした手を摺り抜けてしまう**
- Line 011 (next): 揺れる 焦る 燻ぶった熱を 抱えてる

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — complete sentence: subject unstated (whatever slips away); を marks the space passed through; 〜てしまう for an unwanted result.

The line is grammatical and complete. 伸ばした手 is "the hand (I) stretched out": 伸ばした is a one-verb relative clause on 手, and its subject is the speaker. を here does not mark a direct object. With a verb of motion it marks the space something passes through, as in 人ごみをすり抜ける "slip through a crowd", so 手を摺り抜ける is "slip through the hand". 摺り抜けて + しまう adds the sense that the result is complete and unwanted: "(it) gets away, to my regret". The subject is not the speaker but whatever they were reaching for, and the lyric leaves it unnamed, as Japanese can. しまう is terminal and non-past, so the sentence ends here and describes what keeps happening. Line 009 closes its own sentence with いる, so this line starts a new one. The spelling 摺り抜ける is a less common variant of すり抜ける / 擦り抜ける (see Notes). Nothing needs correcting. Restored prose: （何かが）（わたしの）伸ばした手をすり抜けてしまう。

## Reading

- Kana: のばした て を すりぬけて しまう
- Romaji: nobashita te o surinukete shimau

## How it fits together

`(何かが) [[(わたしが) 伸ばし た] 手] を [摺り抜け て しまう]。`

伸ばした is a relative clause on 手, and 手を is the path of 摺り抜ける. The subject of the main verb, what slips through, is never stated. The た of 伸ばした marks a completed action whose result is still there, the hand already stretched out, rather than past time (like 開いた窓 "an open window"). The main verb is non-past: this is what happens whenever the speaker reaches out.

## Translation

- Literal: "(it) ends up slipping through the hand (I) stretched out"
- Natural: "It slips right through my outstretched hand."

## Notes

- **What slips away?** The subject is unstated, and English has to supply "it". The line follows line 009 (追い付けない ままでいる "I still can't catch up"), so the natural reading is whatever the speaker is chasing: a goal, a dream, other people's level, perhaps a person. The grammar would also allow a sadder picture, someone else's hand reaching out to the speaker and the speaker slipping through it, but nothing in the stanza points that way. The image returns twice. On line 012 something still doesn't reach (届かなくて), and on line 017 the speaker has finally grasped something (掴み取った何か).
- **を for the space passed through.** With verbs of motion such as 歩く, 通る, 渡る and 抜ける, を marks the space moved through: 橋を渡る "cross a bridge", トンネルを抜ける "come out of a tunnel". 手をすり抜ける makes the hand that space, the fingers it slips between. 手からすり抜ける would make the hand the starting point instead, "slip out of my hand"; both occur.
- **〜てしまう.** This subsidiary verb marks an action as complete, and very often as regrettable: 忘れてしまう "(I) forget, (sadly)". Casual speech contracts it to 〜ちゃう (すり抜けちゃう), but the lyric keeps the full form, as it does on line 028 (掛けてしまったの). There it is past, for a single thing the speaker regrets; here it is non-past, for something that keeps happening.
- **Spelling 摺り抜ける.** The usual spellings are すり抜ける and 擦り抜ける. 摺 is not on the 常用漢字 (Jōyō) list. It is an older kanji for する "rub", now mostly used for "print" (摺り物 "printed matter"). Choosing it is a stylistic choice, like 貰 on line 003 and 燻 on line 011. The first element is the same する "rub, brush" as in すれ違う on line 002: in both verbs something passes close by without being held.
- **伸ばす.** 手を伸ばす "reach out one's hand" is literal here, but it also has a figurative sense, "reach for, try one's hand at" (新しい分野に手を伸ばす "branch out into a new field"). Both fit a speaker straining toward something just out of reach. The intransitive partner is 伸びる "grow, stretch" (背が伸びる "grow taller").
- **Link to line 011.** しまう ends the sentence and the stanza of lines 007–010. After the stanza break, the short stanza of lines 011–012 turns inward: 揺れる 焦る 燻ぶった熱を 抱えてる, the agitation and smouldering heat that the failure leaves behind.
