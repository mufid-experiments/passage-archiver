# Line 018 · 最初からあった物なんだろう

## Context

- Line 017 (previous): 掴み取った何かは きっとそこに
- **Line 018 (explained here): 最初からあった物なんだろう**
- Line 019 (next): 取るに足りない 小さな一歩だけど

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 017: noun predicate with explanatory ん and conjectural だろう (〜なんだろう).

The line is grammatical and ends the sentence that line 017 began. Its topic is on line 017, 掴み取った何かは "the something (I) grasped", and this line supplies the predicate, a noun with a relative clause in front of it. 最初からあった "that was there from the start" modifies 物 "thing", and line 017's そこに "there" belongs inside that clause too: そこに最初からあった物 "a thing that had been there from the very start". The copula follows the noun as な + ん + だろう. な is the form だ takes before の, ん is the contracted explanatory の, and だろう is the copula's conjectural form, "probably, I suppose", answering line 017's きっと. The meaning is "it must have been, I suppose, something that was there all along". Nothing is left out but the speaker, who did the grasping. 物 in kanji is a stylistic choice (see Notes). Line 019 starts a new sentence. Restored prose: 掴み取った何かは、きっとそこに最初からあった物なんだろう。

## Reading

- Kana: さいしょ から あった もの なん だろう
- Romaji: saisho kara atta mono nan darō

## How it fits together

`[掴み取った何か] は (line 017) きっと [[そこ に (line 017) 最初 から あっ た] 物] な ん だろう。`

The sentence identifies its topic with its predicate noun: "the thing grasped is a thing that was there from the start". Inside the relative clause, そこに gives the place and 最初から the time from which it was there. The past あった is past from the speaker's present, while the main predicate is a present guess, だろう. The ん makes the guess an explanation: "so that must be what it was".

## Translation

- Literal: "(it) is probably a thing that existed there from the start"
- Natural: "(Whatever I finally grasped,) I'm sure it had been there all along."

## Notes

- **なんだろう: guessing at an explanation.** 物だろう alone would be a plain guess, "it's probably a thing that…". Adding の (物なのだろう, contracted to 物なんだろう) makes it an interpretation of something the speaker has experienced: "so that must be what it was, I suppose". The same string can also be a question, as in 何なんだろう "I wonder what it is", but here きっと on line 017 makes it a confident guess. The song's next sentence ends on なんだ, without the guess (020, わたしなんだ "that's who I am"): from "I suppose" to "it is".
- **The paradox.** 掴み取った (line 017) says the thing was won through effort; 最初からあった says it was there from the start. The two are compatible: the struggle of lines 013–015 did not create what the speaker found, but it was needed to find it. 最初から "from the start, all along" is the key phrase (最初から分かっていた "I knew it all along").
- **ある for things.** ある is used for inanimate things and いる for people and animals, which is why line 009 has ままでいる for the speaker. With ある, the grasped 何か is treated as a thing, not a being, which fits its being unnamed and abstract.
- **物 in kanji.** Style guides write もの in kana when it is abstract or a formal noun (正しいものと認める "recognise as correct") and keep 物 for tangible things (食べ物 "food"). What the speaker grasped is not tangible, so standard writing would have もの. The kanji is a stylistic choice, like 事 on lines 007, 025 and 031.
- **A stanza of two-line sentences.** In the second half of the long stanza, lines 017–018 and 019–020 are each a two-line sentence, and both end on an ん-predicate. Line 016 stands alone between the chain of lines 013–015 and these two. After the run-on te-forms of lines 013–015, the stanza closes on short, settled statements.
- **Link to line 019.** だろう closes the sentence. Line 019 (取るに足りない 小さな一歩だけど) opens a new one, and its unstated subject can be heard as the thing grasped: "(it is) a trifling, small step, but…".
