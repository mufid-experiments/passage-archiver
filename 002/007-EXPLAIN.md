# Line 007 · 出来ない事ばかりが 増えてく

## Context

- Line 006 (previous): 祝福の光の中 また桜は散ってゆく
- **Line 007 (explained here): 出来ない事ばかりが 増えてく**
- Line 008 (next): 努力を積み上げる暇もなく

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — complete sentence (colloquial てく = ていく); it begins and ends on this line.

The line is grammatical and complete. Its subject is 出来ない事ばかり, marked by が: 出来ない "cannot do" is a relative clause on the formal noun 事 "things", and ばかり "nothing but" limits the subject to those things. The predicate, 増えてく, is the casual contraction of 増えていく (te-form 増えて + subsidiary verb いく with its い dropped), "go on increasing". く is a terminal form, so the sentence ends with the line. The one who cannot do these things is the speaker (the わたし of line 020), unstated as usual; inside the relative clause they would be marked with に (わたしに出来ない事). Line 006 ends the refrain on a terminal form of its own (散ってゆく), and after the stanza break this line opens the stanza of lines 007–010 with a new sentence. Line 008 starts the next sentence, which ends on line 009. The space is a pause between subject and predicate, where prose would have nothing. Two features stand out, and neither is an error: the contraction てく, ordinary in casual speech and lyrics (the refrain just before has the full, more literary ゆく), and the kanji spelling 出来ない, where newspapers write できない. Restored prose: （わたしに）出来ない事ばかりが増えていく。

## Reading

- Kana: できない こと ばかり が / ふえてく

## How it fits together

`[[[(わたしに) 出来 ない] 事] ばかり] が ／ [増え て く]。`

出来ない is a relative clause (連体修飾節) on 事, "things (I) can't do", and its unstated subject, the speaker, belongs inside it, marked with に. ばかり caps the noun phrase, and が makes it the subject of 増えてく. 増えて + く is one predicate: the te-form carries the lexical meaning, and the contracted いく adds "go on ~ing", a change moving forward from now. It is non-past, so the increase is under way and set to continue. く is a terminal form, so nothing on this line waits for line 008.

## Translation

- Literal: "nothing but things (I) can't do go on increasing"
- Natural: "It's only the things I can't do that keep on growing."

## Notes

- **Link to line 006.** Line 006 (祝福の光の中 また桜は散ってゆく) ends the refrain, and its sentence, on the terminal form 散ってゆく. After the stanza break the song turns from the spring scene to the speaker's own state, and this line starts a new sentence. The two lines end on the same subsidiary verb in two registers, literary ゆく for the blossoms and casual く for the speaker's troubles. Both mean "go on ~ing": the season moves on, and so does the list of what the speaker can't do. Line 009 (追い付けない ままでいる) then says that the speaker cannot catch up.
- **Link to line 008.** 増えてく closes this sentence. Line 008 (努力を積み上げる暇もなく) opens the next, an adverbial whose predicate comes on line 009 (追い付けない ままでいる). With no punctuation, line 008 can also be heard as an afterthought to this line (see line 008), but the main reading attaches it to line 009. Either way, lines 007–009 describe one situation: more and more that the speaker can't do, no time to work at it, and no catching up.
- **てく = ていく.** 〜ていく adds "go on ~ing", a change that keeps moving forward from now: 増えていく "keep increasing", 寒くなっていく "keep getting colder". Compare 増えている / 増えてる "has increased, is up" (a state now) and 増えてきた "has been increasing (up to now)". Dropping the い (ていく → てく) is casual speech, not an error. The song drops an い again in 抱えてる (= 抱えている, line 011), but keeps the full, more literary ゆく in 散ってゆく (lines 006, 040) and 流れてゆく (line 016). The full ゆく comes in lines about the world around the speaker, the dropped い in lines about the speaker's own state.
- **ばかりが, and だけが on line 031.** ばかり marks too much of something unwanted, as on lines 002 (すれ違ってばかりの) and 028 (負担ばかり): 心配ばかりが増える "all that grows is my worry". What does not grow, the things the speaker can do, is left implied. Here が follows ばかり; on line 028 ばかり stands in place of を. Lines 031–032 have the same shape with a different "only": 積み重ねてきた事だけが 前に / 進む力をくれたんだ "only what I had built up gave me the strength to move forward". There だけ picks out the one thing that counted; here ばかり complains that only the unwanted things increase.
- **出来ない in kanji.** Newspapers and official documents write this verb in kana, できる, as they do the formal noun こと. The kanji spelling, common in lyrics and personal writing, reflects where the verb comes from: 出 "come out" + 来 "come", going back to classical 出で来 (いでく) "come forth". The sense "come into being, be finished" lives on beside "can do": 駅前に店ができる "a shop opens by the station", 準備ができた "everything's ready". In nouns the kanji are normal: 出来事 (できごと) "event", 出来 (でき) "result, quality". Both kanji are Jōyō, and as with 事 (see line 025), the spelling is a choice, not a difference in meaning. 追い付けない on line 009 is a similar case.
