# Line 006 · 祝福の光の中 また桜は散ってゆく

## Context

- Line 005 (previous): 春風は青く香り やがて緑は芽吹いて
- **Line 006 (explained here): 祝福の光の中 また桜は散ってゆく**
- Line 007 (next): 出来ない事ばかりが 増えてく

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 005: adverbial 〜の中 (no particle) + main clause ending on the terminal 散ってゆく.

The line is grammatical and ends the sentence that line 005 began. It has two parts. 祝福の光の中 "amid the light of blessing" is a noun phrase used as an adverbial of setting. Prose could add で (光の中で), but 〜の中、 without a particle is standard written style for "amid, with ~ all around" (see Notes). Then comes the third clause of the refrain: また "again", the third topic 桜は (after 春風は and 緑は on line 005), and 散ってゆく, the te-form of 散る "scatter, fall (of petals)" plus the subsidiary verb ゆく "go on ~ing, ~ away". ゆく is the older, more literary form of いく, and its terminal form closes the sentence. The space separates the setting from the clause, where prose would put a comma. Nothing needs correcting. Restored prose: 春風は青く香り、やがて緑は芽吹いて、祝福の光の中、また桜は散ってゆく。

## Reading

- Kana: しゅくふく の ひかり の なか / また さくら は ちって ゆく
- Romaji: shukufuku no hikari no naka / mata sakura wa chitte yuku

## How it fits together

`(line 005: 春風は青く香り やがて緑は芽吹いて →) [[[祝福 の 光] の] 中] ／ また [桜 は [散っ て ゆく]]。`

The 中-phrase gives the setting, and また modifies 散ってゆく. 桜は is the last of the three topics, and 散ってゆく is the only verb in the sentence with a finite form, so its non-past covers all three clauses: this is what happens, every spring. The setting most naturally belongs to the last clause, the blossoms falling in the light, but it can also be heard as the frame for the whole scene.

## Translation

- Literal: "amid the light of blessing, again the cherry blossoms go on scattering"
- Natural: "and in the light of blessing, the cherry blossoms fall once again." (with line 005: "The spring breeze carries a fresh green scent, soon new green buds appear, and in the light of blessing, the cherry blossoms fall once again.")

## Notes

- **〜の中 without a particle.** For a place where something happens, prose normally says 〜の中で (光の中で遊ぶ "play in the light"), and for a path, 〜の中を (雨の中を歩く "walk in the rain"). But 〜の中、 standing alone is a standard written pattern for the circumstances around an event: 大勢が見守る中、式が始まった "the ceremony began as many looked on", 雨の中、試合は続いた "the match went on in the rain". So the lyric's 祝福の光の中 is not even a poetic omission.
- **散ってゆく, and ゆく against く.** 〜ていく adds "go on ~ing" (a change that continues from now) and, with verbs of motion, "~ away from here". With 散る both fit: the petals keep falling and are carried off. The song uses the full literary ゆく for the world around the speaker (here, on line 016's 流れてゆく and on line 040), and the casual contraction く for the speaker's own state on the very next line, 増えてく (= 増えていく, line 007).
- **また and the cycle.** また says the blossoms fall as they did before: spring has come round again. The refrain itself comes round again too (lines 039–040 repeat lines 005–006), so the word is repeated along with the scene. Placed between the speaker's doubts (lines 001–004) and failures (lines 007–010), it also suggests another year going by while the speaker is stuck.
- **桜 and 散る.** Falling cherry blossoms are the classic Japanese image of beauty that does not last, and of partings. In Japan the season of 桜 is also the season of graduations (March) and entrance ceremonies (April), of endings and new beginnings. 散る is the usual verb for petals and leaves that come apart and fall (花が散る); 落ちる would be plain "drop", and 舞う "dance (in the air)" would stress the floating.
- **祝福の光.** 祝福 combines 祝 "celebrate" and 福 "good fortune"; as a verb, 祝福する is "bless" or "congratulate, celebrate someone's happiness" (二人の門出を祝福する "celebrate the couple's new start"). 祝福の光 is not a fixed phrase. It paints the spring sunshine as if it were blessing someone, perhaps a ceremony or a new start, so the line sets loss (falling blossoms) inside celebration (light).
- **Link to line 007.** 散ってゆく ends the sentence and the refrain. After the stanza break, line 007 (出来ない事ばかりが 増えてく) turns from the spring scene to the speaker's own state and starts a new sentence. Both lines end on "go on ~ing", the literary ゆく for the season and the casual く for the speaker's troubles: the season moves on, and so does the list of what the speaker can't do.
