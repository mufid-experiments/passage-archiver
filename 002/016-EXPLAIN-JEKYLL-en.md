### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="016" %}

The line is a grammatical, complete sentence with nothing omitted. やがて “before long, in time” is an adverb, <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>は “the scenery” is the topic and subject, and <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく is the te-form of <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “flow” plus the subsidiary verb ゆく “go on ~ing, ~ away”. ゆく is the literary form of いく, and its terminal form ends the sentence. Line 015 closes its own sentence on <ruby>募<rt>つの</rt></ruby>る, so this line starts a new one, and line 017 starts another. Two things are open, the meaning rather than the grammar: whether the scenery flows past because the speaker is moving, or because time is passing (see Notes). The register is slightly literary (やがて, ゆく), like the refrain (lines 005–006). Restored prose: やがて<ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="016" %}

やがて modifies the predicate, <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>は is its topic, and <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく is one predicate: the te-form carries the lexical meaning, and ゆく adds continuation and movement away. The non-past describes a process now under way: the scenery is starting to stream by and will keep doing so.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="016" %}

ゆく is an older, more literary いく, and the grammar is that of 4.5.5: 〜ていく for an action moving away (<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っていった “went running toward the station”) or carried forward in time (<ruby>頑<rt>がん</rt>張<rt>ば</rt></ruby>っていく “will try my hardest (toward the future)”). Scenery streaming past fits the first, time going by the second. 3.3.2’s は makes <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby> the topic. 2.5.3 warns that some compounds have special readings that “must be individually memorized”; <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby> is one, with <ruby>景<rt>け</rt></ruby> read け instead of the usual けい.

## Notes

### What does <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる mean?

The phrase is used of scenery seen while moving: <ruby>車<rt>しゃ</rt>窓<rt>そう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “the scenery streams past the train window”, <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>後<rt>うし</rt></ruby>ろへ<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れていく “the view slips away behind (as one runs)”. On that reading the speaker, stuck on line 015 (<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めずに “unable to move forward”), is in fact moving again, because the view is going by. <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる is also the verb for time passing (<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “the months and days go by”), so the line can also mean that time moves on and the scenes of life change, as the seasons do in the refrain. Both readings carry the speaker past the frustration of lines 013–015.

### やがて, again

The song uses やがて twice, here and on line 005 (やがて<ruby>緑<rt>みどり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>芽<rt>め</rt>吹<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>いて “soon new green buds appear”). Both times it marks change that comes by itself, given time, and both times the subject is something outside the speaker, marked with は: <ruby>緑<rt>みどり</rt></ruby>は, <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>は.

### ゆく and く

The literary ゆく appears where the song describes the world around the speaker: <ruby>散<rt>ち</rt></ruby>ってゆく (lines 006, 040) and <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく here. The casual contractions are used for the speaker’s own state: <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えてく (= <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えていく, line 007) and <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えてる (= <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えている, line 011). This line, with its literary ゆく, sounds closer to the refrain than to the lines just before it.

### <ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby>

<ruby>景<rt>け</rt>色<rt>しき</rt></ruby> (けしき) is the everyday word for “view, scenery”. Its reading is irregular: <ruby>景<rt>けい</rt></ruby> is normally けい. The word is in the appendix of special readings (<ruby>付<rt>ふ</rt>表<rt>ひょう</rt></ruby>) of the <ruby>常<rt>じょう</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>漢<rt>かん</rt>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby> list. <ruby>風<rt>ふう</rt>景<rt>けい</rt></ruby> (ふうけい) is close in meaning but more “landscape, scene”, also used of pictures, and <ruby>景<rt>けい</rt>観<rt>かん</rt></ruby> (けいかん) is formal, as in planning.

### <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる

Besides water, time and scenery, <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる is used of sound and news (<ruby>音<rt>おん</rt>楽<rt>がく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “music is playing”, ニュースが<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “news goes out”), and of plans that fall through (<ruby>計<rt>けい</rt>画<rt>かく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “the plan is called off”). Its transitive partner is <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>す “let flow, wash away”, and the passive <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>される “be swept along” describes someone carried by events.

### The hinge of the stanza

Lines 013–015 are all effort and failure, ending on <ruby>募<rt>つの</rt></ruby>る. This short line is a pause and a turn. Lines 017–018 then reflect (<ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かは きっとそこに / <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>初<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>からあった<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>なんだろう “whatever I grasped was surely there from the start”), and lines 019–020 accept the present (それが<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>の わたしなんだ “that’s who I am now”).

### Link to line 017

ゆく ends the sentence, and line 017 opens the next with a new topic: <ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かは “the something (I) grasped”. The verb of grasping comes after the scenery has flowed: once the speaker has moved on, something is in their hand.
