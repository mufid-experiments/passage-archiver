# Line 019 · 取るに足りない 小さな一歩だけど

## Context

- Line 018 (previous): 最初からあった物なんだろう
- **Line 019 (explained here): 取るに足りない 小さな一歩だけど**
- Line 020 (next): それが今の わたしなんだ

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: noun predicate (〜一歩だ) with concessive けど; the main clause comes on line 020.

The line is grammatical, but it is a subordinate clause, not a sentence. Its core is 一歩だ "(it) is a step", with two modifiers on the noun: the set phrase 取るに足りない "trifling, not worth bothering about" and the adnominal 小さな "small". The copula だ is followed by けど "but, although", so the clause concedes something and waits for its main clause, which is line 020 (それが今の わたしなんだ "that's who I am now"). The subject of 一歩だ is unstated. It is most naturally "it", the thing just grasped (lines 017–018) or the progress the speaker has made, and line 020 picks it up with それ "that". Line 018 closes its sentence with だろう, so this line starts a new one. The space separates the two modifiers; prose writes them together. Nothing needs correcting. Restored prose, with line 020: （それは）取るに足りない小さな一歩だけど、それが今のわたしなんだ。

## How it fits together

`(それは) [[取る に 足り ない] [小さな 一歩]] だ けど → line 020 [それ が] [[今 の] わたし] な ん だ`

取るに足りない and 小さな are stacked modifiers on 一歩; the longer clause comes first, as usual. 一歩だ is a complete predicate, and けど turns it into a concession: what follows on line 020 holds in spite of it. The unstated subject of this clause and the それ of line 020 refer to the same thing.

## Translation

- Literal: "(it is) a small step not worth taking up, but…"
- Natural: "It's only a tiny step, nothing to speak of, but…" (with line 020: "It's only a tiny step, nothing to speak of, but that's who I am right now.")

## Notes

- **取るに足りない.** A set phrase for "trivial, insignificant": 取るに足りない問題 "a trivial matter", 取るに足りない存在 "a nobody". Literally it is "not enough to be taken up": not worth picking up or bothering with. The more traditional form uses 足る, the older godan form of 足りる: 取るに足らない. Both are in common use.
- **小さな and 小さい.** 小さい is the i-adjective and can be a predicate (一歩は小さい "the step is small"). 小さな is an adnominal (連体詞) and can only stand before a noun, which is why grammars do not count it as a na-adjective (there is no 小さだ). Before a noun either is possible: 小さな一歩 or 小さい一歩. 小さな often sounds softer and more emotional, and it is common in lyrics. The song uses it again two lines later, after the stanza break: 小さな夢積み上げて (line 021).
- **小さな一歩だけど.** The phrase recalls the usual Japanese rendering of Neil Armstrong's words on the moon: これは一人の人間にとっては小さな一歩だが、人類にとっては偉大な飛躍である "That's one small step for a man, one giant leap for mankind". The structure is the same, small step + "but". Here, though, what comes after "but" is not a giant leap. It is the speaker's own present self.
- **だけど here and on line 035.** Here けど attaches to the copula after a noun (一歩だ + けど), as part of the clause. On line 035 (不甲斐なさに 隠れて泣いて だけど), だけど stands alone at the end of the line as a conjunction, "but", that belongs to the clause on line 036. They are the same words in two different grammatical roles.
- **The walking image.** 一歩 continues the song's path metaphors: 前に進めずに "unable to move forward" (015), 立ち止まって "coming to a stop" (024), 躓いても "even if I stumble" (033), 前に / 進む力 "the strength to move forward" (031–032). Here one step is all the speaker has managed after the struggle of lines 013–015, and they accept it.
- **Link to line 018.** だろう closes the previous sentence about the thing grasped. This line starts a new sentence, but its unstated subject can be heard as that same thing: what the speaker grasped turns out to be one small step, nothing grand, and it had been within reach all along.
