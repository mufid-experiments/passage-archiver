### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="010" %}

The line is grammatical and complete. <ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby> is “the hand (I) stretched out”: <ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした is a one-verb relative clause on <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>, and its subject is the speaker. を here does not mark a direct object. With a verb of motion it marks the space something passes through, as in <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>ごみをすり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける “slip through a crowd”, so <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける is “slip through the hand”. <ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けて + しまう adds the sense that the result is complete and unwanted: “(it) gets away, to my regret”. The subject is not the speaker but whatever they were reaching for, and the lyric leaves it unnamed, as Japanese can. しまう is terminal and non-past, so the sentence ends here and describes what keeps happening. Line 009 closes its own sentence with いる, so this line starts a new one. The spelling <ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける is a less common variant of すり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける / <ruby>擦<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける (see Notes). Nothing needs correcting. Restored prose: （<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かが）（わたしの）<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>をすり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けてしまう。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="010" %}

<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした is a relative clause on <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>, and <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を is the path of <ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける. The subject of the main verb, what slips through, is never stated. The た of <ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした marks a completed action whose result is still there, the hand already stretched out, rather than past time (like <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いた<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby> “an open window”). The main verb is non-past: this is what happens whenever the speaker reaches out.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="010" %}

5.3.1 is しまう after a te-form, for “an action that is done or happened unintentionally”, with the tense decided by しまう itself; its non-past here makes the loss something that keeps happening. The casual 〜ちゃう of 5.3.2 is what the lyric does not use. 3.8.1 lets a place be the direct object of a motion verb, as in <ruby>街<rt>まち</rt></ruby>をぶらぶら<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く “aimlessly walk through town”; the book counts this を as a direct object, and here the place is the hand. 3.10.3 puts the past-tense verb before <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>, and 3.7.2 gives the past of a godan verb in す: す → した. <ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける is a compound of the kind at the end of 4.1.2. た for a lasting result (<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばした<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby> “an outstretched hand”) has no section.

## Notes

### What slips away?

The subject is unstated, and English has to supply “it”. The line follows line 009 (<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>い<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けない ままでいる “I still can’t catch up”), so the natural reading is whatever the speaker is chasing: a goal, a dream, other people’s level, perhaps a person. The grammar would also allow a sadder picture, someone else’s hand reaching out to the speaker and the speaker slipping through it, but nothing in the stanza points that way. The image returns twice. On line 012 something still doesn’t reach (<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かなくて), and on line 017 the speaker has finally grasped something (<ruby>掴<rt>つか</rt></ruby>み<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か).

### を for the space passed through

With verbs of motion such as <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>る and <ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける, を marks the space moved through: <ruby>橋<rt>はし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>る “cross a bridge”, トンネルを<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける “come out of a tunnel”. <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>をすり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける makes the hand that space, the fingers it slips between. <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>からすり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける would make the hand the starting point instead, “slip out of my hand”; both occur.

### 〜てしまう

This subsidiary verb marks an action as complete, and very often as regrettable: <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてしまう “(I) forget, (sadly)”. Casual speech contracts it to 〜ちゃう (すり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けちゃう), but the lyric keeps the full form, as it does on line 028 (<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの). There it is past, for a single thing the speaker regrets; here it is non-past, for something that keeps happening.

### Spelling <ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける

The usual spellings are すり<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける and <ruby>擦<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ける. <ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby> is not on the <ruby>常<rt>じょう</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>漢<rt>かん</rt>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby> (Jōyō) list. It is an older kanji for する “rub”, now mostly used for “print” (<ruby>摺<rt>す</rt></ruby>り<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby> “printed matter”). Choosing it is a stylistic choice, like <ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby> on line 003 and <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby> on line 011. The first element is the same する “rub, brush” as in すれ<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う on line 002: in both verbs something passes close by without being held.

### <ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばす

<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばす “reach out one’s hand” is literal here, but it also has a figurative sense, “reach for, try one’s hand at” (<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>分<rt>ぶん</rt>野<rt>や</rt></ruby>に<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばす “branch out into a new field”). Both fit a speaker straining toward something just out of reach. The intransitive partner is <ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びる “grow, stretch” (<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びる “grow taller”).
