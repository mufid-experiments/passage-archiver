# Line 002 · すれ違ってばかりの 毎日

## Context

- Line 001 (previous): 些細なことで 揉めて 怒って
- **Line 002 (explained here): すれ違ってばかりの 毎日**
- Line 003 (next): 分かって貰えない気持ち

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: noun-ending phrase (体言止め) that completes the phrase begun on line 001.

The line is a grammatical noun phrase, not a sentence. It ends on the noun 毎日 with no predicate (体言止め), so it presents a situation instead of making a statement about it. すれ違って is the third te-form in the chain that line 001 began (揉めて, 怒って, すれ違って), and its subject is the same unstated speaker (the わたし of line 020). ばかり ("nothing but") follows the te-form, and の links the whole chain to the head noun 毎日. As a full sentence it would be something like （わたしは）…すれ違ってばかりの毎日（を送っている） "(I) spend day after day doing nothing but…", or simply …すれ違ってばかりの毎日（だ）. The space before 毎日 is a pause for rhythm; prose would write すれ違ってばかりの毎日. Line 003 does not continue this phrase. It opens a new sentence, which ends on line 004. Nothing needs correcting. Restored prose, with line 001: 些細なことで揉めて、怒って、すれ違ってばかりの毎日。

## Reading

- Kana: すれちがって ばかり の / まいにち
- Romaji: surechigatte bakari no / mainichi

## How it fits together

`[[(line 001: 些細なことで 揉めて 怒って) すれ違っ て] ばかり] の 毎日`

The te-forms of line 001 run straight on into すれ違って, and ばかり follows the last of them. Grammatically ばかり attaches only to すれ違って, but what it describes is the whole cycle: quarrel, get angry, fail to connect, again and again. The の-phrase then modifies 毎日, the head of the two-line phrase. With no predicate, the phrase stands on its own (体言止め), and the te-forms take no tense: the 毎日 is simply the speaker's present.

## Translation

- Literal: "days of nothing but passing each other by"
- Natural: "day after day of never quite connecting" (with line 001: "Day after day of fighting over little things, getting angry, and never quite connecting")

## Notes

- **Link to line 001.** Line 001 has no predicate of its own: its te-forms 揉めて and 怒って hang open until this line supplies the third link, すれ違って, and the noun the chain describes, 毎日. Together the two lines are a single noun phrase, the song's opening picture of everyday friction.
- **Link to line 003.** 毎日 closes the phrase, and line 003 (分かって貰えない気持ち) starts a new sentence that line 004 completes (言葉にして 伝えられるのかな). The link is one of meaning: days of すれ違い leave behind feelings nobody understands, and doubt about getting them across in words. Line 003 also ends on a bare noun, 気持ち, so on first hearing it sounds like a second 体言止め beside 毎日; line 004 then shows that 気持ち is an object (see line 003).
- **〜てばかり, not 〜たばかり.** After the te-form, ばかり means "nothing but": すれ違ってばかり "always missing each other", 泣いてばかりいる "do nothing but cry". After the past た it means "just now": すれ違ったばかり "(we) have only just passed each other". Only the vowel of the verb ending differs, so it is worth hearing carefully. As a predicate, 〜てばかり takes いる (すれ違ってばかりいる); before a noun it takes の, as here. The song's ばかり returns twice for too much of something unwanted: 出来ない事ばかりが (line 007) and 負担ばかり (line 028).
- **毎日 as a noun.** 毎日 is usually an adverb: 毎日すれ違う "(we) miss each other every day". Here it is a noun with a modifier in front of it, the head of the phrase, so it means "days, daily life": すれ違ってばかりの毎日 "a daily life of nothing but missing each other". The song closes this picture on line 030, where the speaker rejects あんな日々 "days like those".
- **すれ違う, literal and figurative.** Literally, two people pass each other going opposite ways, close but without stopping (駅で友達とすれ違う "pass a friend at the station"). Figuratively, they fail to connect: 気持ちがすれ違う "(our) feelings don't meet", and the noun すれ違い is the everyday word for that kind of disconnect. After 揉めて 怒って, the figurative sense is the natural one. The same root, 擦る (する) "brush, rub", begins すり抜ける on line 010 (伸ばした手を摺り抜けてしまう, "slip through my outstretched hand"): in both verbs something passes close by and is not held.
- **Spelling.** The kanji spelling 擦れ違う exists, but すれ違う, with the first element in kana, is the usual one. 毎日 is standard. The song's kanji choices elsewhere (些細, 揉めて, 貰えない) are discussed at lines 001 and 003.
