# Line 011 · 揺れる 焦る 燻ぶった熱を 抱えてる

## Context

- Line 010 (previous): 伸ばした手を摺り抜けてしまう
- **Line 011 (explained here): 揺れる 焦る 燻ぶった熱を 抱えてる**
- Line 012 (next): いつも 今も 届かなくて

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: two bare verbs (揺れる 焦る) set side by side, then a complete clause ending in casual 抱えてる (= 抱えている).

The line is grammatical, though built like a poem rather than prose. It opens with two verbs in their plain non-past form, 揺れる "waver" and 焦る "fret", each standing alone, with no connective and no object. Their subject is the unstated speaker (the わたし of line 020). Then comes a full clause: 燻ぶった熱を抱えてる "(I) carry a smouldering heat", in which 燻ぶった "smouldering" modifies 熱 "heat", を marks it as the object, and 抱えてる is the casual contraction of 抱えている, with the い dropped, as in 増えてく (line 007). Prose would link the first two verbs (揺れて、焦って or 揺れたり焦ったりして); setting them side by side is a staccato, list-like device. Because a verb's terminal and attributive forms are identical, the two could also be read as modifiers of 熱 (see Notes), but the spaces set them apart. 抱えてる is terminal, so the line ends a sentence. Line 010 closes the previous stanza, and this line opens the short stanza of lines 011–012. Restored prose: （わたしは）揺れて、焦って、くすぶった熱を抱えている。

## Reading

- Kana: ゆれる / あせる / くすぶった ねつ を / かかえてる
- Romaji: yureru / aseru / kusubutta netsu o / kakaeteru

## How it fits together

`(わたしは) [揺れる] ／ [焦る] ／ [[[燻ぶっ た] 熱] を [抱え て る]]。`

Three predicates share one unstated subject. The first two are single verbs with nothing attached; the third has an object. All three are non-past and describe the speaker now. 〜ている on 抱える gives a state that continues: the speaker is carrying the heat and has been for a while.

## Translation

- Literal: "(I) waver, (I) fret, (I) am holding a smouldered heat"
- Natural: "Wavering, restless, I carry this smouldering heat inside me."

## Notes

- **Bare verbs side by side.** Listing verbs in their terminal form without a connective is a poetic device, like a list of symptoms read out one by one. The song does something similar in the other two-line stanza, on line 029: 震え 竦み 目を逸らし 立ち上がれなくて. There the verbs are in the continuative form (連用形) and link on to a final te-form. Here each verb is complete in itself. The two stanzas mirror each other: this one is agitation and heat that cannot reach its goal (line 012), and line 029 is fear and paralysis, followed by a rejection (line 030, あんな日々は もう嫌だよ).
- **Another parse.** A verb's terminal and attributive forms are the same, so 揺れる and 焦る could also modify 熱 along with 燻ぶった: "a wavering, fretting, smouldering heat". 焦る気持ち "impatient feelings" is a common phrase, so 焦る熱 is not impossible. But the spaces between the words suggest separate statements; compare line 037, where 信じる素直な is written without a space and is attributive. On either reading the line describes the same agitated state.
- **燻ぶる.** The standard spellings are 燻る and kana くすぶる. 燻 is not on the 常用漢字 (Jōyō) list, and on its own 燻る can also be read いぶる "smoke, smoulder". Writing the extra ぶ fixes the reading as くすぶる. Literally the word is fire or wood smouldering without flame. Figuratively it is a person stuck where they cannot shine (地方でくすぶる "languish in the provinces") or a feeling or dispute that lingers unresolved (不満がくすぶる "discontent smoulders"). 燻ぶった熱 combines these senses: drive and frustration that never break into flame.
- **抱えてる = 抱えている.** The dropped い is ordinary casual speech, as in 増えてく (= 増えていく, line 007). 抱える is literally "hold in one's arms" (荷物を抱える "carry a parcel"). Figuratively it means carrying something heavy or troublesome: 悩みを抱える "have worries", 問題を抱える "have problems". 熱を抱える therefore suggests heat that is a burden as well as a passion, held in rather than let out.
- **熱.** 熱 is physical heat, fever (熱がある "have a temperature") and ardour (熱い思い "burning feelings", 情熱 "passion"). In a song about wanting to move forward, the main sense is passion, but feverishness also suits the restless 揺れる 焦る before it.
- **Link to lines 010 and 012.** Line 010 ends the previous stanza with something slipping through the speaker's hand. This stanza turns inward to what that failure leaves behind. Line 012 (いつも 今も 届かなくて "always, and even now, it doesn't reach") ends on an open te-form, and is most easily heard as the reason for this line, placed after it: the heat smoulders because it never reaches its goal.
