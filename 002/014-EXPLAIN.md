# Line 014 · 倒れ起きて 繰り返して

## Context

- Line 013 (previous): 思い切って挑んで 壁に当たって
- **Line 014 (explained here): 倒れ起きて 繰り返して**
- Line 015 (next): 前に進めずに もどかしさ募る

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: 倒れ起きて joins two verbs into a coined pair ("fall and get up"); a fragment that continues the te-form chain from line 013 to line 015.

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. It continues the chain begun on line 013 (挑んで, 当たって) with two more links, both with the speaker (the わたし of line 020) as subject. The first, 倒れ起きて, joins the continuative form of 倒れる "fall over" directly to the te-form of 起きる "get up". 倒れ起きる is not a dictionary compound. It is formed on the ordinary pattern of verb + verb compounds (泣き叫ぶ "cry and scream", 飛び跳ねる "leap about") and reads as "fall down and get back up". Prose would say 倒れては起きて or 倒れて、起き上がって. The second link, 繰り返して "repeating", has no stated object; what is repeated is the whole cycle just described. Both links stay open, and the chain ends on line 015 with もどかしさ募る. The space separates the two links, where prose would put a comma. Restored prose, lines 013–015: （わたしは）思い切って挑んで、壁に当たって、倒れては起きて、（それを）繰り返して、前に進めずに、もどかしさ（が）募る。

## Reading

- Kana: たおれ おきて / くりかえして
- Romaji: taore okite / kurikaeshite

## How it fits together

`(line 013: 思い切って挑んで 壁に当たって →) (わたしは) [倒れ 起き て] ／ [(それを) 繰り返し て] → line 015 [前 に 進め ず に] [もどかしさ (が) 募る]`

倒れ and 起きて make one link of two actions, falling and getting up. 繰り返して then takes everything before it, trying, hitting a wall, falling, getting up, as its unstated object and turns it into a loop. Like the links on line 013, these have no tense of their own; the chain is completed by the non-past 募る on line 015.

## Translation

- Literal: "falling, getting up, repeating (it), and…"
- Natural: "I fall and get back up, again and again…"

## Notes

- **倒れ起きて.** Dictionaries don't list 倒れ起きる, so the lyric is coining a pair on a familiar pattern. Japanese makes many verb + verb compounds whose parts happen in sequence or together: 泣き叫ぶ, 飛び跳ねる, 立ち止まる (line 024). Two things make the coinage easy to understand: the idiom 七転び八起き "fall down seven times, get up eight", the classic saying about perseverance, and the noun 起き伏し "rising and lying down, daily life". The song keeps the 起き of the idiom. The prose version uses 〜ては for repetition: 倒れては起き、倒れては起き "falling and getting up, falling and getting up".
- **What is repeated?** 繰り返す needs an object (失敗を繰り返す "keep making the same mistake", 同じことを繰り返す "do the same thing over and over"). Here the object is the cycle itself, and Japanese can leave it out because the context makes it obvious. The lines repeat too: the chain of lines 013–014 goes te-form after te-form, each step leading only to the next.
- **倒れる and 起きる.** 倒れる is "fall over" from standing (木が倒れる "a tree falls"), also "collapse" from illness (過労で倒れる "collapse from overwork") and "go under" (会社が倒れる). 転ぶ is "trip and fall". 起きる is "get up" or "wake up", and 起き上がる specifically "get up (from a fall or from lying down)". Here the speaker gets back up every time; on line 029 they cannot (立ち上がれなくて "unable to stand up"). That is the low point of the song's second half.
- **Even rhythm.** 倒れ起きて (たおれおきて) and 繰り返して (くりかえして) have six morae each. The line splits into two equal halves, like two strokes of a repeated movement.
- **繰り返す.** 繰る is "reel in, wind (thread)", so 繰り返す is literally "reel back again". The noun 繰り返し "repetition" is also the word for a refrain in music and poetry, and this song has one: lines 005–006 come back as lines 039–040.
- **Link to lines 013 and 015.** The chain started on line 013 (思い切って挑んで 壁に当たって) and ends on line 015, where 前に進めずに "unable to move forward" is its last link and もどかしさ募る "frustration builds" its main clause. All the effort of lines 013–014 ends in that single result.
