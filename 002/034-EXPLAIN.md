# Line 034 · 未来は閉ざされたりしない

## Context

- Line 033 (previous): 躓いても 立ち止まっても 決して
- **Line 034 (explained here): 未来は閉ざされたりしない**
- Line 035 (next): 不甲斐なさに 隠れて泣いて だけど

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 033: topic 未来は + passive with 〜たりしない, whose ない completes 決して.

The line is grammatical and ends the sentence that line 033 began. 未来は "the future" is the topic and the subject. The predicate is 閉ざされる, the passive of 閉ざす "shut, close off", inside the pattern 〜たりしない "won't ~ (or anything like it)". The ない at the end is the negative that line 033's 決して "never" was waiting for, so 決して…閉ざされたりしない means "will never be closed off". The concessive clauses of line 033 (躓いても 立ち止まっても "even if (I) stumble, even if (I) stop") attach to this predicate: whatever the speaker does, the future stays open. The subject changes between the two lines, from the unstated speaker to 未来. The passive has no agent: no one is named who might close the future. Line 033 put 決して ahead of the topic, at the end of its own line; in neutral prose it would stand next to the verb. Restored prose: 躓いても、立ち止まっても、未来は決して閉ざされたりしない。

## Reading

- Kana: みらい は とざされたり しない
- Romaji: mirai wa tozasaretari shinai

## How it fits together

`(line 033: [躓い て も] [立ち止まっ て も] 決して →) [未来 は] [[閉ざさ れ たり] し ない]。`

The two ても clauses of line 033 state conditions that make no difference to the outcome, and the outcome is this line's main clause. 決して and ない frame the predicate between them, across the line break. The passive 閉ざされる is wrapped in 〜たりする, and する takes the negative. The non-past looks ahead: whatever happens from now on.

## Translation

- Literal: "the future will not do such a thing as be closed"
- Natural: "(Even if I stumble, even if I stop,) the future will never be closed off."

## Notes

- **〜たりしない.** たり usually lists examples (休みの日は本を読んだり映画を見たりする "on days off I read, watch films, things like that"). A single たり before a negative suggests "anything of that kind": 泣いたりしない "I won't cry or anything", 嘘をついたりしない "I'd never lie". The vagueness makes the denial stronger: it rules out the act and anything resembling it. The plain 閉ざされない would be a flat prediction; 閉ざされたりしない sounds like a conviction.
- **閉ざす.** 閉ざす (とざす) is "shut, close up, seal off": 門を閉ざす "close the gate", 雪に閉ざされた村 "a village cut off by snow", 心を閉ざす "close one's heart". It is more literary than 閉める (しめる, "close a door") or 閉じる (とじる, "close one's eyes, a book"), and it suggests a way being blocked for good. 未来が閉ざされる "the future is closed off" is a common phrase for hope cut off, which is exactly what the line denies.
- **A passive with no agent.** 閉ざされる names no one who closes the future: not others, not the speaker, not fate. Only the possibility is raised, so that it can be denied. The topic 未来は also changes the subject: line 033's ても clauses are about the speaker, and the main clause is about the future.
- **未来.** 未 "not yet" + 来 "come": "what has not yet come". The word appears only here in the song. Line 038 takes it up in an everyday word, これから "from now on, the time ahead" (これからも きっと大丈夫 "I'm sure I'll be all right from here on, too").
- **Looking ahead, in the non-past.** The final stanza alternates. Line 032 looks back (進む力をくれたんだ, past + んだ), this line looks ahead, line 036 looks back again (言葉にしてきたんだ), and line 038 ends looking ahead. Both forward-looking sentences begin with a condition: ても…ても here, さえ〜ば on line 037.
- **Link to line 035.** ない ends the sentence. Line 035 (不甲斐なさに 隠れて泣いて だけど) starts the next one and turns back to the past: the speaker cried in secret over their own weakness, but (line 036) faced it.
