# Line 036 · 受け止めて 言葉にしてきたんだ

## Context

- Line 035 (previous): 不甲斐なさに 隠れて泣いて だけど
- **Line 036 (explained here): 受け止めて 言葉にしてきたんだ**
- Line 037 (next): 信じる素直な 気持ちさえあれば

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 035: two te-linked predicates (受け止めて, 言葉にしてきた) with explanatory んだ.

The line is grammatical and ends the sentence that line 035 began; line 035's last word, the conjunction だけど "but", introduces it. It has two predicates with the same unstated subject, the speaker (the わたし of line 020). The first is 受け止めて, the te-form of 受け止める "catch; accept, face squarely". The second is 言葉にしてきた: 言葉にする "put into words" + the subsidiary verb くる, in the past, "have kept ~ing up to now". The sentence ends on ん + だ, the explanatory の contracted plus the copula, a conclusion drawn by looking back. What was faced and put into words is unstated. Most naturally it is the 不甲斐なさ "weakness" of line 035, and with it the feelings the song has been about since line 003. The space separates the two predicates, where prose would put a comma. Line 037 starts the last sentence of the stanza. Restored prose, with line 035: （わたしは）不甲斐なさに隠れて泣いて、だけど（それを）受け止めて、言葉にしてきたんだ。

## Reading

- Kana: うけとめて / ことば に して きた ん だ
- Romaji: uketomete / kotoba ni shite kita n da

## How it fits together

`(line 035: 不甲斐なさに 隠れて泣いて) だけど ／ (わたしは) [(それを) 受け止め て] ／ [(それを) 言葉 に し て き た] ん だ。`

Both predicates have the same unstated object and come in order: first the speaker accepts the thing, then puts it into words. 〜てきた makes the second a process stretching up to the present, not a single act. だけど on line 035 sets these two against the crying in secret: the tears came first, and then this.

## Translation

- Literal: "(but I) accepted (it) and have been putting (it) into words"
- Natural: "(but) I faced it, and I've been putting it into words." (with line 035: "I cried in secret over how weak I was, but I faced it, and I've been putting it into words.")

## Notes

- **The answer to line 004.** The first stanza ends on a doubt: 言葉にして 伝えられるのかな "I wonder if I can put (my feelings) into words and get them across" (line 004). This line answers it with the same words, 言葉にして, now as something the speaker has done: 言葉にしてきたんだ. When the refrain returns on lines 039–040, it comes after this answer, not after the doubt.
- **受け止める.** Literally "catch and stop": ボールを受け止める "catch a ball". Figuratively it means taking something in squarely instead of dodging it: 現実を受け止める "accept reality", 批判を受け止める "take criticism on board", 気持ちを受け止める "take someone's feelings seriously". It is the opposite of line 029's 目を逸らし "looked away". There the speaker could not face what had happened; here they do.
- **〜てきた, twice in the stanza.** This line and line 031 (積み重ねてきた事だけが) both use 〜てきた, a process that ran from the past up to now. Facing things and putting them into words was not a single moment of courage; it went on, like the building up of line 031. Both lines end sentences that look back, with the past + んだ (lines 032 and 036).
- **What was put into words?** The lyric leaves the object out. The nearest candidate is line 035's 不甲斐なさ, the speaker's own weakness. Further back are the feelings of line 003 (分かって貰えない気持ち) and of the confession of lines 025–028. Japanese does not need to choose. Putting something into words here is the opposite of crying in secret: making it something that can be said, and perhaps heard.
- **The stanza's second んだ.** The final stanza has four two-line sentences, ending in くれたんだ (032), しない (034), 言葉にしてきたんだ (036) and 大丈夫 (038). The two んだ sentences look back and draw conclusions from the past; the other two look ahead. Line 037 opens the last of them, a condition for the future: 信じる素直な 気持ちさえあれば "as long as I have an honest heart that believes".
- **Link to line 037.** んだ ends the sentence, and the next one is joined by meaning rather than grammar. Having faced their weakness and put it into words, the speaker names the one thing they need from now on.
