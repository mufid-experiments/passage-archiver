### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="011" %}

The line is grammatical, though built like a poem rather than prose. It opens with two verbs in their plain non-past form, <ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れる “waver” and <ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>る “fret”, each standing alone, with no connective and no object. Their subject is the unstated speaker (the わたし of line 020). Then comes a full clause: <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby>ぶった<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えてる “(I) carry a smouldering heat”, in which <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby>ぶった “smouldering” modifies <ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby> “heat”, を marks it as the object, and <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えてる is the casual contraction of <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えている, with the い dropped, as in <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えてく (line 007). Prose would link the first two verbs (<ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れて、<ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>って or <ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れたり<ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>ったりして); setting them side by side is a staccato, list-like device. Because a verb’s terminal and attributive forms are identical, the two could also be read as modifiers of <ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby> (see Notes), but the spaces set them apart. <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えてる is terminal, so the line ends a sentence. Line 010 closes the previous stanza, and this line opens the short stanza of lines 011–012. Restored prose: （わたしは）<ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れて、<ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>って、くすぶった<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えている。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="011" %}

Three predicates share one unstated subject. The first two are single verbs with nothing attached; the third has an object. All three are non-past and describe the speaker now. 〜ている on <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>える gives a state that continues: the speaker is carrying the heat and has been for a while.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="011" %}

4.5.1 is 〜ている for a continuing state, and it notes that the い “is often omitted in conversational Japanese” (<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてる), which gives <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えてる. 3.10.3 puts <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby>ぶった before <ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>, and 3.7.2 gives the past of a godan verb in る: る → った. た for a resulting state, as here, has no section; the nearest is 4.5.2, where <ruby>結<rt>けっ</rt>婚<rt>こん</rt></ruby>している means “be married”, the state an action has left. Verbs set side by side with no connective, as <ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れる <ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>る are, are a matter of style that the book does not take up; joined, they would be te-forms (4.4.2) or 〜たり (4.4.7).

## Notes

### Bare verbs side by side

Listing verbs in their terminal form without a connective is a poetic device, like a list of symptoms read out one by one. The song does something similar in the other two-line stanza, on line 029: <ruby>震<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>え <ruby>竦<rt>すく</rt></ruby>み <ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>を<ruby>逸<rt>そ</rt></ruby>らし <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がれなくて. There the verbs are in the continuative form (<ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>形<rt>けい</rt></ruby>) and link on to a final te-form. Here each verb is complete in itself. The two stanzas mirror each other: this one is agitation and heat that cannot reach its goal (line 012), and line 029 is fear and paralysis, followed by a rejection (line 030, あんな<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>は もう<ruby>嫌<rt>いや</rt></ruby>だよ).

### Another parse

A verb’s terminal and attributive forms are the same, so <ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れる and <ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>る could also modify <ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby> along with <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby>ぶった: “a wavering, fretting, smouldering heat”. <ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち “impatient feelings” is a common phrase, so <ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby> is not impossible. But the spaces between the words suggest separate statements; compare line 037, where <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じる<ruby>素<rt>す</rt>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>な is written without a space and is attributive. On either reading the line describes the same agitated state.

### <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby>ぶる

The standard spellings are <ruby>燻<rt>くすぶ</rt></ruby>る and kana くすぶる. <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby> is not on the <ruby>常<rt>じょう</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>漢<rt>かん</rt>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby> (Jōyō) list, and on its own <ruby>燻<rt>くすぶ</rt></ruby>る can also be read いぶる “smoke, smoulder”. Writing the extra ぶ fixes the reading as くすぶる. Literally the word is fire or wood smouldering without flame. Figuratively it is a person stuck where they cannot shine (<ruby>地<rt>ち</rt>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>でくすぶる “languish in the provinces”) or a feeling or dispute that lingers unresolved (<ruby>不<rt>ふ</rt>満<rt>まん</rt></ruby>がくすぶる “discontent smoulders”). <ruby>燻<rt>くす</rt></ruby>ぶった<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby> combines these senses: drive and frustration that never break into flame.

### <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えてる = <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えている

The dropped い is ordinary casual speech, as in <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えてく (= <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えていく, line 007). <ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>える is literally “hold in one’s arms” (<ruby>荷<rt>に</rt>物<rt>もつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>える “carry a parcel”). Figuratively it means carrying something heavy or troublesome: <ruby>悩<rt>なや</rt></ruby>みを<ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>える “have worries”, <ruby>問<rt>もん</rt>題<rt>だい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>える “have problems”. <ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>える therefore suggests heat that is a burden as well as a passion, held in rather than let out.

### <ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby> is physical heat, fever (<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>がある “have a temperature”) and ardour (<ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い “burning feelings”, <ruby>情<rt>じょう</rt>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby> “passion”). In a song about wanting to move forward, the main sense is passion, but feverishness also suits the restless <ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れる <ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>る before it.

### Link to lines 010 and 012

Line 010 ends the previous stanza with something slipping through the speaker’s hand. This stanza turns inward to what that failure leaves behind. Line 012 (いつも <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>も <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かなくて “always, and even now, it doesn’t reach”) ends on an open te-form, and is most easily heard as the reason for this line, placed after it: the heat smoulders because it never reaches its goal.
