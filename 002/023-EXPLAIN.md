# Line 023 · 追いかけるのに必死で

## Context

- Line 022 (previous): 願い事は暖味で
- **Line 023 (explained here): 追いかけるのに必死で**
- Line 024 (next): 息切らせ立ち止まって

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: na-adjective 必死 in its continuative form (必死で), with 〜のに "in ~ing"; the third link of the chain of lines 021–024.

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. 追いかける "chase" is nominalised by の ("chasing"), and に marks what the speaker is desperate in: 追いかけるのに必死 "desperate in the chase, frantic just to keep chasing". 必死で is the continuative form (連用形) of the na-adjective 必死だ "desperate, frantic", so it links on to the next line: "(I) was desperate, and…". The subject is the speaker again (the わたし of line 020), after line 022's topic 願い事は. What is chased is unstated; the 小さな夢 of line 021 is the natural candidate. Two things could be misread here. のに is not the conjunction "although", and で is not the particle "at, by" (see Notes). The chain continues to line 024, where it ends, still open, on 立ち止まって. Line 022, written 暖味で, is read as 曖昧で "vague" (see line 022). Restored prose: （わたしは）（夢を）追いかけるのに必死で、…

## Reading

- Kana: おいかける の に ひっし で
- Romaji: oikakeru no ni hisshi de

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 追いかける | おいかける (oikakeru) | ichidan compound verb (複合動詞) 追いかける (追い掛ける: 追う "chase" + かける), attributive form (連体形) | to chase, to run after; what is chased is unstated |
| 2 | の | の (no) | nominalizer (準体助詞) | turns 追いかける into a noun: "chasing" |
| 3 | に | に (ni) | case particle (格助詞), scope | "in, at, for": 〜のに必死 "desperate in ~ing". Not the concessive のに "although" |
| 4 | 必死 | ひっし (hisshi) | na-adjective (形容動詞) 必死だ, stem (語幹) | desperate, frantic; with all one's might (literally "certain death") |
| 5 | で | で (de) | na-adjective ending: continuative form (連用形) of 必死だ (same shape as the copula's で) | "(I) was desperate, and…"; links on to line 024 |

## How it fits together

`(lines 021–022 →) (わたしは) [[[(夢を) 追いかける] の] に 必死 で] → line 024 [息 (を) 切らせ] [立ち止まっ て]`

追いかけるの is a noun phrase, and に links it to 必死, naming the field of the desperation. The whole is a state, like 曖昧で on line 022, and it gives the reason for line 024: the chase was so desperate that it ended with the speaker out of breath, standing still.

## Translation

- Literal: "being desperate in chasing, and…"
- Natural: "so desperate just to keep up the chase…"

## Notes

- **Two kinds of のに.** (1) The conjunction のに "although, even though" (頑張ったのに負けた "I tried hard, yet I lost"). (2) The nominaliser の + the particle に, as here: "in ~ing, for ~ing". The second goes with 必死, 忙しい, 夢中 and 一生懸命 (準備するのに忙しい "busy getting ready", 生きるのに必死 "desperate just to survive") and with words of usefulness (駅に行くのに便利 "handy for getting to the station"). Read as "although", the line would mean "although I chase, desperately…", which leaves 必死で without anything to be desperate about.
- **必死.** 必 "certain" + 死 "death": originally "certain death" (必死の覚悟 "a readiness to die"), now "desperate, all-out". It appears as 必死だ (predicate), 必死に (adverb: 必死に走る "run for dear life") and, as here, 必死で, which can also work as an adverb (必死で走る). In the chain it links clauses, like 曖昧で on line 022 and いっぱいで on line 025.
- **What is chased?** 追いかける has no object here. The stock phrase 夢を追いかける "chase one's dream" makes the 小さな夢 of line 021 the natural object. Line 022 has just said the wishes are vague, so the chase is desperate yet without a clear aim. The verb 追う "chase" also begins line 009's 追い付けない "can't catch up". In the first verse the speaker could not catch up; in the second, they wear themselves out chasing.
- **Spelling.** The kanji spelling 追い掛ける exists, but 追いかける, with the second element in kana, is the usual one. The song writes 掛ける in kanji on line 028 (負担ばかり掛けてしまったの), where it means "impose" rather than "chase". The two are the same verb かける in different senses.
- **Link to line 024.** The chase ends on line 024 (息切らせ立ち止まって "out of breath, I came to a stop"), the last line of the stanza. Line 025 looks back on that moment: the speaker, stopped and breathless, is left with a head full of their own problems (自分の事でいっぱいで).
