## Notes

### How the two lines connect

Neither line has anything left out that must be filled in: unlike most of the song, there is no unstated speaker here, because the refrain is about the season, not about わたし.

### Ending on the refrain

The song’s last new words are on line 038 (これからも きっと<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>丈<rt>じょう</rt>夫<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>). The ending repeats lines 005–006 instead of adding a final statement, so the song closes on an image rather than a conclusion. The first time, the refrain followed a doubt (line 004, <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるのかな) and led into failures (line 007). Now it follows reassurance, and nothing comes after it. The words are the same, but they are heard differently.

### The last word is ゆく

〜てゆく is “go on ~ing”, a change continuing from now into the future. Ending the song on it leaves the motion running past the last line: the petals are still falling, and time is still moving. The ゆく of the refrain is the literary form the song uses for the world around the speaker (<ruby>散<rt>ち</rt></ruby>ってゆく, <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく on line 016); the speaker’s own state got the casual く (<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えてく, line 007).

### また and the cycle

また “again” says that this spring is one of many. The refrain itself comes round again here, so the word repeats along with the scene. After これからも “from now on, too” on line 038, the cycle can be heard as the future the speaker is sure of: more springs, with new green and falling blossoms.

### 〜の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby> without a particle

For the place of an action, prose has 〜の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で, and for a path 〜の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>を (<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>を<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く “walk in the rain”). But 〜の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>、 alone is a standard written pattern for the circumstances around an event (<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt>勢<rt>ぜい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt>守<rt>まも</rt></ruby>る<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>、<ruby>式<rt>しき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まった “the ceremony began as many looked on”). The lyric’s <ruby>祝<rt>しゅく</rt>福<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby> is ordinary written style, not a poetic omission.

### Blessing and falling blossoms

In Japan the cherry blossom season is also the season of graduations and entrance ceremonies, of partings and new starts. Falling blossoms are the classic image of beauty that does not last. <ruby>祝<rt>しゅく</rt>福<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby> “the light of blessing” is not a fixed phrase. It paints the spring sunshine as if it were celebrating someone. The song ends with the two together: something ends, and something is blessed.
