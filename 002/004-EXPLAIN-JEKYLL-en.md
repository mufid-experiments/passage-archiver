### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="004" %}

The line is grammatical and ends a sentence. Its object is on line 003: <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かって<ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち, with を dropped, is what the speaker would put into words (<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして) and convey (<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる). <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして is the te-form of <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にする “put into words”, and it says how the conveying would be done: “by putting (them) into words”. <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる is the potential of <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>える, “can convey”. After it, の (the explanatory の of のだ), か (question) and な (musing) together make のかな, “I wonder if…”, a question the speaker asks themselves rather than anyone else. The subject (the speaker, わたし of line 020) and the person the feelings should reach (<ruby>相<rt>あい</rt>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>に “to them”) are unstated, as usual. The space separates the means from the verb, where prose would have nothing or a comma. The register is plain and casual, and the potential keeps its ら (<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる, not the colloquial <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えれる). A stanza break follows, and line 005 starts the refrain with a new sentence. Restored prose: （わたしは）<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かって<ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち（を）、<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして（<ruby>相<rt>あい</rt>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>に）<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるのかな。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="004" %}

<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして and <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる share their object, <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち on line 003. The te-form is not a separate event here: putting the feelings into words is how they would be conveyed. <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる is potential, so the doubt is about ability, “can I?”, not about will. の turns the clause into a situation, and かな asks about it without expecting an answer.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="004" %}

4.18.1 has な after か “to indicate that the speaker is considering something”, as in いい<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt>学<rt>がく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けるかな？ “I wonder if I can go to a good college”, a potential before かな, as here. 4.3.2 says that in casual speech か is less a question to someone than a way “to consider whether something is true or not”, and the explanatory の of 3.11.5 makes the doubt about the whole situation. 4.6.3 prefers が or は to を with a potential verb (<ruby>富<rt>ふ</rt>士<rt>じ</rt>山<rt>さん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>れた); the lyric never has to choose, since <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち has no particle. 4.7.1’s noun + にする is “decide on” (ハンバーガーとサラダにします “I’ll have the hamburger and salad”); the “make into” of <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にする is closer to 3.12.1’s <ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>部<rt>へ</rt>屋<rt>や</rt></ruby>をきれいにした, “did her own room toward clean”.

## Notes

### The whole sentence, 003–004

<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かって<ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち（を）、<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるのかな。 It closes the first stanza on a question with no answer. The song answers it near the end, on line 036, which takes up the same words: <ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>めて <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にしてきたんだ “(but) I faced it and have been putting it into words”.

### かな and のかな

<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるかな “I wonder if I’ll be able to convey them” looks ahead to a single outcome. <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるのかな adds the explanatory の, and with it a doubt about the whole premise: “is it even possible to…?” Both are casual and self-directed; to ask someone else, the speaker would use <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる？ or <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるかな？ with rising intonation.

### <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる: potential, not passive

られる is also the passive and honorific auxiliary, so <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられる could in form mean “be conveyed”. Here the agent of <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして is the speaker, and the feelings are its object, so only the potential fits: “can (I) convey”. Casual speech often drops the ら (<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えれる, ら<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>), but the lyric keeps the standard form. The song’s other potentials belong to godan verbs, where the question of ら does not arise: <ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない (003), <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>い<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けない (009), <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めずに (015), <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がれなくて (029).

### <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にする

Xにする means “make into X”: に marks the result of a change (<ruby>形<rt>かたち</rt></ruby>にする “give shape to”, <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>形<rt>かたち</rt></ruby>にする “make a dream real”). <ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にする “put into words” is a stock phrase for feelings that are hard to express (<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にできない “beyond words”). <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>える and its intransitive <ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>わる form a pair: <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちを<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>える “convey one’s feelings” (the speaker acts) and <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちが<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>わる “one’s feelings get through” (the result). The line asks about the speaker’s own ability, not about whether the feelings will arrive.

### Link to line 003

Line 003 ends on the bare noun <ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち; only this line shows that it is an object. In prose the を would normally be said: <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かって<ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby>えない<ruby>気<rt>き</rt>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちを<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>にして<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられるのかな.

### Link to line 005: the refrain

のかな is sentence-final, and after the stanza break line 005 (<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>く<ruby>香<rt>かお</rt></ruby>り やがて<ruby>緑<rt>みどり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>芽<rt>め</rt>吹<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>いて) starts a new sentence with its own topic, <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>は. Nothing carries over in grammar. The refrain moves from the speaker’s doubt to a spring scene of breeze, buds and falling cherry blossoms. Line 038 notes how different the same refrain sounds when it returns after the reassurance of lines 037–038.
