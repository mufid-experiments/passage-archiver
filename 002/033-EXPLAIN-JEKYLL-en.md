### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="033" %}

The line is grammatical but has no predicate of its own. <ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby>いても and <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まっても are two parallel concessive clauses (〜ても、〜ても “even if…, even if…”), and their subject, the speaker (わたし, line 020), is unstated, as is normal. <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して “never” is an adverb that can only be completed by a negative, so the line stops before its statement is finished: the negative arrives on line 034 as the ない of <ruby>閉<rt>と</rt></ruby>ざされたりしない, together with the main clause and its topic, <ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>は. Line 032 closes its sentence with the terminal だ of くれたんだ, so this line starts a new sentence. The one marked feature is the position of <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して. The neutral order puts it after the topic, just before the predicate it negates (<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>閉<rt>と</rt></ruby>ざされたりしない). Placing it before the topic is also possible in prose, as an emphatic order, and here the line break makes the listener wait for the negative. Restored prose: <ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby>いても、<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まっても、<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>閉<rt>と</rt></ruby>ざされたりしない。

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="033" %}

The two ても clauses are parallel, and both attach to the main predicate on line 034: “whether (I) stumble or (I) stop, …”. Their subject is the unstated speaker, while the main clause has a different one, <ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby> (topic は). A ても clause states a condition that does not change the outcome: whatever happens there, the main clause still holds. <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して modifies the negated predicate <ruby>閉<rt>と</rt></ruby>ざされたりしない. Like <ruby>全<rt>ぜん</rt>然<rt>ぜん</rt></ruby>…ない and <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しも…ない, it announces a negative ending before the verb arrives.

## Notes

### Link to lines 032 and 034

Lines 031–032 form a finished sentence: what the speaker has built up gave them the strength to <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む “move forward”. This line starts a new one about the things that interrupt that movement, tripping and stopping, and line 034 completes it: <ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby>いても、<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まっても、<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>閉<rt>と</rt></ruby>ざされたりしない “Even if I stumble, even if I stop, the future will never be closed off.”

### <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して…ない

In modern Japanese <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して pairs only with a negative (<ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れない “I’ll never forget”) or a prohibition (<ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けるな “never open it”). It cannot mean “definitely” in a positive sentence; that is <ruby>必<rt>かなら</rt></ruby>ず or <ruby>絶<rt>ぜっ</rt>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>に. It began as the te-form of <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>する “decide”, but it is now a fixed adverb. <ruby>絶<rt>ぜっ</rt>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>に…ない says much the same in a more everyday tone; <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して sounds more earnest and slightly written.

### Line-final pointers

Three times in this stanza a line ends on a word that grammatically belongs with the next line: <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に (031) goes with <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む (032), <ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して (033) with the negative on 034, and だけど (035) with the clause on 036. Each time the line break holds the sentence open for a moment.

### The walking metaphor

<ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby>く (trip over something underfoot) and <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まる (stop walking) continue the song’s path imagery: <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めずに “unable to move forward” (015), <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt>歩<rt>ぽ</rt></ruby> “a small step” (019), <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby> (031–032). The song has already shown both kinds of setback: the speaker stopped, out of breath, on line 024 (<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって), and fell on lines 014 (<ruby>倒<rt>たお</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きて) and 029 (<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がれなくて). The ても clauses allow for them happening again.

### <ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby>く

Dictionaries trace つまずく to <ruby>爪<rt>つま</rt></ruby> (つま, the tip of the foot) + <ruby>突<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く (つく, “strike”), which is why the kana spelling つまづく is also permitted, though つまずく is standard. The literal use takes に for what one trips on (<ruby>石<rt>いし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby>く “trip over a stone”); figuratively it means to fail at some point in life (<ruby>人<rt>じん</rt>生<rt>せい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby>く). <ruby>躓<rt>つまず</rt></ruby> is not among the <ruby>常<rt>じょう</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>漢<rt>かん</rt>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>, so everyday writing uses kana; like <ruby>貰<rt>もら</rt></ruby> (003) and <ruby>竦<rt>すく</rt></ruby> (029), the kanji is a stylistic choice.
