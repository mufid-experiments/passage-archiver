# Line 003 · 分かって貰えない気持ち

## Context

- Line 002 (previous): すれ違ってばかりの 毎日
- **Line 003 (explained here): 分かって貰えない気持ち**
- Line 004 (next): 言葉にして 伝えられるのかな

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: noun phrase whose particle (を) is omitted; it is the object of the verbs on line 004.

The line is a grammatical noun phrase: the relative clause 分かって貰えない "that (I) can't get (anyone) to understand" modifies the noun 気持ち "feelings". It is not a sentence. The noun has no particle after it, and its verbs come on the next line: 気持ちを言葉にする "put feelings into words" and 気持ちを伝える "convey feelings" are both stock phrases, so 気持ち is the object of 言葉にして and 伝えられる on line 004, with を dropped, as casual speech and lyrics often do. A topic は (気持ちは) would also fit, with almost the same meaning. Line 002 closes its phrase on the noun 毎日 (体言止め), so this line starts a new sentence, and line 004 completes it. Two people are left unstated, as is normal. The one whose feelings these are is the speaker (the わたし of line 020), and the one who fails to understand would be marked with に (誰にも "by anyone", or a particular あなたに). The spelling 貰えない is a stylistic choice; standard writing has 分かってもらえない. Restored prose, with line 004: （わたしは）分かって貰えない気持ち（を）、言葉にして伝えられるのかな。

## Reading

- Kana: わかって もらえない きもち
- Romaji: wakatte moraenai kimochi

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 分かっ | わかっ (wakat-) | godan verb 分かる, continuative form (連用形) with sound change to small っ (促音便: 分かり → 分かっ) before て | to understand |
| 2 | て | て (te) | conjunctive particle (接続助詞), te-form | joins 分かっ to the subsidiary verb 貰える |

| 4 | ない | ない (nai) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) ない, negative, attributive form (連体形) | "not": 分かって貰えない "(I) can't get (anyone) to understand"; closes the relative clause (連体修飾節) on 気持ち |
| 5 | 気持ち | きもち (kimochi) | noun | feeling(s), heart; head of the relative clause, and object of 言葉にして / 伝えられる on line 004, with を omitted |

## How it fits together

`[[(わたしが) (誰にも) 分かっ て 貰え ない] 気持ち] (を) → line 004 [言葉 に し て] [伝え られる] の かな`

分かって貰えない is a relative clause on 気持ち, and the head noun stands for what would be understood: (誰かに)気持ちを分かって貰う "get someone to understand one's feelings" becomes 分かって貰えない気持ち "feelings (I) can't get anyone to understand". In the 〜てもらう construction the one who receives the favour, here the speaker, is the subject, and the one who does it would take に. Neither is stated. The noun phrase then waits, without its particle, for the verbs of line 004.

## Translation

- Literal: "feelings that (I) can't get (anyone) to understand"
- Natural: "These feelings no one understands…" (with line 004: "These feelings no one understands: I wonder if I can put them into words and get them across.")

## Notes

- **〜てもらえない and 〜てくれない.** Both mean, in effect, "(they) don't understand me", but from different sides. 分かってくれない takes the side of the other person: "they won't understand me", a complaint about them. 分かってもらえない takes the speaker's side: "I can't get them to understand", a failure to get through. The second fits line 004, where the speaker doubts their own ability to put the feelings into words. The plain passive 理解されない "is not understood" would be neutral and much colder.
- **Link to line 002.** Lines 001–002 are a noun phrase that ends on 毎日; there is no grammatical tie to this line. The tie is one of meaning: days of quarrels and すれ違い "failing to connect" leave the speaker with feelings that nobody understands.
- **Link to line 004.** On first hearing, the bare noun 気持ち sounds like a second 体言止め beside 毎日 (line 002). Line 004 then turns it into an object: 言葉にして 伝えられるのかな "I wonder if I can put (them) into words and get (them) across". Dropping を before a verb that follows at once is common in casual speech (ご飯食べた？ "have you eaten?"). Here the line break stands between the noun and its verbs.
- **分かる with を.** 分かる usually marks what is understood with が (日本語が分かる "understand Japanese"). In 〜てもらう and 〜てほしい, を is now common and natural (気持ちを分かってほしい "I want you to understand my feelings"). The lyric avoids the choice by dropping the particle. 分かる is the standard spelling; 解る and 判る are alternatives that songs sometimes use for nuance.
- **Spelling 貰.** 貰 is not on the 常用漢字 (Jōyō) list, and style guides write subsidiary verbs in kana anyway (〜てもらう, 〜ていく, 〜てしまう). The kanji here is a stylistic choice, like 些細 and 揉めて on line 001. The song's other giving and receiving verbs make an arc. Here the speaker cannot get understanding from others; on line 027 they ask for comfort (慰めて欲しい); on line 032, what they themselves have built up 進む力をくれた "gave (them) the strength to move forward".
- **気持ち.** 気 "spirit, mind" + 持ち "holding": how one's mind is held, "feelings, mood". The word appears only twice in the song: here, as feelings nobody understands, and on line 037, as 信じる素直な気持ち "an honest, believing heart", near the end.
