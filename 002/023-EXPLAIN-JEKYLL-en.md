### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="023" %}

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける “chase” is nominalised by の (“chasing”), and に marks what the speaker is desperate in: <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかけるのに<ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby> “desperate in the chase, frantic just to keep chasing”. <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で is the continuative form (<ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>形<rt>けい</rt></ruby>) of the na-adjective <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>だ “desperate, frantic”, so it links on to the next line: “(I) was desperate, and…”. The subject is the speaker again (the わたし of line 020), after line 022’s topic <ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い<ruby>事<rt>ごと</rt></ruby>は. What is chased is unstated; the <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby> of line 021 is the natural candidate. Two things could be misread here. のに is not the conjunction “although”, and で is not the particle “at, by” (see Notes). The chain continues to line 024, where it ends, still open, on <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって. Line 022, written <ruby>暖<rt>あい</rt>味<rt>まい</rt></ruby>で, is read as <ruby>曖<rt>あい</rt>昧<rt>まい</rt></ruby>で “vague” (see line 022). Restored prose: （わたしは）（<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を）<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかけるのに<ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で、…

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="023" %}

<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかけるの is a noun phrase, and に links it to <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>, naming the field of the desperation. The whole is a state, like <ruby>曖<rt>あい</rt>昧<rt>まい</rt></ruby>で on line 022, and it gives the reason for line 024: the chase was so desperate that it ended with the speaker out of breath, standing still.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="023" %}

3.11.4 turns a verb into a noun with の, as in <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt>勉<rt>べん</rt>強<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>するのは<ruby>大<rt>たい</rt>変<rt>へん</rt></ruby> “the thing of studying every day is tough”: that is the <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかけるの of this line. The に after it, “in, for”, has no section. 4.4.4 is the other のに, “despite” (<ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>日<rt>にち</rt>運<rt>うん</rt>動<rt>どう</rt></ruby>したのに、<ruby>全<rt>ぜん</rt>然<rt>ぜん</rt>痩<rt>や</rt></ruby>せなかった “despite exercising every day, I didn’t get thinner”), which the note below sets against this one. 4.4.1 chains a na-adjective with で, as on line 022, so <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で leads on to line 024. <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける is <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>う + かける, a compound of the kind at the end of 4.1.2.

## Notes

### Two kinds of のに

(1) The conjunction のに “although, even though” (<ruby>頑<rt>がん</rt>張<rt>ば</rt></ruby>ったのに<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けた “I tried hard, yet I lost”). (2) The nominaliser の + the particle に, as here: “in ~ing, for ~ing”. The second goes with <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>, <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい, <ruby>夢<rt>む</rt>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> and <ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt>生<rt>しょう</rt>懸<rt>けん</rt>命<rt>めい</rt></ruby> (<ruby>準<rt>じゅん</rt>備<rt>び</rt></ruby>するのに<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい “busy getting ready”, <ruby>生<rt>い</rt></ruby>きるのに<ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby> “desperate just to survive”) and with words of usefulness (<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くのに<ruby>便<rt>べん</rt>利<rt>り</rt></ruby> “handy for getting to the station”). Read as “although”, the line would mean “although I chase, desperately…”, which leaves <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で without anything to be desperate about.

### <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>

<ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt></ruby> “certain” + <ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby> “death”: originally “certain death” (<ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>の<ruby>覚<rt>かく</rt>悟<rt>ご</rt></ruby> “a readiness to die”), now “desperate, all-out”. It appears as <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>だ (predicate), <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>に (adverb: <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>に<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る “run for dear life”) and, as here, <ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で, which can also work as an adverb (<ruby>必<rt>ひっ</rt>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>で<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る). In the chain it links clauses, like <ruby>曖<rt>あい</rt>昧<rt>まい</rt></ruby>で on line 022 and いっぱいで on line 025.

### What is chased?

<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける has no object here. The stock phrase <ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける “chase one’s dream” makes the <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby> of line 021 the natural object. Line 022 has just said the wishes are vague, so the chase is desperate yet without a clear aim. The verb <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>う “chase” also begins line 009’s <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>い<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けない “can’t catch up”. In the first verse the speaker could not catch up; in the second, they wear themselves out chasing.

### Spelling

The kanji spelling <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>い<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける exists, but <ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>いかける, with the second element in kana, is the usual one. The song writes <ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける in kanji on line 028 (<ruby>負<rt>ふ</rt>担<rt>たん</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>けてしまったの), where it means “impose” rather than “chase”. The two are the same verb かける in different senses.

### Link to line 024

The chase ends on line 024 (<ruby>息<rt>いき</rt>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>らせ<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>まって “out of breath, I came to a stop”), the last line of the stanza. Line 025 looks back on that moment: the speaker, stopped and breathless, is left with a head full of their own problems (<ruby>自<rt>じ</rt>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事<rt>こと</rt></ruby>でいっぱいで).
