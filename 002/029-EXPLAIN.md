# Line 029 · 震え 竦み 目を逸らし 立ち上がれなくて

## Context

- Line 028 (previous): 負担ばかり掛けてしまったの
- **Line 029 (explained here): 震え 竦み 目を逸らし 立ち上がれなくて**
- Line 030 (next): あんな日々は もう嫌だよ

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: three verbs in the continuative form (連用中止法), set apart by spaces, then a negative te-form; a fragment that line 030 sums up as あんな日々.

The line is grammatical but has no main predicate. It is a list of four states of the speaker (the わたし of line 020), each separated by a space. The first three are verbs in their continuative form (連用形), used to link clauses without て (連用中止法, a written, slightly literary style): 震え "trembling", 竦み "freezing up", and 目を逸らし "looking away", the only one with an object. The fourth is a negative potential in its te-form: 立ち上がれなくて "unable to stand up". None of them has a tense, and the last one is open. Line 030 picks up the whole list with あんな日々は "days like those" and rejects it (もう嫌だよ "I don't want that anymore"). The te-form is not a modifier of 日々, since a te-form cannot modify a noun. It is a clause of its own, summed up by あんな. Prose would link the verbs with te-forms: 震えて、竦んで、目を逸らして、立ち上がれなくて. Line 028 ends the previous stanza with の, so this line starts a new sentence. Restored prose, with line 030: （わたしは）震えて、竦んで、目を逸らして、立ち上がれなくて——あんな日々はもう嫌だよ。

## Reading

- Kana: ふるえ / すくみ / め を そらし / たちあがれなくて
- Romaji: furue / sukumi / me o sorashi / tachiagarenakute

## How it fits together

`(わたしは) [震え] ／ [竦み] ／ [目 を 逸らし] ／ [立ち上がれ なく て] → line 030 [[あんな 日々] は] もう [嫌 だ] よ`

Four predicates, one unstated subject. The list gets worse as it goes: the body shakes, then freezes, then the eyes turn away, and finally the speaker cannot get up at all. The first three links are the bare continuative form, and only the last has て. That last link connects the list to line 030, where あんな gathers it all up as "days like those".

## Translation

- Literal: "trembling, cowering, averting (my) eyes, not able to stand up, and…"
- Natural: "Trembling, frozen with fear, looking away, unable to get back on my feet…"

## Notes

- **Parallel with line 011.** The song's two short stanzas open in the same way. Line 011 (揺れる 焦る 燻ぶった熱を 抱えてる) begins with bare verbs separated by spaces, in their terminal form, each a statement on its own. This line uses the continuative form, so its verbs link on. Both lines describe the speaker's state, and both stanzas have a なくて at their centre: 届かなくて on line 012, 立ち上がれなくて here. Line 012 leaves its なくて hanging. Here line 030 follows with a decision: あんな日々は もう嫌だよ.
- **Getting up, and not.** In the first half, the speaker falls and gets up again (倒れ起きて, line 014). Here they cannot stand at all. Later the song allows for falling again without despair: 躓いても 立ち止まっても… 未来は閉ざされたりしない "even if I stumble, even if I stop, the future will never be closed off" (lines 033–034). This line is the low point between the two.
- **竦む.** 足が竦む and 身が竦む describe being frozen by fear, legs that won't move. The noun すくみ appears in 三すくみ "a three-way standoff", where no one can move. 竦 is not on the 常用漢字 (Jōyō) list, so everyday writing uses すくむ; the kanji is a stylistic choice, like 貰 (003) and 躓 (033).
- **目を逸らす.** Literally "turn one's eyes away", and very commonly figurative: 現実から目を逸らす "turn away from reality", 問題から目を逸らす "avoid looking at the problem". Both senses fit: not meeting anyone's eyes, and not facing what has gone wrong (the burden of line 028). 逸 is a Jōyō kanji, but only with the reading イツ (逸話 "anecdote"). そらす is not a Jōyō reading, so standard writing has 目をそらす. The song later turns this around on line 036, 受け止めて "faced it squarely".
- **The continuative form as a link.** Using the bare 連用形 to end one clause and start the next (連用中止法) is ordinary in writing (資料を読み、要点をまとめる "read the materials and summarise the points"). In speech the te-form is more usual. The song uses it in its literary moments: 香り in the refrain (line 005) and the list here.
- **Link to line 028.** Line 028 ends the second verse with a confession, 負担ばかり掛けてしまったの. This line does not continue that sentence. It sums up, in four images, what the speaker was like in those days, before line 030 rejects them.
