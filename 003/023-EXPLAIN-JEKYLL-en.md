### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="023" %}

The line is grammatical and falls into three parts at the full-width spaces. <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたける “can take wing” is the potential form of <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたく “flap one’s wings”. <ruby>導<rt>みちび</rt></ruby>かれる “be guided, be led” is the passive of <ruby>導<rt>みちび</rt></ruby>く “guide”. まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>へ “toward what lies ahead, not yet seen” is a direction phrase with no verb of its own. It belongs to both verbs before it, so the order is inverted. Prose would say まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたける、<ruby>導<rt>みちび</rt></ruby>かれる “we can take wing, and are led, toward the unseen beyond”. The subject is unstated. It is “we”, the <ruby>僕<rt>ぼく</rt></ruby>たち of line 024. The one who does the leading is not named either, and the nearest candidate is the melody of line 022. まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ uses the old negative ぬ (= ない), which survives in set phrases and gives the line a literary ring. Both verbs could also be heard as modifiers of <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> alongside まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ (“toward the unseen place where we can fly, where we are led”), but reading them as two short statements is more natural. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="023" %}

The two verbs share an unstated subject (“we”), and both take the direction phrase at the end of the line. Prose order would be まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたける、<ruby>導<rt>みちび</rt></ruby>かれる. Putting the direction last makes “where to” the end point of the line, and of the movement.

## Notes

### Three phrases, one movement

Each part is short, and the spaces between them are breaths. The order runs from what we can do (<ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたける), to what is done for us (<ruby>導<rt>みちび</rt></ruby>かれる), to where it goes (まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>へ). Line 021 set two phrases side by side (<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ　<ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ), and this line sets three.

### <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたく and <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたける

<ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたく comes from <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby> (は) “feather, wing” + はたく “beat”. Literally it is a bird flapping its wings. Figuratively, it means “spread one’s wings, launch out into the world”: <ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたく “go out into the world”, a phrase used for graduates and young people starting out. Godan verbs form their potential by changing the final -u to -eru: <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける, <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたく → <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたける “be able to take wing”. The potential says the ability is there now.

### Flying and being led

The first verb is active and the second passive: the travellers fly by their own strength, and they are also carried. <ruby>導<rt>みちび</rt></ruby>かれる names no guide (no 〜に), but coming straight after お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>みたいな<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ (line 022), the natural guide is the melody, or the voice behind it. The song’s other passive, <ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく (lines 007, 019), also has something larger surrounding the people in it.

### まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ and the old negative ぬ

ぬ is the attributive form of the classical negative ず, and it survives in set phrases: <ruby>見<rt>み</rt>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らぬ<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> “a stranger”, <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らぬ<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に “before one knows it”, <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>わぬ “unexpected”. まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ “not yet seen” is a favourite in lyrics and slogans: まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>へ “toward a world not yet seen”, まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>へ. Everyday speech would say まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことのない “never yet seen”.

### <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> again

On line 017, <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> was the end of a road already followed: <ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>った<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>には<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう “at the end of the road, what’s real must be waiting”. Here <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> is a place no one has seen. The song’s “ahead” has moved from a destination it expects to one it cannot yet picture.

### へ and に

へ marks the direction (“toward”), and に the destination itself (“to, at”). まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>へ stresses setting out more than arriving. へ is the usual particle in slogans and titles of this kind: <ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>へ, <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>へ.

### Link to line 024

Line 024 (<ruby>幻<rt>まぼろし</rt></ruby>なんかじゃない　<ruby>僕<rt>ぼく</rt></ruby>たちは<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってるはず) answers a doubt the listener might have. After a melody “like a fairy tale” and a flight into the unknown, it insists: this is no illusion, and we know it.
