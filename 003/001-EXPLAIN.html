Line 001

Valid

みえない もの を しんじあうmienai mono o shinjiau

“We believe, together, in things we cannot see.”

Literal“(we) mutually believe in things that cannot be seen”

Is it valid Japanese?

complete sentenceobject + compound verb 信しんじ合あう in the terminal form; subject unstated.

The line is grammatical and complete. 見みえないもの (“things that can’t be seen”) is the object, marked by を, and 信しんじ合あう (“believe together, believe in each other”) is the predicate in the plain non-past, which here states something people generally do rather than a single event. The subject is not stated. Line 002 talks about 人ひと “people” in general, so the natural subject is people as a whole, the singer included (“we”). The song’s only personal pronoun, 僕ぼくたち “we”, does not appear until line 024. 信しんじ合あう usually means “trust each other” and has no object. Here it takes one, and the meaning becomes “believe in something together”, like 想おもいを分わけ合あう “share feelings” on line 009. That is a slightly poetic use, not an error. The register is plain and casual. Nothing needs correcting.

How it fits together

見みえ ない もの を 信しんじ 合あう

[[[見え ない] もの] を] [信じ 合う]

見みえない modifies もの, and the noun phrase is the object of 信しんじ合あう. The subject, people in general or “we”, is left unsaid. The sentence ends here, and line 002 adds the reason.

Line 002

Valid

だって ひと は よわい からdatte hito wa yowai kara

“Because, after all, people are weak.”

Literal“because people are weak”

With line 001“We believe, together, in things we cannot see, because people are weak, after all.”

Is it valid Japanese?

colloquiala reason clause (だって〜から) standing as its own sentence, after the statement it explains.

The line is grammatical and typical of speech. だって opens a justification (“because, after all”), and から closes the reason. There is no main clause after から. The statement it explains came first, on line 001, so the two lines work as 見みえないものを信しんじ合あう。だって人ひとは弱よわいから。 “We believe in what we can’t see. Because people are weak.” Ending a sentence on から is ordinary in speech when the reason comes as an afterthought. 人ひとは is a generic topic: the line is a statement about people as such, not about any one person. Prose that puts the reason first would be 人ひとは弱よわいから、見みえないものを信しんじ合あう。 Nothing needs correcting.

Notes

How lines 001 and 002 connect

Statement, then reason. Line 001 is complete on its own, and line 002 adds why. Prose would usually put the reason first (人ひとは弱よわいから、見みえないものを信しんじ合あう), but the order here is the order of speech: say what you do, then defend it, as in 行いかない。だって寒さむいから。 “I’m not going. It’s cold, that’s why.”

〜合あう, “each other, together”

After a verb’s continuative form, 合あう makes the action mutual: 助たすけ合あう “help each other”, 話はなし合あう “talk it over”, 見みつめ合あう “gaze at each other”. Without an object, 信しんじ合あう means “trust each other” (互たがいに信しんじ合あう). With an object, as here, the action is shared: each person believes in what can’t be seen, and each relies on the others believing it too. The same pattern returns in the chorus, 想おもいを分わけ合あって “sharing our feelings with each other” (lines 009, 025, 029). Both times, what is shared is something no one can hold.

見みえる, not 見みられる

見みえる means “be visible, can be seen”. The seeing happens by itself, so 見みえないもの is “the invisible”: trust, love, the future, things no one can see. 見みられないもの would be “things one doesn’t get to see, isn’t allowed to see”. The everyday phrase is 目めに見みえないもの “things invisible to the eye”.

The parallel with lines 003–004

見みえないもの “what can’t be seen” here is matched by 触ふれられないもの “what can’t be touched” on line 003: sight, then touch. Line 001 says what people do, and lines 003–004 ask why: 触ふれられないものばかり / 求もとめてしまうのは　なぜ “why do we end up seeking only what we can’t touch?”. Line 002’s reason, 人ひとは弱よわいから, sits between the statement and the question. It reads like an answer given before the question is asked, and the song asks it anyway.

だって, two different words

At the start of a sentence, だって is a conjunction: “because, after all; but”, used to justify oneself or to object (だって知しらなかったんだもん “But I didn’t know!”). It is casual and can sound like an excuse or a protest. The formal equivalent is なぜなら〜からだ. After a noun, だって is a different word, a particle meaning “even, too” (子こ供どもだって分わかる “even a child understands”). Here it is the conjunction, paired with から.

人ひとは弱よわい as a maxim

人ひと here means human beings, and は marks a generic topic, as in sayings like 人ひとは一人ひとりでは生いきられない “people can’t live alone”. The plain non-past of both lines (信しんじ合あう, 弱よわい) fits this: they state how things are, not what happened once. The singer is included, but the song only says “we” outright at line 024 (僕ぼくたちは知しってるはず).
