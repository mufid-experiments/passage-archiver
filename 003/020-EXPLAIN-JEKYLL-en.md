### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="020" %}

The line is grammatical and ends a sentence. まほろのうたごえ “the singing voice of mahoro” is the subject. だけ “only” follows it in place of が (だけが is also correct), and <ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く “resound, ring out” is the predicate, in the plain terminal form. Line 019’s <ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby>と<ruby>影<rt>かげ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく modifies the noun, so the full sentence is <ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby>と<ruby>影<rt>かげ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていくまほろのうたごえだけ（が）<ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く “only the singing voice of mahoro, enfolded in light and shadow, rings out”. The full-width space before <ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く is a pause that holds the verb back a beat. The line takes line 008’s bare noun phrase (まほろのうたごえ) and gives it a verb: what was an image is now a sound. For まほろ, see line 008. Line 021 starts a new sentence whose それ points back to this voice. Nothing needs correcting.

The relative clause from line 019 and まほろの both modify うたごえ. だけ limits the subject to that voice, and <ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く is its verb. The sentence ends here.

## Notes

### Line 008, completed

The first time, the phrase stood alone: まほろのうたごえ (line 008), a noun with no verb. This time it is the subject of a sentence and has a verb. The two sections end differently: lines 005–008 with a picture, after an open “if”, and lines 017–020 with a sound, after a sentence that expects the truth at the end of the road.

### だけ, “only”

だけ is the neutral “only, just”: <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しだけ “just a little”, <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>だけ “only you”. It can replace が and を, or stand in front of them (だけが, だけを). With a subject, it says that everything else is quiet: only the voice can be heard. It differs from ばかり on line 003 (<ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないものばかり “nothing but things we can’t touch”), which hinted at too much of one thing. だけ here clears the space for the voice.

### <ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く

<ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く is used for a sound that fills a space and carries: <ruby>鐘<rt>かね</rt></ruby>の<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く “the bell rings out”, <ruby>足<rt>あし</rt>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>廊<rt>ろう</rt>下<rt>か</rt></ruby>に<ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く “footsteps echo down the corridor”. It is also used of words or music that reach the heart: <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby> “a song that moves you”. Both senses fit: the voice rings out, and it reaches the listener.

### A pause before the verb

The full-width space sets <ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く apart, the same device as on line 004 (<ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めてしまうのは　なぜ), where the question word was held back. The verb gets a beat to itself, much as a sound hangs in the air.
