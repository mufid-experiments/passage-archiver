### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="022" %}

The line is a grammatical noun phrase. お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>みたいな “like a fairy tale” modifies <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ “melody, strains of music”. みたいな is the casual form of のような. The phrase has no predicate of its own, and it completes line 021 in one of two ways. (a) If line 021’s two つなぐ ended their sentences, this line is an extra description of それ: “…a melody like a fairy tale.” (b) If they modify <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ, lines 021–022 make one “A is B” sentence with the copula left out: それは、<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ、<ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ、お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>みたいな<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ（だ） “it is a melody like a fairy tale, linking to the future and to the past”. Either way, <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ gives the voice of line 020 a new name: now it is a melody. The line mixes registers: お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby> is old-fashioned, <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ literary, and みたいな everyday speech. The kanji <ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt></ruby> and <ruby>噺<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> are not on the Jōyō list, so the spelling is a deliberate, storybook-like choice. Line 023 starts a new phrase. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="022" %}

お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>みたいな modifies <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ. In reading (b), <ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ and <ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ from line 021 modify <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ too, and the whole noun phrase is the predicate of それは, with だ left out. In reading (a), the phrase stands alone as a description of それ.

## Notes

### <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ, a literary word for “melody”

<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ is used in writing and poetry for the sound of music: <ruby>美<rt>うつく</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ “a beautiful melody”, <ruby>琴<rt>こと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ “the strains of the koto”. Everyday words would be メロディー or <ruby>曲<rt>きょく</rt></ruby>, and the technical term is <ruby>旋<rt>せん</rt>律<rt>りつ</rt></ruby> (せんりつ). The same noun also means “investigation” (<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ “questioning by the police”), from <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる “look into”. The musical sense goes back to an older use of <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる, “tune an instrument, play”. The song’s words for the sound have changed from うたごえ “singing voice” (lines 008, 020) to <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ “melody”.

### お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>

お is a polite or beautifying prefix. <ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt></ruby> (とぎ) originally meant keeping someone company, for example through a long night, with talk. In the 16th century, warlords such as Toyotomi Hideyoshi kept <ruby>御<rt>お</rt>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>衆<rt>しゅう</rt></ruby> (おとぎしゅう), companions who entertained them with talk and stories. <ruby>噺<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> is a kanji made in Japan (<ruby>国<rt>こく</rt>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>), used for spoken tales and especially for rakugo stories. An お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby> is the kind of tale told to children: <ruby>桃<rt>もも</rt>太<rt>た</rt>郎<rt>ろう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>浦<rt>うら</rt>島<rt>しま</rt>太<rt>た</rt>郎<rt>ろう</rt></ruby>. Neither <ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt></ruby> nor <ruby>噺<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> is on the Jōyō list, and the usual modern spelling is おとぎ<ruby>話<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>.

### みたいな and のような

Both mean “like”. みたいな (みたいだ) is casual. のような (ようだ) is neutral and the usual choice in writing. The song uses both kinds: <ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のように and <ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように (lines 013, 015), and here みたいな. With the old-fashioned お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby> and the literary <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ around it, みたいな keeps the line close to speech.

### Like a fairy tale, but real

Fairy tales are retold from one generation to the next, which suits a melody that links the future and the past (line 021). But a fairy tale is also something that isn’t real. Two lines later the song insists <ruby>幻<rt>まぼろし</rt></ruby>なんかじゃない “it’s no illusion” (line 024). The melody sounds like something from a story, and the song says it is true all the same.
