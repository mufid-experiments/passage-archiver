### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="017" %}

The line is grammatical but is not a sentence. ひとつひとつ<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>った “followed one by one” is a relative clause on <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> “the point ahead, the end of the way”, and には marks that point as the place, and the topic, of what follows. The main clause comes on line 018: <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう “what’s real must be waiting”. Together: ひとつひとつ<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>った<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>には<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう “at the end of what we follow, one by one, what’s real must be waiting”. The object of <ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る (“follow, trace”) is not stated, and neither is its subject. The natural objects are a path or a trail, or the memories and signs along the way. The subject is “we”, as elsewhere in the song. 〜た<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に “beyond, at the end of ~ing” is a standard pattern. <ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る is usually written in kana (たどる), and the kanji is a stylistic choice. Line 016 closed the previous section with “Ah”, and this line starts a new one. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="017" %}

ひとつひとつ modifies <ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>った, and the clause modifies <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>. に marks <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> as a place, and は makes it the topic. Line 018 says what is there.

## Notes

### A section that mirrors lines 005–008

Lines 017–020 have the same plan as lines 005–008. A two-line sentence about <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> “what’s real” comes first, then <ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby>と<ruby>影<rt>かげ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく, then the singing voice of mahoro. In the first section, <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> was something one might find out, with dread (もしも…<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってしまったら, lines 005–006). Here it is waiting at the end of the road (<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう, line 018). The wording is close, but the feeling has changed.

### ひとつひとつ

ひとつひとつ is “one by one, each and every one”: ひとつひとつ<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめる “check them one by one”, <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>をひとつひとつ<ruby>数<rt>かぞ</rt></ruby>える “count the memories one by one”. It stresses care and patience, every step taken in turn, not all at once. ひとつずつ “one at a time” is close in meaning.

### <ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る

<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る is “follow (a road, a trail), trace”: <ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る “follow a road”, <ruby>足<rt>あし</rt>跡<rt>あと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る “follow footprints”, <ruby>記<rt>き</rt>憶<rt>おく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る “trace back through one’s memories”, <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt>路<rt>じ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>る “make one’s way home”. It is often used of a way that has to be followed with care, step by step. The song gives no object. A path, a trail of signs, or memories all fit, and ひとつひとつ suggests separate stepping stones. <ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby> is not on the Jōyō list, so newspapers write たどる in kana.

### 〜た<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に

<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> is “the point ahead”. After a verb in た, it means the place you reach once the action is done: この<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>んだ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>がある “the station is further along this road”, <ruby>角<rt>かど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がった<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> “just around the corner”. Figuratively: <ruby>努<rt>ど</rt>力<rt>りょく</rt></ruby>した<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に “at the end of hard work”. The た marks the following as finished by the time one gets there, not as the past from now, so <ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>った<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> can lie in the future: “where we’ll be once we’ve followed it all”. <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby> returns on line 023, まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>へ “toward what lies ahead, not yet seen”.

### には

に marks the place, and は makes it the topic and adds contrast: “there, at the end”, as opposed to here and now. 〜には〜がある / いる is the usual frame for saying what is to be found somewhere, and line 018’s <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている “is waiting” fits that frame.

### Link to line 016
