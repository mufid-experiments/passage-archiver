# Line 016 · 流れてゆく日々よ　Ah

## Context

- Line 015 (previous): ふわり優しい風のように
- **Line 016 (explained here): 流れてゆく日々よ　Ah**
- Line 017 (next): ひとつひとつ辿った先には

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: a vocative noun phrase (relative clause + 日々 + よ) that completes line 015's simile; "Ah" closes the pair.

The line is grammatical. 流れてゆく "that flow on" is a relative clause on 日々 "days". The verb is the one line 015's adverbials were waiting for, so the two lines make one phrase: ふわり優しい風のように流れてゆく日々 "the days that drift softly by, like a gentle breeze". The particle よ after the noun is the vocative よ: it calls out to the days, "O days…!", as in 友よ "O friend". A phrase addressed to someone or something needs no predicate, so the line is complete as an exclamation. ゆく is the literary form of いく, here as the subsidiary verb in 〜てゆく "go on ~ing, ~ away". "Ah", after a full-width space, is a sung sound outside the grammar, and it closes the second pair of the section as it closed the first on line 014. Line 017 starts a new section. Nothing needs correcting.

## Reading

- Kana: ながれて ゆく ひび よ / Ah

## How it fits together

`(line 015: ふわり 優しい風のように →) [[流れ て ゆく] 日々] よ ／ Ah`

ふわり and 優しい風のように (line 015) both modify 流れてゆく. 流れてゆく modifies 日々, and よ turns the whole noun phrase into an address. The result is an exclamation to the days, with no predicate needed.

## Translation

- Literal: "O days that go on flowing — ah"
- Natural: "O days, drifting by… ah" (with line 015: "O days that drift softly by, like a gentle breeze… ah")

## Notes

- **Completes line 015.** The whole phrase is ふわり優しい風のように流れてゆく日々よ "O days that drift softly by, like a gentle breeze". Of the section's two pairs, this is the one that finishes its grammar (lines 013–014 left their adverbials open), and what it arrives at is time: the days.
- **Vocative よ.** After a noun, よ calls out to what the noun names: 友よ "O friend", 神よ "O God", 春よ、来い "Spring, come!". It is solemn and literary, more at home in songs and poems than in conversation. It is a different use from sentence-final よ (行くよ "I'm going, OK?"), which presses a statement on a listener. Here the singer speaks to the days themselves, as if they could hear.
- **〜てゆく and 〜ていく.** ゆく is the older, more literary form of いく, and in songs it is often preferred for its softer sound. Both are standard. The song uses both: 包まれていく (lines 007, 019) and 流れてゆく here. 〜てゆく puts the movement on a line leading away from the present: the days do not just pass, they pass on and are gone.
- **日々 and the mark 々.** 々 repeats the kanji before it: 日日 → 日々, 人々 (ひとびと) "people", 時々 (ときどき) "sometimes", 色々 (いろいろ) "various". The repeated syllable often takes rendaku, ひ → び, ひと → びと. 々 is not a kanji but a repetition mark (踊り字). 日々 means "the days, day after day, daily life": 忙しい日々 "busy days".
- **Time that flows.** 流れる is the everyday verb for time passing: 時が流れる "time flows", 月日が流れる "the months and days go by". The image here is gentle: the days pass like a breeze, softly and beyond holding. Earlier the chorus spoke of getting past time (時間を乗り越えて, line 010). Here the song simply lets time pass.
- **Link to line 017.** Line 017 (ひとつひとつ辿った先には "at the end of what we have followed, one by one") starts a new section. It moves from days that flow past by themselves to a path walked step by step. Line 018 finishes that sentence: 本当が待っているんだろう "what's real is surely waiting there".
