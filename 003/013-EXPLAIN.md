# Line 013 · 夜明け光る星のように

## Context

- Line 012 (previous): 日が昇る場所
- **Line 013 (explained here): 夜明け光る星のように**
- Line 014 (next): 蒼い月深く Ah

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: a simile phrase (〜のように) with no verb of its own to modify; 夜明け is used without the particle に.

The line is grammatical as a phrase, but it is not a sentence. 夜明け光る星 is "a star that shines at dawn": 光る is a relative clause on 星, and 夜明け gives the time. Prose would mark that time with に (夜明けに光る星). Dropping it is a poetic compression that makes 夜明け光る sound like a single image. A second reading takes 夜明け as a noun on its own, "daybreak", followed by "like a shining star". のように "like" is adverbial and needs a predicate to modify, but this line has none, and line 014 (蒼い月深く Ah) offers only the adverb 深く. The matching pair two lines later is complete: ふわり優しい風のように / 流れてゆく日々よ (lines 015–016), "O days that flow on like a soft, gentle breeze". This section builds images more than sentences, so the simile may reach forward to 流れてゆく on line 016 as well. Line 012 closed the chorus, and this line starts a new section. Nothing needs correcting.

## Reading

- Kana: よあけ ひかる ほし の ように
- Romaji: yoake hikaru hoshi no yō ni

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 夜明け | よあけ (yoake) | noun, compound of 夜 "night" + 明け (continuative of 明ける "(of night) end, (of day) break") | dawn, daybreak; here the time when the star shines, with に left out |
| 2 | 光る | ひかる (hikaru) | godan verb 光る, attributive form (連体形) | to shine, to glitter; the clause (夜明け)光る modifies 星 |
| 3 | 星 | ほし (hoshi) | noun | star |
| 4 | の | の (no) | case particle (格助詞), attributive | links 星 to ようだ (noun + の + ようだ) |
| 5 | ように | ように (yō ni) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) ようだ, simile (比況), continuative form (連用形) | "like, as": an adverbial simile; the predicate it should modify is not on this line |

## How it fits together

`[[[夜明け（に） 光る] 星] の ように] → (line 014: 蒼い月深く Ah …)`

夜明け光る modifies 星, and 星のように makes the whole phrase a comparison: "like a star that shines at dawn". The comparison is adverbial, so it is waiting for a verb or adjective. Line 014 gives the adverb 深く and no verb, and only line 016's 流れてゆく completes a ように in this section.

## Translation

- Literal: "like a star that shines at dawn"
- Natural: "Like a star still shining at daybreak…"

## Notes

- **A new section, at dawn.** The chorus ended on sunrise, 日が昇る場所 (line 012), and this section steps back to the moment just before it, when the night ends and a star still shines. Lines 013–016 come in two pairs, each closed by "Ah": 夜明け光る星のように / 蒼い月深く Ah, then ふわり優しい風のように / 流れてゆく日々よ　Ah. The first pair has the sky (star, moon), and the second has the air and time (breeze, days).
- **夜明け without に.** Some time words take no particle: 今日, 明日, 毎朝. Points in time and events normally take に: 三時に, 誕生日に, 夜明けに. 夜明けに光る星 is the prose form. Lyrics often drop particles for rhythm and compactness, and here the result reads almost like a compound, "the dawn-shining star".
- **A star at dawn.** As the sky grows light, the stars fade. The one that stays longest is usually Venus, the morning star, called 明けの明星 (あけのみょうじょう) in Japanese. A star still shining at daybreak is the last light of the night and the first sign of the day, and the line recalls that image without naming it.
- **〜のように.** Noun + のように means "like ~" and modifies a verb or an adjective: 星のように光る "shine like a star", 風のように走る "run like the wind". Before a noun it becomes のような: 星のような瞳 "star-like eyes". The casual equivalents are みたいに and みたいな, and the song uses those too: お伽噺みたいな調べ "a melody like a fairy tale" (line 022).
- **What the simile describes.** Line 014 has no verb, only 深く "deeply". The second pair shows the full pattern, a simile followed by a verb: 風のように流れてゆく "flow on like the wind" (lines 015–016). By that model, this simile can be heard as reaching ahead to the same days: days that pass like a star at dawn, like a gentle breeze. It can also be heard with line 014 alone, setting a star beside the moon. Lyrics often leave this kind of link open.
- **夜明け and 明ける.** 明ける, written with 明 "bright", means "grow light" and so "come to an end" (of a night, or a period): 夜が明ける "day breaks", literally "the night grows light, the night ends". The same verb gives 年が明ける "the old year ends, the new year begins". 夜明け is a little more poetic than 明け方 (あけがた, "around dawn") or 早朝 (そうちょう, "early morning").
