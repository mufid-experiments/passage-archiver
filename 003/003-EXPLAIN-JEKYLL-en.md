### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="003" %}

The line is grammatical but is not a sentence. <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられない (“that can’t be touched”) modifies もの (“things”), and ばかり (“nothing but”) follows. There is no verb. The phrase is the object of <ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めてしまう on line 004, and line 004 also ends the sentence: <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないものばかり<ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めてしまうのはなぜ “why is it that (we) end up seeking nothing but things (we) can’t touch?”. No を is needed, because ばかり can take the place of を (and of が) on its own. ばかりを is also correct, but the bare ばかり is the more common choice. <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられる is the standard potential form of <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れる. Casual speech often drops the ら (<ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れれる), but the song uses the standard form. Line 002 does not continue into this line. It ended its own sentence on から, and this line starts a new one. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="003" %}

<ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられない is a relative clause on もの. Its gap is the に-object of <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れる: the things one can’t touch. ばかり narrows the phrase to “nothing but those things”. The verb it belongs to, <ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めて, opens line 004, and のは　なぜ then turns the whole clause into a question.

## Notes

### The parallel with line 001

<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えないもの “what can’t be seen” (line 001) and <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないもの “what can’t be touched” (this line) have the same shape: a negative “can’t” clause on the noun もの. Sight first, then touch. Line 001 states something people do (believe in the invisible), and lines 003–004 ask a question about the intangible: why it is all we seem to reach for.

### Link to line 002

Line 002 (だって<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>いから) ended its sentence on から, giving the reason for line 001. Heard straight after it, this line sounds like it might be the effect: “people are weak, so they reach for nothing but…”. Line 004’s のは　なぜ then makes clear it is a new sentence and a question.

### ばかり after a noun

ばかり means “nothing but, only, all”: <ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる “eat nothing but meat”, <ruby>失<rt>しっ</rt>敗<rt>ぱい</rt></ruby>ばかりだ “it’s nothing but failures”. It can replace が and を, or stand in front of them (ばかりを, ばかりが). Unlike neutral だけ “only”, ばかり usually suggests too much of one thing, which fits the rueful てしまう on line 004. The song’s other limiting particle is だけ, on line 020 (まほろのうたごえだけ　<ruby>響<rt>ひび</rt></ruby>く “only the singing voice resounds”), where the sense is neutral or even positive.

### <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れる and <ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>る

Both are written with <ruby>触<rt>しょく</rt></ruby>, and the okurigana decides the reading: <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れる is ふれる, <ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>る is さわる. さわる is plainer and more physical (“touch, handle”: <ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>らないで “don’t touch”). ふれる is lighter and wider (“come into contact with”), and it is used figuratively: <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れる “touch the heart”, <ruby>話<rt>わ</rt>題<rt>だい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れる “touch on a topic”. <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないもの can therefore be what is physically out of reach or, more broadly, what can’t be grasped at all.

### Potential, passive, and the dropped ら

For an ichidan verb like <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れる, the ending られる serves as potential, passive and honorific alike. In this line only the potential reading makes sense: <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないもの “things one can’t touch” (passive would be “things that aren’t touched”). In casual speech, the potential of ichidan verbs often drops the ら (ら<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>言<rt>こと</rt>葉<rt>ば</rt></ruby>): <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>れる, <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>れる, and <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れれる. That form is common but still counted as non-standard. The song uses the standard <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられる.
