# Line 014 · 蒼い月深く Ah

## Context

- Line 013 (previous): 夜明け光る星のように
- **Line 014 (explained here): 蒼い月深く Ah**
- Line 015 (next): ふわり優しい風のように

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid as a poetic fragment: noun phrase 蒼い月 + the adverbial form 深く, with no particle and no verb; "Ah" is a sung exclamation.

The line is not a sentence, and its grammar is left unfinished on purpose. 蒼い月 "the pale-blue moon" is a noun phrase with no particle after it, so the line does not say whether the moon is a subject, a place or something else. 深く is the continuative form (連用形) of 深い "deep". It is used as an adverb, "deeply, deep", and an adverb needs a verb or adjective to modify. None follows: the line ends on "Ah", which is a sung sound, not a word in the sentence. A listener fills the gap, and the likely fillings are in the Notes. Line 013's simile (星のように "like a star") was also left without a verb, so lines 013–014 together make a pair of images rather than a statement. The next pair, lines 015–016, does finish its pattern (風のように流れてゆく). 蒼い is a literary spelling of あおい, usually written 青い. "Ah" is written in Latin letters and preceded by a half-width space. Line 016 has a full-width space before its "Ah", which is a typing difference with no meaning. Nothing here is an error to be corrected.

## Reading

- Kana: あおい つき ふかく / Ah
- Romaji: aoi tsuki fukaku / ah

## How it fits together

`(line 013: 夜明け光る星のように →) [蒼い 月] 深く（…verb unsaid） ／ Ah`

蒼い modifies 月. 深く hangs open, waiting for a verb, and line 013's ように is waiting too. Neither gets one, so the two lines form a pair of images: a star at dawn, and the pale moon. "Ah" stands outside the sentence.

## Translation

- Literal: "pale-blue moon, deeply — ah"
- Natural: "the pale-blue moon, deep… ah" (with line 013: "Like a star still shining at daybreak, the pale-blue moon, deep… ah")

## Notes

- **What 深く belongs to.** The 〜く form of an adjective can modify a verb (深く眠る "sleep deeply") or link two clauses (夜は深く、月は蒼い "the night is deep, the moon pale"). Here neither follows, so the listener completes the line, most plausibly in one of three ways. (1) With an unsaid verb: 蒼い月（が）深く（沈む） "the pale moon (sinks) deep", as it does near dawn. (2) As "deep in", noun + 深く, a common pattern: 森深く "deep in the forest", 地中深く "deep underground", 胸の奥深く "deep in one's heart". Then the phrase is something like "deep in the pale-blue moon(light)". (3) As a modifier moved after its noun, from 深く蒼い月 "a deeply blue moon". The song does not choose.
- **The first pair, 013–014.** Both lines end on an adverbial that never reaches its verb: ように (line 013) and 深く (this line). The star and the moon are set side by side at dawn, building a mood rather than a statement, and "Ah" closes the pair. The second pair is grammatically complete: ふわり優しい風のように / 流れてゆく日々よ "O days that flow on like a soft, gentle breeze" (lines 015–016).
- **蒼 and 青.** 青い is the standard spelling of あおい. 蒼 is not on the Jōyō list (it is used in names, such as 蒼 そう/あおい), so 蒼い is a literary choice. It suggests a deeper, darker or greyer blue, like the deep sky or sea, and also pallor: 蒼白 (そうはく) "deathly pale". A 蒼い月 is a pale, cool-coloured moon, the way the moon looks in a sky that is turning blue at dawn.
- **The moon at dawn.** A moon still in the sky at daybreak is a classical image in Japanese poetry, 有明の月 (ありあけのつき) "the moon at dawn", often tied to waiting and to parting. The song doesn't use the term, but with 夜明け on line 013 the picture is the same: the night is ending and the moon has not yet gone.
- **"Ah".** Many J-pop lyrics write sung sounds and English words in Latin letters. "Ah" is the singer's breath of feeling, and the Japanese equivalent would be ああ, an interjection (感動詞). It has no grammatical role. It marks the end of each pair in this section, here and on line 016 (流れてゆく日々よ　Ah).
- **Link to line 015.** Line 015 (ふわり優しい風のように) starts the second pair with another simile, this time a soft breeze, and line 016 gives it its verb.
