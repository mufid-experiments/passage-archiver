# Line 015 · ふわり優しい風のように

## Context

- Line 014 (previous): 蒼い月深く Ah
- **Line 015 (explained here): ふわり優しい風のように**
- Line 016 (next): 流れてゆく日々よ　Ah

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: the adverb ふわり (without と) + a simile, 優しい風のように; both lead into the verb 流れてゆく on line 016.

The line is grammatical but is not a sentence. ふわり is a mimetic adverb, "softly, lightly", for an airy, floating movement. 優しい風のように is a simile, "like a gentle breeze", and it is adverbial. Both need a verb, and unlike line 013's simile this one gets it: line 016 begins with 流れてゆく "flow on, drift by". So the two lines read ふわり（と）、優しい風のように流れてゆく日々よ "O days that drift softly by, like a gentle breeze". ふわり usually takes と (ふわりと), and dropping it is common in lyrics. Because it stands right before 優しい風, it also colours the breeze, but its grammatical partner is the verb. Line 014 ended its pair with "Ah", and this line starts the second pair. Nothing needs correcting.

## Reading

## How it fits together

`[ふわり（と）] [[優しい 風] の ように] → [流れ て ゆく] 日々 よ (line 016)`

Two adverbials stack up before one verb: ふわり says how the movement looks (soft and airy), and 優しい風のように says what it is like (a gentle breeze). The verb is 流れてゆく on line 016, which in turn describes 日々 "days".

## Translation

- Literal: "softly, like a gentle wind"
- Natural: "Drifting softly, like a gentle breeze…" (with line 016: "O days that drift softly by, like a gentle breeze… ah")

## Notes

- **The second pair is complete.** The section has two pairs of lines. In the first, both adverbials were left without a verb (星のように on line 013, 深く on line 014). In this pair the simile gets its verb at once: ふわり優しい風のように流れてゆく日々よ. After two lines of open images, the section settles into one clear phrase, and its subject is time.
- **ふわり.** A mimetic word for a light, soft, airy movement, such as floating up, drifting or landing gently: 風船がふわりと浮かぶ "a balloon floats up", 髪がふわりと揺れる "hair stirs softly". ふわふわ, the doubled form, describes an ongoing state ("fluffy, floating"), and ふんわり is "soft, fluffy". The single ふわり is one soft motion. Mimetic adverbs usually take と, but lyrics often drop it (ふわり浮かぶ).
- **What ふわり modifies.** An adverb modifies a predicate, so ふわり pairs most naturally with 流れてゆく: ふわりと流れてゆく "drift softly by". 優しい is a predicate too (an adjective), so ふわり優しい "airily gentle" is also possible as a poetic pairing. In listening the two merge: the breeze is soft, and so is the passing of the days.
- **優しい and 易しい.** Both are read やさしい. 優しい is "gentle, kind", and 易しい is "easy". Said of things, 優しい means mild or soft: 優しい風 "a gentle breeze", 優しい光 "soft light", 優しい味 "a mild flavour".
- **A gentle 風のように.** Wind similes often mean speed: 風のように走る "run like the wind". With ふわり and 優しい before it, this one is about lightness and softness instead. The days pass the way a breeze does, gently and without being held.
- **The two similes of this section.** 星のように (line 013) and 風のように (this line) open the two pairs, one from the sky and one from the air. Both pairs end with "Ah". Only the second simile reaches a verb, and that verb is about the days flowing by. Line 013's simile can also be heard as reaching ahead to it.
