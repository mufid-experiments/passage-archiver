# Line 012 · 日が昇る場所

## Context

- Line 011 (previous): それは「奇跡」じゃない　透き通った世界でふたり
- **Line 012 (explained here): 日が昇る場所**
- Line 013 (next): 夜明け光る星のように

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: noun-ending phrase (体言止め), a relative clause (日が昇る) on 場所; it closes the chorus.

The line is a grammatical noun phrase, not a sentence. 日が昇る "the sun rises" is a relative clause, and it modifies 場所 "place": "the place where the sun rises". Japanese needs no word for "where", because the head noun 場所 is simply the place in which the clause happens. There is no predicate, so the line ends on a noun (体言止め), like line 011, which ended on ふたり. The song does not say how the two noun phrases connect. The clear world of line 011 may be the place where the sun rises, or it may be where the two of them are, or where they are going. The chorus ends on the image and leaves it open. In the second chorus, line 027 drops ふたり, and then 透き通った世界で attaches straight to this line's verb: 透き通った世界で日が昇る場所 "the place where the sun rises in a clear world". Line 013 starts a new section, which also opens at daybreak. Nothing needs correcting.

## Reading

- Kana: ひ が のぼる ばしょ
- Romaji: hi ga noboru basho

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 日 | ひ (hi) | noun | the sun (also "day"); here the sun |
| 2 | が | が (ga) | case particle (格助詞), subject | marks 日 as the subject inside the relative clause; の could replace it (日の昇る場所) |
| 3 | 昇る | のぼる (noboru) | godan verb 昇る, attributive form (連体形) | to rise, to go up into the sky; the clause 日が昇る modifies 場所 |
| 4 | 場所 | ばしょ (basho) | noun | place; the head of the phrase (体言止め) |

## How it fits together

`(line 011: …透き通った世界でふたり →) [[日 が 昇る] 場所]`

日が昇る is a complete little clause, "the sun rises", and it describes 場所. The whole line is one noun phrase. Grammatically it stands beside line 011's noun phrase and does not depend on it. The link between the two is one of images, not of grammar.

## Translation

- Literal: "the place where the sun rises"
- Natural: "the place where the sun comes up" (with line 011: "It isn't 'a miracle': it's the two of us, in a world grown clear: the place where the sun rises.")

## Notes

- **How the chorus ends.** Lines 009–011 each deny a label (「魔法」, 「最後」, 「奇跡」) and say what is true instead. This line adds no denial. It gives a place. It can be heard as what the clear world is, as where the two of them stand, or as the answer to all three denials: what "it" is, in the end, is somewhere the sun rises. The song leaves these together instead of choosing one.
- **A beginning after 「最後」.** Sunrise is where a day begins, so the chorus ends on a start, after line 010 has denied that this is 「最後」 "the end". The next section opens at daybreak too, 夜明け光る星のように "like a star shining at dawn" (line 013), so the image runs across the break.
- **日 meaning "sun".** 日 is both "day" and "the sun": 日が昇る "the sun rises", 日が沈む "the sun sets", 日の出 "sunrise", 日差し "sunlight". 太陽 is the more technical word for the sun, and the plain 日 is the everyday choice in phrases like this.
- **昇る, 上る, 登る.** All three are read のぼる. 昇る is for rising into the sky: the sun, smoke, a rank (昇進 "promotion"). 上る is general "go up" (坂を上る "go up a slope"), and 登る is "climb", usually with effort (山に登る "climb a mountain"). 日が昇る is the standard spelling for sunrise.
- **が or の in a relative clause.** Inside a clause that modifies a noun, the subject can take の instead of が: 日が昇る場所 or 日の昇る場所, 背が高い人 or 背の高い人 "a tall person". The の version is a little more literary. The song uses が.
- **Where the sun rises.** "The place where the sun rises" is also the east, and an old way of naming Japan: 日本 means "origin of the sun", and a famous letter sent from Japan to the Chinese court in 607 opens with 日出づる処の天子 "the Son of Heaven where the sun rises". With the ancient word まほろ on line 008, a listener may hear that echo here. The song does not press it.
- **The line returns.** 日が昇る場所 closes every chorus: lines 012, 028 and 032. At line 032 it is the last line of the song, so the song ends on 場所, a place.
