# Line 003 · 触れられないものばかり

## Context

- Line 002 (previous): だって人は弱いから
- **Line 003 (explained here): 触れられないものばかり**
- Line 004 (next): 求めてしまうのは　なぜ

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — fragment: noun phrase + ばかり; it is the object of 求めて on line 004, with ばかり standing in for を.

The line is grammatical but is not a sentence. 触れられない ("that can't be touched") modifies もの ("things"), and ばかり ("nothing but") follows. There is no verb. The phrase is the object of 求めてしまう on line 004, and line 004 also ends the sentence: 触れられないものばかり求めてしまうのはなぜ "why is it that (we) end up seeking nothing but things (we) can't touch?". No を is needed, because ばかり can take the place of を (and of が) on its own. ばかりを is also correct, but the bare ばかり is the more common choice. 触れられる is the standard potential form of 触れる. Casual speech often drops the ら (触れれる), but the song uses the standard form. Line 002 does not continue into this line. It ended its own sentence on から, and this line starts a new one. Nothing needs correcting.

## Reading

- Kana: ふれられない もの ばかり
- Romaji: furerarenai mono bakari

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | 触れ | ふれ (fure) | ichidan verb 触れる, irrealis form (未然形) | to touch, to come into contact with; takes に for what is touched (〜に触れる) |
| 2 | られ | られ (rare) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) られる, potential, irrealis form (未然形) before ない | "can": 触れられる = "can touch". The same ending can also be passive ("be touched"), but the parallel with 見えない on line 001 makes it potential |
| 3 | ない | ない (nai) | auxiliary verb (助動詞) ない, negative, attributive form (連体形) | "not": 触れられない = "that can't be touched"; the clause modifies もの |
| 4 | もの | もの (mono) | noun | thing(s): 触れられないもの = "what can't be touched, the intangible" |
| 5 | ばかり | ばかり (bakari) | adverbial particle (副助詞) | "nothing but, only": limits the object to one kind of thing, usually with a sense of imbalance; here it stands in for を, and the verb follows on line 004 |

## How it fits together

`[[[触れ られ ない] もの] ばかり] → 求めて しまう の は　なぜ (line 004)`

触れられない is a relative clause on もの. Its gap is the に-object of 触れる: the things one can't touch. ばかり narrows the phrase to "nothing but those things". The verb it belongs to, 求めて, opens line 004, and のは　なぜ then turns the whole clause into a question.

## Translation

- Literal: "only things that cannot be touched"
- Natural: "nothing but things we can't touch" (with line 004: "Why is it that we end up seeking nothing but things we can't touch?")

## Notes

- **The parallel with line 001.** 見えないもの "what can't be seen" (line 001) and 触れられないもの "what can't be touched" (this line) have the same shape: a negative "can't" clause on the noun もの. Sight first, then touch. Line 001 states something people do (believe in the invisible), and lines 003–004 ask a question about the intangible: why it is all we seem to reach for.
- **Link to line 002.** Line 002 (だって人は弱いから) ended its sentence on から, giving the reason for line 001. Heard straight after it, this line sounds like it might be the effect: "people are weak, so they reach for nothing but…". Line 004's のは　なぜ then makes clear it is a new sentence and a question.
- **ばかり after a noun.** ばかり means "nothing but, only, all": 肉ばかり食べる "eat nothing but meat", 失敗ばかりだ "it's nothing but failures". It can replace が and を, or stand in front of them (ばかりを, ばかりが). Unlike neutral だけ "only", ばかり usually suggests too much of one thing, which fits the rueful てしまう on line 004. The song's other limiting particle is だけ, on line 020 (まほろのうたごえだけ　響く "only the singing voice resounds"), where the sense is neutral or even positive.
- **触れる and 触る.** Both are written with 触, and the okurigana decides the reading: 触れる is ふれる, 触る is さわる. さわる is plainer and more physical ("touch, handle": 触らないで "don't touch"). ふれる is lighter and wider ("come into contact with"), and it is used figuratively: 心に触れる "touch the heart", 話題に触れる "touch on a topic". 触れられないもの can therefore be what is physically out of reach or, more broadly, what can't be grasped at all.
- **Potential, passive, and the dropped ら.** For an ichidan verb like 触れる, the ending られる serves as potential, passive and honorific alike. In this line only the potential reading makes sense: 触れられないもの "things one can't touch" (passive would be "things that aren't touched"). In casual speech, the potential of ichidan verbs often drops the ら (ら抜き言葉): 見れる, 来れる, and 触れれる. That form is common but still counted as non-standard. The song uses the standard 触れられる.
