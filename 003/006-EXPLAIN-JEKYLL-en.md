### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="006" %}

The line is grammatical. <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を (“what is real”, as object) and <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってしまったら (“if (we) were to find out”) make the second clause of the hypothesis that もしも opened on line 005. たら is the conditional ending もしも was waiting for, and it covers both clauses: “if that turned out not to be the truth, and we came to know what’s real…”. The subject is unstated, most naturally “we”. <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> is used here as a bare noun, “the real, the truth”. Everyday speech would more often say <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>のことを<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る, but bare <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> is established (<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うと “to tell the truth”) and suits a lyric. What happens “if” is not given as an ordinary main clause. The next verb, <ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく on line 007, can be heard as the result, but it can also describe the noun on line 008. The Notes cover both readings. Restored prose with line 005: もしもそれが<ruby>真<rt>しん</rt>実<rt>じつ</rt></ruby>ではなくて、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>（のこと）を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってしまったら… Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="006" %}

もしも (line 005) opens the hypothesis and たら closes it. In between are two clauses, the first ending in the te-form (<ruby>真<rt>しん</rt>実<rt>じつ</rt></ruby>じゃなくて) and the second in たら. The result clause that たら leads into is the open question for lines 007–008.

## Notes

### Completes the “if” of line 005

The whole hypothesis is もしもそれが<ruby>真<rt>しん</rt>実<rt>じつ</rt></ruby>じゃなくて、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってしまったら “if that wasn’t the truth, and we came to know what’s real”. もしも, at the start of line 005, is answered by たら here, at the end of line 006.

### What happens “if”?

No plain “then…” clause follows. Line 007’s <ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby>と<ruby>影<rt>かげ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく is the first verb after たら, and it can be heard as the result: “(we) would go on being wrapped in light and shadow”, knowing the truth bringing both light and darkness. But the same form, <ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく, can also describe a noun, and line 008 supplies one: <ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby>と<ruby>影<rt>かげ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく まほろのうたごえ “the singing voice of mahoro, wrapped in light and shadow”. Heard that way, the “if” is followed by an image instead of a statement. Either way, the song answers the hypothesis with a picture.

### 〜てしまったら

しまう adds that something is done and cannot be taken back: <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってしまったら “once (we) have found out”. Plain <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ったら would be the same “if” without that weight. The しまう echoes line 004 (<ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めてしまう): both times, something happens to people that they can’t easily stop or undo.

### <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る, “find out”, and <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている, “know”

<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る is an event, the moment of coming to know. The state of knowing is <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている, or casually <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってる, which is how line 024 has it: <ruby>僕<rt>ぼく</rt></ruby>たちは<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってるはず “we must know”. The negative is irregular: “I don’t know” is <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない, not <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていない.

### <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> as a noun

<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> usually modifies (<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> “a true story”, <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>に “really”) or is a predicate (<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>だ “it’s true”). As a bare noun object, <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る is less usual than <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>のことを<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る “learn the truth”, but it is established in fixed phrases (<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>えば “if I’m honest”). It makes “the real” sound like a thing in itself, which suits a song that sets it against <ruby>真<rt>しん</rt>実<rt>じつ</rt></ruby> on line 005. Line 018 uses <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> the same way, as a subject: <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう “what’s real is surely waiting”.

### たら, なら, ば

たら is the conditional for one event that leads to a result: “if/when X happens”. なら is “if it is the case that…”, and ば leans toward general conditions (<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になれば<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かくなる “when spring comes it gets warm”). たら fits a moment of discovery: “once we find out”.
