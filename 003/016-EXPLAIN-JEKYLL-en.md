### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="016" %}

The line is grammatical. <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく “that flow on” is a relative clause on <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby> “days”. The verb is the one line 015’s adverbials were waiting for, so the two lines make one phrase: ふわり<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby> “the days that drift softly by, like a gentle breeze”. The particle よ after the noun is the vocative よ: it calls out to the days, “O days…!”, as in <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>よ “O friend”. A phrase addressed to someone or something needs no predicate, so the line is complete as an exclamation. ゆく is the literary form of いく, here as the subsidiary verb in 〜てゆく “go on ~ing, ~ away”. “Ah”, after a full-width space, is a sung sound outside the grammar, and it closes the second pair of the section as it closed the first on line 014. Line 017 starts a new section. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="016" %}

ふわり and <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように (line 015) both modify <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく. <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく modifies <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>, and よ turns the whole noun phrase into an address. The result is an exclamation to the days, with no predicate needed.

### In Tae Kim’s guide

{% include explain/taekim.html n="016" %}

ゆく is an older, more literary いく. 4.5.5 uses いく, and the grammar is the same: an action that carries on in time. The よ that calls out to the days, “O days”, is not in the book. Its よ, in 3.12.4 (「よ」 sentence ending), is the one that ends a sentence to tell the listener something new.

## Notes

### Completes line 015

Of the section’s two pairs, this is the one that finishes its grammar (lines 013–014 left their adverbials open), and what it arrives at is time: the days.

### Vocative よ

After a noun, よ calls out to what the noun names: <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>よ “O friend”, <ruby>神<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>よ “O God”, <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>よ、<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>い “Spring, come!”. It is solemn and literary, more at home in songs and poems than in conversation. It is a different use from sentence-final よ (<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くよ “I’m going, OK?”), which presses a statement on a listener. Here the singer speaks to the days themselves, as if they could hear.

### 〜てゆく and 〜ていく

ゆく is the older, more literary form of いく, and in songs it is often preferred for its softer sound. Both are standard. The song uses both: <ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>まれていく (lines 007, 019) and <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく here. 〜てゆく puts the movement on a line leading away from the present: the days do not just pass, they pass on and are gone.

### <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby> and the mark 々

々 repeats the kanji before it: <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>日<rt>び</rt></ruby> → <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>, <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt>々<rt>びと</rt></ruby> (ひとびと) “people”, <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt>々<rt>どき</rt></ruby> (ときどき) “sometimes”, <ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt>々<rt>いろ</rt></ruby> (いろいろ) “various”. The repeated syllable often takes rendaku, ひ → び, ひと → びと. 々 is not a kanji but a repetition mark (<ruby>踊<rt>おど</rt></ruby>り<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>). <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby> means “the days, day after day, daily life”: <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby> “busy days”.

### Time that flows

<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる is the everyday verb for time passing: <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “time flows”, <ruby>月<rt>つき</rt>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れる “the months and days go by”. The image here is gentle: the days pass like a breeze, softly and beyond holding. Earlier the chorus spoke of getting past time (<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>えて, line 010). Here the song simply lets time pass.
