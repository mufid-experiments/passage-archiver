# Line 018 · 本当が待っているんだろう

## Context

- Line 017 (previous): ひとつひとつ辿った先には
- **Line 018 (explained here): 本当が待っているんだろう**
- Line 019 (next): 光と影に包まれていく

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 017: subject 本当が + 待っている + the conjectural ending んだろう.

The line is grammatical and closes the sentence that line 017 opened: ひとつひとつ辿った先には本当が待っているんだろう "at the end of what we follow, one by one, what's real must be waiting". 本当 "the real, the truth" is the subject, marked by が. 待っている is "be waiting", the te-form of 待つ + いる for an ongoing state. The ending んだろう is the explanatory の, shortened to ん, + だろう "probably, I suppose". It presents the line as the speaker's inference: "it must be that…". Without か, the line reads as a guess the speaker is fairly sure of, not as a question. Saying that the truth "waits" is personification, like 明るい未来が待っている "a bright future awaits". As on line 006, 本当 is used as a bare noun. Line 019 starts a new phrase. Nothing needs correcting.

## Reading

- Kana: ほんとう が まって いる ん だろう
- Romaji: hontō ga matte iru n darō

## How it fits together

`(line 017: [ひとつひとつ辿った先] には →) [本当 が] [[待っ て いる] ん だろう]`

Line 017's 〜先には sets the place and the topic. 本当が待っている says what is there, and んだろう wraps the statement as the speaker's inference. The sentence ends here.

## Translation

- Literal: "the real is (probably) waiting, I suppose"
- Natural: "what's real must be waiting there." (with line 017: "At the end of the road we follow step by step, what's real must be waiting for us.")

## Notes

- **Completes line 017.** The whole sentence is ひとつひとつ辿った先には、本当が待っているんだろう. It has the usual frame for saying what is to be found somewhere: [place]には[thing]がある / いる / 待っている.
- **本当, then and now.** On line 006, 本当 was something one might find out with dread: もしも…本当を知ってしまったら "what if… we came to know what's real". Here the same word is waiting at the end of a road the speaker chooses to walk. In both lines 本当 stands as a noun on its own, "the real", not 本当のこと. What was feared in the first section is something to walk toward in this one.
- **〜が待っている.** 待つ is used of people waiting, and figuratively of what lies ahead of someone: 明るい未来が待っている "a bright future awaits", 試練が待っている "trials lie ahead". 待っている, with いる, describes a state: the truth is already there and waiting, not something that will start waiting later.
- **んだろう and だろう.** 待っているだろう on its own is a plain prediction: "will probably be waiting". With ん, 待っているんだろう presents a conclusion the speaker has reached, "it must be that it's waiting", as if drawn from the steps taken so far. Spoken with a rising tone, it can be a question ("is it waiting, I wonder?"), but a written line without か is usually read as the falling, fairly confident guess. The casual form is んだろ, and the polite one is んでしょう.
- **Guess, then knowledge.** だろう is a guess, and the speaker hasn't arrived yet. On line 024, the song moves to 僕たちは知ってるはず "we must know", where はず expresses an expectation with reasons behind it. The second half of the song goes from "surely" to "we know".
- **Link to line 019.** Line 019 (光と影に包まれていく) repeats line 007 word for word. Line 007 followed an open "if", so it could be heard as that clause's result. Here the sentence before it is closed, so line 019 starts fresh and is most naturally heard as a description of the voice on line 020: 光と影に包まれていくまほろのうたごえ "the singing voice of mahoro, wrapped in light and shadow".
