### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="012" %}

The line is a grammatical noun phrase, not a sentence. <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る “the sun rises” is a relative clause, and it modifies <ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> “place”: “the place where the sun rises”. Japanese needs no word for “where”, because the head noun <ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> is simply the place in which the clause happens. There is no predicate, so the line ends on a noun (<ruby>体<rt>たい</rt>言<rt>げん</rt>止<rt>ど</rt></ruby>め), like line 011, which ended on ふたり. The song does not say how the two noun phrases connect. The clear world of line 011 may be the place where the sun rises, or it may be where the two of them are, or where they are going. The chorus ends on the image and leaves it open. In the second chorus, line 027 drops ふたり, and then <ruby>透<rt>す</rt></ruby>き<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>った<ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>で attaches straight to this line’s verb: <ruby>透<rt>す</rt></ruby>き<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>った<ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>で<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> “the place where the sun rises in a clear world”. Line 013 starts a new section, which also opens at daybreak. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="012" %}

<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る is a complete little clause, “the sun rises”, and it describes <ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>. The whole line is one noun phrase. Grammatically it stands beside line 011’s noun phrase and does not depend on it. The link between the two is one of images, not of grammar.

## Notes

### How the chorus ends

Lines 009–011 each deny a label (「<ruby>魔<rt>ま</rt>法<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>」, 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」, 「<ruby>奇<rt>き</rt>跡<rt>せき</rt></ruby>」) and say what is true instead. This line adds no denial. It gives a place. It can be heard as what the clear world is, as where the two of them stand, or as the answer to all three denials: what “it” is, in the end, is somewhere the sun rises. The song leaves these together instead of choosing one.

### A beginning after 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」

Sunrise is where a day begins, so the chorus ends on a start, after line 010 has denied that this is 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」 “the end”. The next section opens at daybreak too, <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のように “like a star shining at dawn” (line 013), so the image runs across the break.

### <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby> meaning “sun”

<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby> is both “day” and “the sun”: <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る “the sun rises”, <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>沈<rt>しず</rt></ruby>む “the sun sets”, <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby> “sunrise”, <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>差<rt>ざ</rt></ruby>し “sunlight”. <ruby>太<rt>たい</rt>陽<rt>よう</rt></ruby> is the more technical word for the sun, and the plain <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby> is the everyday choice in phrases like this.

### <ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>上<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る

All three are read のぼる. <ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る is for rising into the sky: the sun, smoke, a rank (<ruby>昇<rt>しょう</rt>進<rt>しん</rt></ruby> “promotion”). <ruby>上<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る is general “go up” (<ruby>坂<rt>さか</rt></ruby>を<ruby>上<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る “go up a slope”), and <ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る is “climb”, usually with effort (<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る “climb a mountain”). <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る is the standard spelling for sunrise.

### が or の in a relative clause

Inside a clause that modifies a noun, the subject can take の instead of が: <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> or <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> or <ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> “a tall person”. The の version is a little more literary. The song uses が.

### Where the sun rises

“The place where the sun rises” is also the east, and an old way of naming Japan: <ruby>日<rt>に</rt>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby> means “origin of the sun”, and a famous letter sent from Japan to the Chinese court in 607 opens with <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>出<rt>い</rt></ruby>づる<ruby>処<rt>ところ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>天<rt>てん</rt>子<rt>し</rt></ruby> “the Son of Heaven where the sun rises”. With the ancient word まほろ on line 008, a listener may hear that echo here. The song does not press it.

### The line returns

<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> closes every chorus: lines 012, 028 and 032. At line 032 it is the last line of the song, so the song ends on <ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>, a place.
