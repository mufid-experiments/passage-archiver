# Line 007 · 光と影に包まれていく

## Context

- Line 006 (previous): 本当を知ってしまったら
- **Line 007 (explained here): 光と影に包まれていく**
- Line 008 (next): まほろのうたごえ

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — passive 包まれる + 〜ていく; the plain form 包まれていく can end a clause (as the result of the "if" on lines 005–006) or modify the noun on line 008, and the line allows both.

The line is grammatical. 光と影に ("by light and shadow") gives what does the wrapping, 包まれる is the passive of 包む ("wrap"), and 〜ていく adds "go on, more and more". The question is what the verb belongs to. In Japanese the form that ends a sentence and the form that modifies a noun are the same for verbs, so 包まれていく can be either. (a) It can end the hypothesis of lines 005–006: "if we came to know what's real, (we) would go on being wrapped in light and shadow." (b) It can describe line 008's noun: 光と影に包まれていくまほろのうたごえ "the singing voice of mahoro, wrapped in light and shadow". In (a), the subject is unstated (we, or everything around us). In (b), it is the singing voice itself. The same line returns at line 019, where the noun after it has a verb of its own (まほろのうたごえだけ　響く), and there reading (b) is the natural one. Here, straight after たら, the listener expects a result, and (a) is heard first. Neither reading needs any correction.

## Reading

- Kana: ひかり と かげ に つつまれて いく
- Romaji: hikari to kage ni tsutsumarete iku

## Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |

| 1 | 光 | ひかり (hikari) | noun | light |
| 2 | と | と (to) | case particle (格助詞), parallel (並立) | "and": joins 光 and 影 in a closed pair, "light and shadow" |

## How it fits together

`[[光 と 影] に] [包ま れ て いく]`
`(a) (line 006: …知ってしまったら →) 光と影に包まれていく。`
`(b) [光と影に包まれていく] → まほろのうたごえ (line 008)`

光と影 is one noun phrase, and に marks it as the agent of the passive. 包まれていく is the predicate. If it is the end of a clause, it gives the result of the "if" (a). If it modifies the next noun, it describes the singing voice of line 008 (b).

## Translation

- Literal: "go on being wrapped in light and shadow"
- Natural: (a) "…we would be wrapped, more and more, in light and shadow"; (b) with line 008: "the singing voice of mahoro, enfolded in light and shadow"

## Notes

- **Two ways to hear the line.** Japanese verbs have one form for ending a sentence and for modifying a noun, so a lyric line ending in a verb can lead into the next noun or stop before it. Here the line follows a conditional (知ってしまったら, line 006), which makes it sound like the result, and it precedes a noun (まほろのうたごえ, line 008), which makes it sound like a description. The song gives no punctuation to decide. At line 019 the same words lead into まほろのうたごえだけ　響く "only the singing voice resounds", which has its own verb, and there they are most naturally a description of the voice.
- **〜に包まれる.** 包む is "wrap" (a present, a parcel), and its passive 包まれる is often used for being surrounded completely: 炎に包まれる "be engulfed in flames", 謎に包まれる "be shrouded in mystery", 拍手に包まれる "be surrounded by applause", 光に包まれる "be bathed in light". The agent is marked with に. The line takes the common image 光に包まれる and adds shadow to it.
- **光と影.** Light and shadow come as a pair: where there is light there is shadow. 光と影 is also an everyday phrase for "the bright and dark sides" of something (成功の光と影 "the bright and dark sides of success"). After lines 005–006, the pair can be heard as what the truth brings: some light, and some darkness too.
- **と for a closed pair.** と joins nouns into a complete list, "light and shadow", and nothing else. や would make the list open, "light, shadow and so on". The pair 光と影 comes back unchanged on line 019.
- **〜ていく.** The subsidiary verb いく puts a change on a line running forward from now: 暗くなっていく "go on getting darker", 消えていく "fade away". 包まれていく is therefore not a single moment but an enveloping that grows. The song writes this いく in kana, as is usual for subsidiary verbs. On line 016 it uses the literary variant ゆく (流れてゆく). The two are the same word.
- **Link to line 008.** Line 008 (まほろのうたごえ) is a bare noun phrase. In reading (b) it is the head that this line describes, and the two lines make one noun phrase. In reading (a) it stands alone after the result clause, as an image (see line 008).
