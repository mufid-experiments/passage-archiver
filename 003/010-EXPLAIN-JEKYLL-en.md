### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="010" %}

The line is grammatical and has the same two-part shape as line 009. それは「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」じゃない “it isn’t ‘the end’” is a complete sentence: topic それは, noun predicate <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby> in brackets, negated with the spoken じゃない. After the full-width space, <ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>えて “getting past time” is a te-form clause with nothing after it. It gives the answer to the denial: not the end, because it carries on past time. The subject of <ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>えて is unstated. It can be “we”, like the sharers of line 009, or それ itself, the thing that does not end. <ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える is not a fixed phrase (the usual “transcend time” is <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>超<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える), but it is clear, and it treats time as an obstacle to be climbed over. The line returns unchanged at lines 026 and 030. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="010" %}

As on line 009, the first half denies a label and the second half says what is true instead. The te-form <ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>えて does not end anything. It stands parallel to line 009’s <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>って, and the chorus runs on.

## Notes

### Second of three

Lines 009–011 have the same frame: それは「X」じゃない + what it is instead. 「<ruby>魔<rt>ま</rt>法<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>」 (009) was something that would come from outside, and 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」 here is something that ends. Line 011’s 「<ruby>奇<rt>き</rt>跡<rt>せき</rt></ruby>」 “a miracle” is like magic again: something that happens to people, not something they do. Each time, the answer puts the cause in people instead: feelings shared, time got through, two people together.

### 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」

<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby> is “the last, the end”: <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby> “the last day”, <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>まで “to the end”, これが<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby> “this is the last time”. Turning it down says that whatever “it” is, it is not over. The chorus then ends on a beginning, <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> “the place where the sun rises” (line 012). By ear, さいご could also be <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>期<rt>ご</rt></ruby>, “one’s last moments, the end of a life”. The written <ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby> shows the general sense, “the end”.

### <ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える

<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える usually takes an obstacle: <ruby>困<rt>こん</rt>難<rt>なん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える “overcome hardship”, <ruby>壁<rt>かべ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える “get over a wall”, <ruby>悲<rt>かな</rt></ruby>しみを<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える “get over grief”. The usual phrase for “transcend time” is <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>超<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える, with plain <ruby>超<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える “go beyond”. <ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える makes time something to be climbed over with effort, such as a long wait, time apart, or the passing years that might bring an end. That fits the denial of 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」 just before it.

### Who gets past time?

The te-form clause has no stated subject. Read like line 009, where <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う needs more than one person, it is “we”, the two of line 011. It can also be それ itself, which outlasts time. Line 021 points that way: それは<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ　<ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ “it links to the future, links to the past”.

### <ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby> and <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>

Both mean “time”. <ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby> is the everyday word and also covers measurable time (<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>がない “there’s no time”). <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby> (とき) is softer and more poetic, and it is the usual choice in phrases like <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>超<rt>こ</rt></ruby>える. The song uses the everyday word, which keeps the line plain.

### Link to line 011

Line 011 (それは「<ruby>奇<rt>き</rt>跡<rt>せき</rt></ruby>」じゃない　<ruby>透<rt>す</rt></ruby>き<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>った<ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>でふたり) completes the three. Its second half is not a te-form verb but ends on a noun, ふたり “the two of us”, and line 012 then closes the chorus.
