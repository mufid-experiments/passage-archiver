### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="014" %}

The line is not a sentence, and its grammar is left unfinished on purpose. <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby> “the pale-blue moon” is a noun phrase with no particle after it, so the line does not say whether the moon is a subject, a place or something else. <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く is the continuative form (<ruby>連<rt>れん</rt>用<rt>よう</rt>形<rt>けい</rt></ruby>) of <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>い “deep”. It is used as an adverb, “deeply, deep”, and an adverb needs a verb or adjective to modify. None follows: the line ends on “Ah”, which is a sung sound, not a word in the sentence. A listener fills the gap, and the likely fillings are in the Notes. Line 013’s simile (<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のように “like a star”) was also left without a verb, so lines 013–014 together make a pair of images rather than a statement. The next pair, lines 015–016, does finish its pattern (<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく). <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い is a literary spelling of あおい, usually written <ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い. “Ah” is written in Latin letters and preceded by a half-width space. Line 016 has a full-width space before its “Ah”, which is a typing difference with no meaning. Nothing here is an error to be corrected.

## Notes

### What <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く belongs to

The 〜く form of an adjective can modify a verb (<ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>る “sleep deeply”) or link two clauses (<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>は<ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く、<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い “the night is deep, the moon pale”). Here neither follows, so the listener completes the line, most plausibly in one of three ways. (1) With an unsaid verb: <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby>（が）<ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く（<ruby>沈<rt>しず</rt></ruby>む） “the pale moon (sinks) deep”, as it does near dawn. (2) As “deep in”, noun + <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く, a common pattern: <ruby>森<rt>もり</rt>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く “deep in the forest”, <ruby>地<rt>ち</rt>中<rt>ちゅう</rt>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く “deep underground”, <ruby>胸<rt>むね</rt></ruby>の<ruby>奥<rt>おく</rt>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く “deep in one’s heart”. Then the phrase is something like “deep in the pale-blue moon(light)”. (3) As a modifier moved after its noun, from <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く<ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby> “a deeply blue moon”. The song does not choose.

### The first pair, 013–014

Both lines end on an adverbial that never reaches its verb: ように (line 013) and <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く (this line). The star and the moon are set side by side at dawn, building a mood rather than a statement, and “Ah” closes the pair. The second pair is grammatically complete: ふわり<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように / <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>よ “O days that flow on like a soft, gentle breeze” (lines 015–016).

### <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby> and <ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>

<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い is the standard spelling of あおい. <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby> is not on the Jōyō list (it is used in names, such as <ruby>蒼<rt>そう</rt></ruby> そう/あおい), so <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い is a literary choice. It suggests a deeper, darker or greyer blue, like the deep sky or sea, and also pallor: <ruby>蒼<rt>そう</rt>白<rt>はく</rt></ruby> (そうはく) “deathly pale”. A <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby> is a pale, cool-coloured moon, the way the moon looks in a sky that is turning blue at dawn.

### The moon at dawn

A moon still in the sky at daybreak is a classical image in Japanese poetry, <ruby>有<rt>あり</rt>明<rt>あけ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby> (ありあけのつき) “the moon at dawn”, often tied to waiting and to parting. The song doesn’t use the term, but with <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け on line 013 the picture is the same: the night is ending and the moon has not yet gone.

### “Ah”

Many J-pop lyrics write sung sounds and English words in Latin letters. “Ah” is the singer’s breath of feeling, and the Japanese equivalent would be ああ, an interjection (<ruby>感<rt>かん</rt>動<rt>どう</rt>詞<rt>し</rt></ruby>). It has no grammatical role. It marks the end of each pair in this section, here and on line 016 (<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>よ　Ah).

### Link to line 015

Line 015 (ふわり<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように) starts the second pair with another simile, this time a soft breeze, and line 016 gives it its verb.
