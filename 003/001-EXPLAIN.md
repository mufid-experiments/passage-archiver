# Line 001 · 見えないものを信じ合う / だって人は弱いから

This window has only two lines (start of the passage), so both lines are explained.

## Context

## Line 001 · 見えないものを信じ合う

### Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — complete sentence: object + compound verb 信じ合う in the terminal form; subject unstated.

The line is grammatical and complete. 見えないもの ("things that can't be seen") is the object, marked by を, and 信じ合う ("believe together, believe in each other") is the predicate in the plain non-past, which here states something people generally do rather than a single event. The subject is not stated. Line 002 talks about 人 "people" in general, so the natural subject is people as a whole, the singer included ("we"). The song's only personal pronoun, 僕たち "we", does not appear until line 024. 信じ合う usually means "trust each other" and has no object. Here it takes one, and the meaning becomes "believe in something together", like 想いを分け合う "share feelings" on line 009. That is a slightly poetic use, not an error. The register is plain and casual. Nothing needs correcting.

### Reading

- Kana: みえない もの を しんじあう
- Romaji: mienai mono o shinjiau

### How it fits together

`[[[見え ない] もの] を] [信じ 合う]`

見えない modifies もの, and the noun phrase is the object of 信じ合う. The subject, people in general or "we", is left unsaid. The sentence ends here, and line 002 adds the reason.

### Translation

- Literal: "(we) mutually believe in things that cannot be seen"
- Natural: "We believe, together, in things we cannot see."

## Line 002 · だって人は弱いから

### Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — colloquial: a reason clause (だって〜から) standing as its own sentence, after the statement it explains.

The line is grammatical and typical of speech. だって opens a justification ("because, after all"), and から closes the reason. There is no main clause after から. The statement it explains came first, on line 001, so the two lines work as 見えないものを信じ合う。だって人は弱いから。 "We believe in what we can't see. Because people are weak." Ending a sentence on から is ordinary in speech when the reason comes as an afterthought. 人は is a generic topic: the line is a statement about people as such, not about any one person. Prose that puts the reason first would be 人は弱いから、見えないものを信じ合う。 Nothing needs correcting.

### Reading

- Kana: だって ひと は よわい から
- Romaji: datte hito wa yowai kara

### Word by word, particle by particle

| # | Japanese | Reading | Type | Meaning / function |
|---|----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | だって | だって (datte) | conjunction (接続詞), colloquial | "because, after all": opens a justification for what was just said; it looks ahead to から at the end |
| 2 | 人 | ひと (hito) | noun | person; here people in general, human beings |
| 3 | は | は (wa) | binding particle (係助詞), topic | "as for people": marks a generic topic, as in a maxim |
| 4 | 弱い | よわい (yowai) | i-adjective (形容詞) 弱い, terminal form (終止形) | weak |
| 5 | から | から (kara) | conjunctive particle (接続助詞), reason | "because": the reason clause ends the sentence; what it explains is line 001, said first |

### How it fits together

`だって [人 は 弱い] から ← (line 001: 見えないものを信じ合う)`

人は弱い is a complete clause, "people are weak". から turns it into a reason, and だって at the front signals in advance that a reason is coming. The clause it explains is not after it but before it, on line 001.

### Translation

- Literal: "because people are weak"
- Natural: "Because, after all, people are weak." (with line 001: "We believe, together, in things we cannot see, because people are weak, after all.")

## Notes

- **How lines 001 and 002 connect.** Statement, then reason. Line 001 is complete on its own, and line 002 adds why. Prose would usually put the reason first (人は弱いから、見えないものを信じ合う), but the order here is the order of speech: say what you do, then defend it, as in 行かない。だって寒いから。 "I'm not going. It's cold, that's why."
- **〜合う, "each other, together".** After a verb's continuative form, 合う makes the action mutual: 助け合う "help each other", 話し合う "talk it over", 見つめ合う "gaze at each other". Without an object, 信じ合う means "trust each other" (互いに信じ合う). With an object, as here, the action is shared: each person believes in what can't be seen, and each relies on the others believing it too. The same pattern returns in the chorus, 想いを分け合って "sharing our feelings with each other" (lines 009, 025, 029). Both times, what is shared is something no one can hold.
- **見える, not 見られる.** 見える means "be visible, can be seen". The seeing happens by itself, so 見えないもの is "the invisible": trust, love, the future, things no one can see. 見られないもの would be "things one doesn't get to see, isn't allowed to see". The everyday phrase is 目に見えないもの "things invisible to the eye".
- **The parallel with lines 003–004.** 見えないもの "what can't be seen" here is matched by 触れられないもの "what can't be touched" on line 003: sight, then touch. Line 001 says what people do, and lines 003–004 ask why: 触れられないものばかり / 求めてしまうのは　なぜ "why do we end up seeking only what we can't touch?". Line 002's reason, 人は弱いから, sits between the statement and the question. It reads like an answer given before the question is asked, and the song asks it anyway.
- **だって, two different words.** At the start of a sentence, だって is a conjunction: "because, after all; but", used to justify oneself or to object (だって知らなかったんだもん "But I didn't know!"). It is casual and can sound like an excuse or a protest. The formal equivalent is なぜなら〜からだ. After a noun, だって is a different word, a particle meaning "even, too" (子供だって分かる "even a child understands"). Here it is the conjunction, paired with から.
- **人は弱い as a maxim.** 人 here means human beings, and は marks a generic topic, as in sayings like 人は一人では生きられない "people can't live alone". The plain non-past of both lines (信じ合う, 弱い) fits this: they state how things are, not what happened once. The singer is included, but the song only says "we" outright at line 024 (僕たちは知ってるはず).
