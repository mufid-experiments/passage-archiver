### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="013" %}

The line is grammatical as a phrase, but it is not a sentence. <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby> is “a star that shines at dawn”: <ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る is a relative clause on <ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>, and <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け gives the time. Prose would mark that time with に (<ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けに<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>). Dropping it is a poetic compression that makes <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る sound like a single image. A second reading takes <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け as a noun on its own, “daybreak”, followed by “like a shining star”. のように “like” is adverbial and needs a predicate to modify, but this line has none, and line 014 (<ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く Ah) offers only the adverb <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く. The matching pair two lines later is complete: ふわり<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように / <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>よ (lines 015–016), “O days that flow on like a soft, gentle breeze”. This section builds images more than sentences, so the simile may reach forward to <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく on line 016 as well. Line 012 closed the chorus, and this line starts a new section. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="013" %}

<ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る modifies <ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>, and <ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のように makes the whole phrase a comparison: “like a star that shines at dawn”. The comparison is adverbial, so it is waiting for a verb or adjective. Line 014 gives the adverb <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く and no verb, and only line 016’s <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく completes a ように in this section.

## Notes

### A new section, at dawn

The chorus ended on sunrise, <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> (line 012), and this section steps back to the moment just before it, when the night ends and a star still shines. Lines 013–016 come in two pairs, each closed by “Ah”: <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のように / <ruby>蒼<rt>あお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く Ah, then ふわり<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように / <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>よ　Ah. The first pair has the sky (star, moon), and the second has the air and time (breeze, days).

### <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け without に

Some time words take no particle: <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>, <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>. Points in time and events normally take に: <ruby>三<rt>さん</rt>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>に, <ruby>誕<rt>たん</rt>生<rt>じょう</rt>日<rt>び</rt></ruby>に, <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けに. <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けに<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby> is the prose form. Lyrics often drop particles for rhythm and compactness, and here the result reads almost like a compound, “the dawn-shining star”.

### A star at dawn

As the sky grows light, the stars fade. The one that stays longest is usually Venus, the morning star, called <ruby>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けの<ruby>明<rt>みょう</rt>星<rt>じょう</rt></ruby> (あけのみょうじょう) in Japanese. A star still shining at daybreak is the last light of the night and the first sign of the day, and the line recalls that image without naming it.

### 〜のように

Noun + のように means “like ~” and modifies a verb or an adjective: <ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のように<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>る “shine like a star”, <ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る “run like the wind”. Before a noun it becomes のような: <ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のような<ruby>瞳<rt>ひとみ</rt></ruby> “star-like eyes”. The casual equivalents are みたいに and みたいな, and the song uses those too: お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>みたいな<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ “a melody like a fairy tale” (line 022).

### What the simile describes

Line 014 has no verb, only <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>く “deeply”. The second pair shows the full pattern, a simile followed by a verb: <ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく “flow on like the wind” (lines 015–016). By that model, this simile can be heard as reaching ahead to the same days: days that pass like a star at dawn, like a gentle breeze. It can also be heard with line 014 alone, setting a star beside the moon. Lyrics often leave this kind of link open.

### <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け and <ruby>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける

<ruby>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける, written with <ruby>明<rt>めい</rt></ruby> “bright”, means “grow light” and so “come to an end” (of a night, or a period): <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける “day breaks”, literally “the night grows light, the night ends”. The same verb gives <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける “the old year ends, the new year begins”. <ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け is a little more poetic than <ruby>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>方<rt>がた</rt></ruby> (あけがた, “around dawn”) or <ruby>早<rt>そう</rt>朝<rt>ちょう</rt></ruby> (そうちょう, “early morning”).
