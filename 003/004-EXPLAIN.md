# Line 004 · 求めてしまうのは　なぜ

## Context

- Line 003 (previous): 触れられないものばかり
- **Line 004 (explained here): 求めてしまうのは　なぜ**
- Line 005 (next): もしもそれが真実じゃなくて

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — completes the sentence begun on line 003: nominalized clause 〜のは + なぜ, with the ending (だろう, なのか) left unsaid.

The line is grammatical and ends the sentence that line 003 began. 求めてしまう ("end up seeking") takes line 003's 触れられないものばかり as its object. の turns the clause into a noun ("the fact that we end up seeking…"), は makes that fact the topic, and なぜ ("why") is the question asked about it. This is the standard cleft pattern 〜のはなぜ(か) "why is it that ~", as in 空が青いのはなぜ？ "why is the sky blue?". Ending on the bare question word, without だろう or なのか, is ordinary in speech and in titles, and the question is carried by the intonation. The lyric has no question mark. The subject is unstated: people in general, or "we", as on lines 001–002. The full-width space holds なぜ back for a beat, so the question word lands alone. Line 005 starts a new sentence. Restored prose with line 003: 触れられないものばかり求めてしまうのは、なぜ（だろう）？ Nothing needs correcting.

## Reading

- Kana: もとめて しまう の は / なぜ
- Romaji: motomete shimau no wa / naze

## How it fits together

`(line 003: 触れられないもの ばかり →) [[[求め て しまう] の] は]　なぜ（だろう）`

Line 003 supplies the object, and 求めてしまう is its verb. の wraps the whole clause up as a noun, は sets it out as the topic, and なぜ is the question about it: "the fact that we end up seeking only what we can't touch: why?".

## Translation

- Literal: "the fact that (we) end up seeking (nothing but things that can't be touched): why?"
- Natural: "Why is it that we keep reaching for nothing but things we can't touch?"

## Notes

- **Completes line 003.** The whole sentence is 触れられないものばかり求めてしまうのは、なぜ（だろう）. Line 003 on its own sounded like a fragment, and this line gives it a verb and makes it a question.
- **〜のはなぜ, the cleft question.** Plain なぜ求めてしまうのか? asks the same thing, but the cleft puts the fact first and the "why" last: 空が青いのはなぜ "why is the sky blue?", 人が夢を見るのはなぜか "why do people dream?". It can end in several ways: なぜ？, なぜだろう (wondering), なぜなのか (insisting), なぜですか (polite). The bare なぜ is the most open: it could be asked aloud or only to oneself.
- **A question with an answer already given.** Line 002 said 人は弱いから "because people are weak". The song still asks why, so the question is not really a request for information. It reads as wonder, or as reproach aimed at ourselves: we know we are weak, and we keep reaching for what we can't touch anyway.
- **〜てしまう.** The subsidiary verb しまう adds "end up ~ing, do ~ without meaning to", often with regret: つい食べてしまう "I end up eating it anyway". In casual speech it shrinks to 〜ちゃう (求めちゃう). Here it makes the seeking almost involuntary: people do not choose the intangible, they find themselves reaching for it. The same しまう returns on line 006 (知ってしまったら), where it marks something that can't be undone.
- **求める.** 求める is "seek, ask for, want": 助けを求める "ask for help", 答えを求める "look for an answer", 愛を求める "seek love". It is stronger and more formal than 欲しがる "want", and it suggests reaching or searching, which suits something that can't be touched.
- **なぜ, どうして, なんで.** All three mean "why". なぜ is the most neutral and is common in writing. どうして is the everyday spoken word and often sounds more emotional. なんで is casual. なぜ suits a reflective, almost philosophical question about people in general.
- **Link to line 005.** Line 005 (もしもそれが真実じゃなくて) starts a new sentence, a hypothesis that runs on to line 006: "what if it were not the truth…". Its それ "that" points back, most naturally to what lines 001–004 were about: the invisible, intangible things people believe in and reach for.
