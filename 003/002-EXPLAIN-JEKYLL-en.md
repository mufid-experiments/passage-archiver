### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="002" %}

The line is grammatical and typical of speech. だって opens a justification (“because, after all”), and から closes it. There is no main clause after から. The statement being explained came first, on line 001, so the two lines work as <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えないものを<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う。だって<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>いから。 “We believe in what we can’t see. Because people are weak.” Ending a sentence on から when the reason comes as an afterthought is ordinary in speech. <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は is a generic topic, so the line is a statement about people as such, the singer included, and not about any one person. Line 003 begins a new sentence, which ends on line 004. Restored prose with the reason first: <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>いから、<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えないものを<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う。 Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

{% include explain/tree.html n="002" %}

<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い is a complete clause, “people are weak”. から makes it a reason, and だって at the front signals in advance that a reason is coming. What it explains is not after it but before it: the sentence on line 001.

## Notes

### Link to line 001

Line 001 (<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えないものを<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う) is complete on its own, and this line adds the reason for it. Prose would usually put the reason first, but the order here is the order of speech: say what you do, then defend it, as in <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない。だって<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いから。 “I’m not going. It’s cold, that’s why.” The reason is weakness: people need something to hold on to, and they share their belief in it.

### Link to line 003

Line 003 (<ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないものばかり) starts a new sentence, which ends on line 004 with a question: <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないものばかり / <ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めてしまうのは　なぜ “why do we end up seeking only what we can’t touch?”. Heard in order, <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>いから → <ruby>触<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れられないものばかり<ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めてしまう sounds like cause and effect, “people are weak, so they reach for what they can’t touch”, until のは　なぜ turns the clause into a question. The song seems to offer an answer before it asks the question, and then asks it anyway.

### だって, two different words

At the start of a sentence, だって is a conjunction: “because, after all; but”, used to justify oneself or to object (だって<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らなかったんだもん “But I didn’t know!”). It is casual and can sound like an excuse or a protest. It is usually closed by から, もの/もん or んだ. The formal equivalent is なぜなら〜からだ. After a noun, だって is a different word, a particle meaning “even, too” (<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt>供<rt>ども</rt></ruby>だって<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる “even a child understands”). Here it is the conjunction.

### Ending on から

A sentence ending in から leaves the main clause either unsaid or, as here, already said. In speech it is the usual way to add a reason afterwards. 〜からだ, with the copula, sounds more like an explanation given to someone (“it is because…”). The bare から is softer and more personal.

### <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い as a maxim

<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> here means human beings, and は marks a generic topic, as in sayings like <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>では<ruby>生<rt>い</rt></ruby>きられない “people can’t live alone”. The plain non-past fits: it states how things are, not what happened once. The song narrows its “we” as it goes: <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> “people” here, ふたり “the two of us” on line 011, and <ruby>僕<rt>ぼく</rt></ruby>たち “we” on line 024.

### What <ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い means here

<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い covers physical weakness (<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い “in poor health”) and weakness of heart or will (<ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い, <ruby>意<rt>い</rt>志<rt>し</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い). After <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えないものを<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う, it is the second kind: people are not strong enough to go without something to believe in.
