### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="021" %}

The line is grammatical. それは is the topic, and the nearest thing it can point to is the singing voice that rang out on line 020. Two short phrases follow, set side by side with only a space between them: <ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ “connects to the future” and <ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ “connects to the past”. つなぐ is transitive (“connect X to Y”), but no を-object is given. What gets connected, whether us, our feelings or the present moment, is left to the listener. Like other plain verb forms, つなぐ looks the same whether it ends a sentence or modifies a noun, so there are two readings. (a) Each つなぐ ends a short sentence: “It links (us) to the future; it links (us) to the past.” Line 022 then adds a noun phrase describing それ, “a melody like a fairy tale”. (b) Both つなぐ modify <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ on line 022, and the two lines form one “A is B” sentence with the copula left out: それは、<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ、<ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ、お<ruby>伽<rt>とぎ</rt>噺<rt>ばなし</rt></ruby>みたいな<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ（だ） “it is a melody like a fairy tale, one that links to the future and links to the past”. Nothing needs correcting.

## Notes

### Two readings, one picture

Read as (a), the line makes two statements and line 022 adds a description afterwards. Read as (b), lines 021–022 are a single sentence, それは〜<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ(だ) “it is a ~ melody”, with three modifiers stacked before the noun. Japanese verbs give no sign of which is meant, and either way the voice links times.

### What それ is

The voice of line 020 is the last thing named, and line 022 names it again as <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ “melody”. That makes this それ the clearest one in the song. The chorus’s それは (lines 009–011) also came right after the voice (line 008), which supports hearing it the same way there.

### つなぐ with no object

つなぐ is “connect X to Y” (XをYにつなぐ): <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>をつなぐ “hold hands”, <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>と<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>をつなぐ “bring people together”, <ruby>伝<rt>でん</rt>統<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ “hand a tradition on to the future”. <ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ is a common phrase for passing something on. <ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ is rarer, and it makes the link run the other way. Without an object, the listener supplies one: us, our feelings, the present. The intransitive partner is つながる “be connected, lead to” (<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につながる “lead to the future”). With つなぐ, the voice does the linking.

### The future first

The song names the future before the past, so the voice looks ahead first and then back. Line 010 said it is not 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」 “the end”, and that it gets past time (<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>えて). Here it reaches across time in both directions.

### Parallel phrases with no conjunction

<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>につなぐ　<ruby>過<rt>か</rt>去<rt>こ</rt></ruby>につなぐ sets two phrases of the same shape side by side, with only the noun changed and no と or そして between them. This kind of parallelism (<ruby>対<rt>つい</rt>句<rt>く</rt></ruby>) is common in lyrics. Line 023 goes further and sets three phrases in a row: <ruby>羽<rt>は</rt></ruby>ばたける　<ruby>導<rt>みちび</rt></ruby>かれる　まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ぬ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>へ.

### Spelling

<ruby>繋<rt>つな</rt></ruby> is not on the Jōyō list, so the standard spelling is つなぐ, in kana, as here. <ruby>繋<rt>つな</rt></ruby>ぐ is common in everyday writing all the same.
