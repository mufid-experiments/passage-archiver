### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="018" %}

The line is grammatical and closes the sentence that line 017 opened: ひとつひとつ<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>った<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>には<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう “at the end of what we follow, one by one, what’s real must be waiting”. <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> “the real, the truth” is the subject, marked by が. <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている is “be waiting”, the te-form of <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ + いる for an ongoing state. The ending んだろう is the explanatory の, shortened to ん, + だろう “probably, I suppose”. It presents the line as the speaker’s inference: “it must be that…”. Without か, the line reads as a guess the speaker is fairly sure of, not as a question. Saying that the truth “waits” is personification, like <ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るい<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている “a bright future awaits”. As on line 006, <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> is used as a bare noun. Line 019 starts a new phrase. Nothing needs correcting.

## Notes

### Completes line 017

The whole sentence is ひとつひとつ<ruby>辿<rt>たど</rt></ruby>った<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>には、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう. It has the usual frame for saying what is to be found somewhere: [place]には[thing]がある / いる / <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている.

### <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>, then and now

On line 006, <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> was something one might find out with dread: もしも…<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってしまったら “what if… we came to know what’s real”. Here the same word is waiting at the end of a road the speaker chooses to walk. In both lines <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby> stands as a noun on its own, “the real”, not <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt>当<rt>とう</rt></ruby>のこと. What was feared in the first section is something to walk toward in this one.

### 〜が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている

<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ is used of people waiting, and figuratively of what lies ahead of someone: <ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るい<ruby>未<rt>み</rt>来<rt>らい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている “a bright future awaits”, <ruby>試<rt>し</rt>練<rt>れん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている “trials lie ahead”. <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている, with いる, describes a state: the truth is already there and waiting, not something that will start waiting later.

### んだろう and だろう

<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているだろう on its own is a plain prediction: “will probably be waiting”. With ん, <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているんだろう presents a conclusion the speaker has reached, “it must be that it’s waiting”, as if drawn from the steps taken so far. Spoken with a rising tone, it can be a question (“is it waiting, I wonder?”), but a written line without か is usually read as the falling, fairly confident guess. The casual form is んだろ, and the polite one is んでしょう.

### Guess, then knowledge

だろう is a guess, and the speaker hasn’t arrived yet. On line 024, the song moves to <ruby>僕<rt>ぼく</rt></ruby>たちは<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってるはず “we must know”, where はず expresses an expectation with reasons behind it. The second half of the song goes from “surely” to “we know”.
