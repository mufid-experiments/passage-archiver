### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="031" %}

The line is grammatical and repeats line 011 word for word. それは「<ruby>奇<rt>き</rt>跡<rt>せき</rt></ruby>」じゃない “it isn’t ‘a miracle’” is a complete sentence: the topic それは and the noun predicate <ruby>奇<rt>き</rt>跡<rt>せき</rt></ruby> in brackets, negated with the spoken じゃない. The brackets mark <ruby>奇<rt>き</rt>跡<rt>せき</rt></ruby> as a label the song turns down. After the full-width space, <ruby>透<rt>す</rt></ruby>き<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>った<ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>で “in a clear, transparent world” is a place phrase, and で needs a verb to belong to. None comes. The line ends on the noun ふたり “the two of us”. Prose would need a particle and a predicate, for example <ruby>透<rt>す</rt></ruby>き<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>った<ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>でふたり（でいる） “(we are) together, the two of us, in a clear world”. The second half answers the denial: not a miracle, but the two of us. The second chorus left ふたり out (line 027). The last chorus brings it back, so ふたり stands between <ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>で and line 032, which is therefore a separate noun phrase. Nothing needs correcting.

### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="032" %}

The line is a grammatical noun phrase, not a sentence, and it is the last line of the song. <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る “the sun rises” is a relative clause on <ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> “place”: “the place where the sun rises”. The song stops on the image of sunrise. Nothing needs correcting.

## Notes

### How the two lines connect

They are two noun phrases side by side: <ruby>透<rt>す</rt></ruby>き<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>った<ruby>世<rt>せ</rt>界<rt>かい</rt></ruby>でふたり and <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>. The first stops on ふたり without the verb its で is waiting for. The second has its own verb inside, <ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る, but no predicate. Nothing in the grammar joins them, so the listener does: two people in a clear world, and a place where the sun rises, perhaps the same place.

### Three endings for one chorus

<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>昇<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt>所<rt>しょ</rt></ruby> closes every chorus. At line 012 it follows ふたり and stands apart. Here it stands apart again, as the last line of the song. The song’s final picture keeps the people and the place side by side.

### From the unseen to the sunrise

The song opened on <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えないものを<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う “we believe together in what we can’t see” (line 001). It ends on a world that is <ruby>透<rt>す</rt></ruby>き<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>った, clear enough to see through, and on the moment the sun comes up and everything becomes visible. Heard that way, the song moves from believing without seeing toward light. The lyric never says so directly. It only sets the images in that order.

### Ending on a beginning

Two lines earlier, the chorus denied that “it” is 「<ruby>最<rt>さい</rt>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>」 “the end” (line 030). The song keeps its word and ends not on a closing word but on sunrise, the start of a day.

### Where the sun rises

“The place where the sun rises” is also the east, and an old way of naming Japan (<ruby>日<rt>に</rt>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>, “origin of the sun”). Together with the ancient word まほろ on line 008, a listener may hear the echo of an old, beloved homeland. The song does not press it (see line 012).
