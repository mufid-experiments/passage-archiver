# Line 023 · 羽ばたける　導かれる　まだ見ぬ先へ

## Context

- Line 022 (previous): お伽噺みたいな調べ
- **Line 023 (explained here): 羽ばたける　導かれる　まだ見ぬ先へ**
- Line 024 (next): 幻なんかじゃない　僕たちは知ってるはず

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: two short verbs (potential 羽ばたける, passive 導かれる) and a direction phrase (まだ見ぬ先へ) set side by side; in prose the direction would come first (inversion, 倒置).

The line is grammatical and falls into three parts at the full-width spaces. 羽ばたける "can take wing" is the potential form of 羽ばたく "flap one's wings". 導かれる "be guided, be led" is the passive of 導く "guide". まだ見ぬ先へ "toward what lies ahead, not yet seen" is a direction phrase with no verb of its own. It belongs to both verbs before it, so the order is inverted. Prose would say まだ見ぬ先へ羽ばたける、導かれる "we can take wing, and are led, toward the unseen beyond". The subject is unstated. It is "we", the 僕たち of line 024. The one who does the leading is not named either, and the nearest candidate is the melody of line 022. まだ見ぬ uses the old negative ぬ (= ない), which survives in set phrases and gives the line a literary ring. Both verbs could also be heard as modifiers of 先 alongside まだ見ぬ ("toward the unseen place where we can fly, where we are led"), but reading them as two short statements is more natural. Nothing needs correcting.

## How it fits together

`[羽ばたける]　／　[導かれる]　／　[[[まだ 見 ぬ] 先] へ] ← (belongs to both verbs: inversion)`

The two verbs share an unstated subject ("we"), and both take the direction phrase at the end of the line. Prose order would be まだ見ぬ先へ羽ばたける、導かれる. Putting the direction last makes "where to" the end point of the line, and of the movement.

## Translation

- Literal: "(we) can flap (our) wings; (we) are guided; toward the ahead not yet seen"
- Natural: "We can spread our wings, we're being led on, toward a place we've never seen."

## Notes

- **Three phrases, one movement.** Each part is short, and the spaces between them are breaths. The order runs from what we can do (羽ばたける), to what is done for us (導かれる), to where it goes (まだ見ぬ先へ). Line 021 set two phrases side by side (未来につなぐ　過去につなぐ), and this line sets three.
- **羽ばたく and 羽ばたける.** 羽ばたく comes from 羽 (は) "feather, wing" + はたく "beat". Literally it is a bird flapping its wings. Figuratively, it means "spread one's wings, launch out into the world": 世界へ羽ばたく "go out into the world", a phrase used for graduates and young people starting out. Godan verbs form their potential by changing the final -u to -eru: 書く → 書ける, 羽ばたく → 羽ばたける "be able to take wing". The potential says the ability is there now.
- **Flying and being led.** The first verb is active and the second passive: the travellers fly by their own strength, and they are also carried. 導かれる names no guide (no 〜に), but coming straight after お伽噺みたいな調べ (line 022), the natural guide is the melody, or the voice behind it. The song's other passive, 包まれていく (lines 007, 019), also has something larger surrounding the people in it.
- **まだ見ぬ and the old negative ぬ.** ぬ is the attributive form of the classical negative ず, and it survives in set phrases: 見知らぬ人 "a stranger", 知らぬ間に "before one knows it", 思わぬ "unexpected". まだ見ぬ "not yet seen" is a favourite in lyrics and slogans: まだ見ぬ世界へ "toward a world not yet seen", まだ見ぬ明日へ. Everyday speech would say まだ見たことのない "never yet seen".
- **先 again.** On line 017, 先 was the end of a road already followed: 辿った先には本当が待っているんだろう "at the end of the road, what's real must be waiting". Here 先 is a place no one has seen. The song's "ahead" has moved from a destination it expects to one it cannot yet picture.
- **へ and に.** へ marks the direction ("toward"), and に the destination itself ("to, at"). まだ見ぬ先へ stresses setting out more than arriving. へ is the usual particle in slogans and titles of this kind: 未来へ, 明日へ.
- **Link to line 024.** Line 024 (幻なんかじゃない　僕たちは知ってるはず) answers a doubt the listener might have. After a melody "like a fairy tale" and a flight into the unknown, it insists: this is no illusion, and we know it.
