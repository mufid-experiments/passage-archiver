### Is it valid Japanese?

{% include explain/verdict.html n="015" %}

The line is grammatical but is not a sentence. ふわり is a mimetic adverb, “softly, lightly”, for an airy, floating movement. <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように is a simile, “like a gentle breeze”, and it is adverbial. Both need a verb, and unlike line 013’s simile this one gets it: line 016 begins with <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく “flow on, drift by”. So the two lines read ふわり（と）、<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby>よ “O days that drift softly by, like a gentle breeze”. ふわり usually takes と (ふわりと), and dropping it is common in lyrics. Because it stands right before <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>, it also colours the breeze, but its grammatical partner is the verb. Line 014 ended its pair with “Ah”, and this line starts the second pair. Nothing needs correcting.

### How it fits together

Two adverbials stack up before one verb: ふわり says how the movement looks (soft and airy), and <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように says what it is like (a gentle breeze). The verb is <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく on line 016, which in turn describes <ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt>々<rt>び</rt></ruby> “days”.

### ふわり

A mimetic word for a light, soft, airy movement, such as floating up, drifting or landing gently: <ruby>風<rt>ふう</rt>船<rt>せん</rt></ruby>がふわりと<ruby>浮<rt>う</rt></ruby>かぶ “a balloon floats up”, <ruby>髪<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>がふわりと<ruby>揺<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>れる “hair stirs softly”. ふわふわ, the doubled form, describes an ongoing state (“fluffy, floating”), and ふんわり is “soft, fluffy”. The single ふわり is one soft motion. Mimetic adverbs usually take と, but lyrics often drop it (ふわり<ruby>浮<rt>う</rt></ruby>かぶ).

### What ふわり modifies

An adverb modifies a predicate, so ふわり pairs most naturally with <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく: ふわりと<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れてゆく “drift softly by”. <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい is a predicate too (an adjective), so ふわり<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい “airily gentle” is also possible as a poetic pairing. In listening the two merge: the breeze is soft, and so is the passing of the days.

### <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい and <ruby>易<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい

Both are read やさしい. <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい is “gentle, kind”, and <ruby>易<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい is “easy”. Said of things, <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい means mild or soft: <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby> “a gentle breeze”, <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby> “soft light”, <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>味<rt>あじ</rt></ruby> “a mild flavour”.

### A gentle <ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように

Wind similes often mean speed: <ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る “run like the wind”. With ふわり and <ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい before it, this one is about lightness and softness instead. The days pass the way a breeze does, gently and without being held.

### The two similes of this section

<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>のように (line 013) and <ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のように (this line) open the two pairs, one from the sky and one from the air. Both pairs end with “Ah”. Only the second simile reaches a verb, and that verb is about the days flowing by. Line 013’s simile can also be heard as reaching ahead to it.
