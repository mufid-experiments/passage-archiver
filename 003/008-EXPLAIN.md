# Line 008 · まほろのうたごえ

## Context

- Line 007 (previous): 光と影に包まれていく
- **Line 008 (explained here): まほろのうたごえ**
- Line 009 (next): それは「魔法」じゃない　想いを分け合って

## Is it valid Japanese?

**Verdict:** Valid — poetic: noun phrase ending (体言止め); まほろ is an old poetic word for "a splendid land" (the same as まほろば), unless it is a name.

The line is a grammatical noun phrase with no predicate. まほろ is a noun, の links it to うたごえ ("singing voice"), and the line ends on that noun, the 体言止め common in lyrics. まほろ is not an everyday word. It is an old form of まほろば, "a fine land, the best of places", known from the ancient line 倭は国のまほろば "Yamato is the finest of lands". In some works, Mahoro is also a name, of a character or a place, and if this song belongs to one, the phrase means "Mahoro's singing voice". The lyric gives no hint either way. The whole line is in hiragana: うたごえ is usually written 歌声, so the kana spelling is a choice that keeps the line soft. Line 007's 包まれていく can describe this noun: 光と影に包まれていくまほろのうたごえ, "the singing voice of mahoro, enfolded in light and shadow". The two lines are then one noun phrase, and that phrase answers the "if" of lines 005–006 with an image instead of a statement. Nothing needs correcting.

## How it fits together

`(line 007: 光と影に包まれていく →) [まほろ の うたごえ]`

まほろ modifies うたごえ through の. If line 007 is read as a description, it is a relative clause on うたごえ too, and lines 007–008 are one noun phrase: "the singing voice of mahoro that is enfolded in light and shadow". If line 007 is read as the result of the "if", this line stands alone after it as an image.

## Translation

- Literal: "the singing voice of mahoro"
- Natural: "the singing voice of Mahoro", or "the song of that fair land" (with line 007: "the singing voice of Mahoro, enfolded in light and shadow")

## Notes

- **まほろ, an old word.** まほら, まほろ and まほろば are forms of one ancient word meaning "a splendid place, the finest land". The best-known use is in the 古事記 (Kojiki, 712), in a song given to the hero Yamato Takeru: 倭は国のまほろば "Yamato is the finest of lands". The parts are usually explained as ま (a prefix meaning "true, perfect"), ほ (秀, "excellent, outstanding") and a place suffix. In the Kojiki, the hero sings the song far from home, longing for it shortly before he dies, so the word has carried a sense of a beloved, longed-for land ever since. Today it belongs to poetry, titles and names. The song never says what or where まほろ is.
- **Or a name.** In some anime, games and novels, Mahoro (まほろ) is the name of a character or a place. If this song comes from one of them, まほろのうたごえ is simply "Mahoro's singing voice". In kana, both readings stay open.
- **うたごえ, 歌 + 声.** In compounds, 声 (こえ) often voices to ごえ (rendaku): 歌声 (うたごえ), 話し声 (はなしごえ) "speaking voices", 笑い声 (わらいごえ) "laughter". 歌声 means both "a singing voice" (美しい歌声 "a beautiful singing voice") and "the sound of singing" (子供たちの歌声が聞こえる "children can be heard singing"). The usual spelling is 歌声. With うたごえ in kana as well as まほろ, the whole line is hiragana and looks soft and storybook-like on the page.
- **体言止め.** The line stops on a noun with no verb. In reading (b) of line 007, lines 007–008 are one long noun phrase that follows the hypothesis of lines 005–006, so the song answers "what if we came to know what's real?" with a picture: a voice, wrapped in light and shadow. At line 020 the same phrase gets a verb: まほろのうたごえだけ　響く "only the singing voice of mahoro resounds".
- **Link to line 009.** The chorus begins on line 009: それは「魔法」じゃない "it isn't 'magic'". The それ "it, that" points back to something just mentioned, and the nearest candidate is this singing voice. The second half of the song has the same order. Line 020 ends on the voice, and line 021 starts それは未来につなぐ "it links (us) to the future", which line 022 then calls お伽噺みたいな調べ "a melody like a fairy tale".
